# Designation of State Institutions for Service of Federal Sentence

> [!NOTE]
> Read in full on 2026-10-30 from the preserved official PDF. Replaces PS 5160.05, which is no longer published.

## Holding or rule used

- p. 1: rescinds PS 5160.05 (1/16/2003); adds the five 18 U.S.C. § 3621(b) factors to DSCC review, Setser, Mangum II and Dotson; eliminates Regional Director decisions on concurrent-designation requests; updates nunc pro tunc procedures.
- § 2.b, p. 3: the DSCC Sentence Computation Section Chief (delegated to DSCC Operations Managers) designates state institutions; after Setser v. United States, 566 U.S. 231 (2012), the Bureau 'does not have the authority to independently determine' concurrent versus consecutive service under § 3621(b) (for pre-Nov. 1, 1987 offenses the DSCC researches the record and decides).
- § 3, p. 3: a federal sentence cannot commence before it is imposed, so a state institution can be designated no earlier than the imposition date.
- § 4.a, p. 4: where the federal sentence follows an already-imposed state sentence and the court is silent, the Bureau treats it as consecutive under 18 U.S.C. § 3584(a) (offenses on or after Nov. 1, 1987).
- § 4.b, p. 4: where the federal sentence came first, the state had primary jurisdiction, and the judgment is silent, the sentence commences under § 3585(a); the Bureau updates commencement if the court later orders or recommends concurrency.
- § 5.a-c, p. 5: court intent is shown by a J&C order, a J&C recommendation naming a state institution, or a later order or recommendation, which the Bureau follows regardless of when it is made.
- § 5.d(1), p. 6: under Barden v. Keohane, 921 F.2d 476 (3d Cir. 1990), the Bureau must consider an inmate's nunc pro tunc request but has no obligation to grant it; a request for credit for state time credited to a state sentence is treated as a nunc pro tunc request.
- § 5.d(3), (5), pp. 6-7: the DSCC writes to the sentencing court for its position (courtesy copies to the U.S. Attorney and U.S. Probation) and, per Dotson v. Kizziah, 966 F.3d 443 (6th Cir. 2020), decides under the five § 3621(b) factors; that decision determines commencement under § 3585(a).
- § 5.d(6), p. 7: if the court has not responded after at least 60 days, the Section Chief decides on the five factors, documents the decision, and notifies the inmate in writing.
- § 5.d(7)-(8), p. 7: no letter is sent if concurrency is not appropriate or the court already made clear consecutive intent; no concurrent designation where the court already decided the order of service.
- § 6, pp. 7-8: Mangum v. Hallembaek, 910 F.3d 770 (4th Cir. 2018) exception for inmates sentenced in the Fourth Circuit between Dec. 29, 2006 and Mar. 27, 2012: full § 3621(b) evaluation, the sentencing court's views are not invoked, and the state court's preference is weighed heavily (not binding; reasons for overriding it must be stated).
- § 7.d, p. 9: concurrent designation is not generally considered where a statute mandates consecutive service (e.g., 18 U.S.C. §§ 924(c), 1028A, 3146), but the Bureau follows a court order and writes to advise the court.
- § 8.b, p. 9; p. 10: a nunc pro tunc designation triggers a jail-credit review under the sentence computation manuals and is normally effective from the imposition date unless a later date is entered.
