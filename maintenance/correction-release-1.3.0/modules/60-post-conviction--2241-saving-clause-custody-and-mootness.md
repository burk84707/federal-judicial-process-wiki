#### CLM-2241S-006: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A prior adverse § 2255 decision, procedural default, expiration of § 2255(f), adverse precedent, or a preference for the district of confinement does not by itself make the motion remedy inadequate or ineffective. Those circumstances concern a claim's availability, timing, or outcome rather than the saving clause's remedial-vehicle inquiry. [*Jones*, 599 U.S. at 477–80, 480 n.3]

After:

> An asserted error of law by the § 2255 court, adverse circuit precedent, or the prisoner's present inability to file a § 2255 motion does not by itself make the motion remedy inadequate or ineffective; the saving clause concerns the adequacy or effectiveness of the remedial vehicle, not any court's asserted errors of law. *Jones* also described the sentencing court, not the district of confinement, as the best venue for a federal prisoner's collateral attack. [*Jones*, 599 U.S. at 479–82 & n.4]

#### CLM-2241S-009: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> The Court identified dissolution of the sentencing court as the clearest historical example of practical unavailability and referred to circumstances such as inability to access necessary records as an example in the pre-§ 2255 regime. These examples do not establish a broad equitable override of § 2255(h). [*Jones*, 599 U.S. at 474–75]

These examples do not create a general equitable exception to § 2255(h). [*Jones*, 599 U.S. at 474–75, 482]

#### CLM-2241S-014: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Sections 2241 and 2255 do not make a successful saving-clause petition automatically use § 2255's merits standard or remedial menu. The underlying right, procedural posture, governing circuit law, and scope of habeas relief must be separately established. [28 U.S.C. §§ 2241(c), 2255(b), (e)]

After:

> Section 2255 does not direct a successful saving-clause petition to use § 2255(b)'s grounds or remedial menu: § 2255(b)'s findings and remedies (vacating the judgment and discharging the prisoner, resentencing, granting a new trial, or correcting the sentence) are prescribed for "the motion," and § 2255(e) addresses only whether a habeas application shall be entertained. A court deciding a habeas application must instead "dispose of the matter as law and justice require." [28 U.S.C. § 2255(b), (e)]; [28 U.S.C. § 2243]

#### CLM-2241S-018: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> *Maleng* separately construed a pro se petition as attacking a current sentence enhanced by an allegedly invalid prior conviction. That construction does not place the person “in custody” under the fully expired prior conviction or automatically make the enhancement challenge cognizable. [*Maleng*, 490 U.S. at 493–94]

After:

> *Maleng* separately read the pro se petition as challenging the 1978 state sentences that the petitioner had not yet begun to serve, as enhanced by the allegedly invalid prior conviction, and held under *Peyton* and *Braden* that he was "in custody" under those sentences because the State had lodged a detainer to return him to serve them after his federal term. That reading does not place a person in custody under the fully expired prior conviction itself, and the Court expressed no view on the extent to which that conviction may be challenged in the attack on the enhanced sentences. [*Maleng*, 490 U.S. at 490, 493–94]

#### CLM-2241S-025: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> If no qualifying custody existed at filing, routing to coram nobis is a separate extraordinary-writ inquiry, not an automatic remedy. The issuing court, continuing consequences, delay, fundamental-error standard, and circuit test remain to be verified in the dedicated extraordinary-writ module. [*Maleng*, 490 U.S. at 490–94]

After:

> Habeas jurisdiction requires custody under the conviction or sentence under attack at the time the petition is filed; without it, routing to coram nobis is a separate extraordinary-writ inquiry, not an automatic remedy. In *Morgan*, where the record did not show whether counsel had been properly waived, the Court held that a motion in the nature of coram nobis must be heard by the federal trial court, no other remedy being then available and sound reasons existing for failure to seek earlier relief, while limiting this extraordinary remedy to circumstances compelling such action to achieve justice and noting that a conviction's results may persist after the term is served. [*Maleng*, 490 U.S. at 490–91]; [*Morgan*, 346 U.S. at 511–13]

#### CLM-2241S-027: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> COA classification and the permissible custody remedy are addressed separately in the § 2241 appeal-and-relief module. Saving-clause availability alone neither requires a COA nor promises vacatur, resentencing, credits, placement, or release. [28 U.S.C. §§ 2241, 2255(e)]

After:

> COA classification and the permissible custody remedy are addressed separately in the § 2241 appeal-and-relief module. Section 2253(c)(1) requires a certificate of appealability for appeals from final orders in state-process habeas proceedings and § 2255 proceedings, and *Jones*, in rejecting a saving-clause route for second or successive nonconstitutional claims, observed that such claims, after escaping § 2255 through the saving clause, would no longer be subject to that requirement. Saving-clause availability under § 2255(e) decides only whether the habeas application may be entertained; if it is, the court must dispose of the matter as law and justice require. [28 U.S.C. § 2253(c)(1)]; [*Jones*, 599 U.S. at 479]; [28 U.S.C. § 2255(e)]; [28 U.S.C. § 2243]
