#### CLM-XWRIT-003: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A requested writ must therefore be tied to the issuing court’s existing or potential jurisdiction and to relief the court has legal authority to effect; the All Writs Act is not a general roving commission to supervise another actor. [*Pennsylvania Bureau*, 474 U.S. at 41–43]

After:

> A requested writ must therefore be in aid of the issuing court’s jurisdiction otherwise obtained; the All Writs Act is “a residual source of authority to issue writs that are not otherwise covered by statute,” a statute that specifically addresses the issue controls, and the Act does not authorize “ad hoc writs whenever compliance with statutory procedures appears inconvenient or less appropriate.” [*Pennsylvania Bureau*, 474 U.S. at 40–43]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Removed the unsupported “potential jurisdiction” and “roving commission” language and restated the limit in Pennsylvania Bureau’s own words; pinpoint widened to 40–43 to cover the “jurisdiction otherwise obtained” passage.
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-PENN-BUREAU-474-US-34, 474 U.S. at 40: "in its exercise of jurisdiction otherwise obtained."
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-PENN-BUREAU-474-US-34, 474 U.S. at 43: "The All Writs Act is a residual source of authority to issue writs that are not otherwise covered by statute."
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-PENN-BUREAU-474-US-34, 474 U.S. at 43: "Where a statute specifically addresses the particular issue at hand, it is that authority, and not the All Writs Act, that is controlling."
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-PENN-BUREAU-474-US-34, 474 U.S. at 43: "it does not authorize them to issue ad hoc writs whenever compliance with statutory procedures appears inconvenient or less appropriate."

#### CLM-XWRIT-018: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Before routing to coram nobis, determine whether qualifying custody under the challenged federal judgment existed at filing; incarceration and supervised release can support § 2255, while collateral consequences of a fully expired sentence alone do not satisfy the habeas custody requirement. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(a)]; [*Maleng*, 490 U.S. at 490–94]

After:

> Before routing to coram nobis, determine whether qualifying custody under the challenged federal sentence existed at filing: § 2255(a) is available to “[a] prisoner in custody under sentence of a court established by Act of Congress,” and *Maleng*, construing the federal habeas statute’s “in custody” requirement, held that once a sentence has completely expired, the collateral consequences of the conviction are not themselves sufficient to render a person in custody. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(a)]; [*Maleng*, 490 U.S. at 490–92]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Removed the unsupported statement that supervised release supports § 2255 custody, quoted § 2255(a)’s own custody language, and identified Maleng as construing the habeas statute (§§ 2241(c)(3), 2254(a)).
  - AUTH-USC-28-2255, § 2255(a): "A prisoner in custody under sentence of a court established by Act of Congress"
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-MALENG-490-US-488, 490 U.S. at 490: "requiring that the habeas petitioner be "in custody" under the conviction or sentence under attack at the time his"

#### CLM-XWRIT-026: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Corrected “directed to a district judge” to “directed to a district court” per Rule 21(a)(1), and added that the judge receives only a copy and the other trial-court parties are the respondents, so a filer does not name the judge as respondent.

#### CLM-XWRIT-033: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Rule 21 requires a petition filed with the circuit clerk, service on all parties, a copy provided to the trial-court judge, a statement of the issues and necessary facts, reasons the writ should issue, and essential record parts; the appellate court may deny the petition without an answer or order an answer and set further proceedings. [Fed. R. App. P. 21(a)–(b)]

After:

> Rule 21 requires a petition titled “In re [name of petitioner],” filed with the circuit clerk, served on all parties, and copied to the trial-court judge; it must state the relief sought, the issues presented, the necessary facts, and the reasons the writ should issue, and include any order or opinion or essential parts of the record. The clerk dockets the petition upon receiving the docket fee; the court may deny it without an answer but otherwise must order the respondent, if any, to answer within a fixed time. Unless the court permits otherwise, the petition may not exceed 7,800 words if computer-produced or 30 pages if handwritten or typewritten (excluding the required attachments), and an original and 3 copies must be filed unless a local rule or order requires a different number. [Fed. R. App. P. 21(a)–(b), (d)]
