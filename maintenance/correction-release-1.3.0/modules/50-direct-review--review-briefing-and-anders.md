#### CLM-DR-021: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> There is no single standard of review for every criminal appeal: the claimed error, legal or factual question, procedural posture, requested relief, and whether a timely and sufficiently specific position was presented under Rule 51 must be identified before selecting de novo, clear-error, abuse-of-discretion, harmless-error, plain-error, waiver, or another governing rule. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 51, 52]

After:

> The appellant's brief must give, for each issue, a concise statement of the applicable standard of review. [Fed. R. App. P. 28(a)(8)(B)] A party preserves a claim of error by informing the court, when the ruling or order is made or sought, of the action it wishes the court to take or its objection and the grounds; the absence of an objection does not later prejudice a party who had no opportunity to object, and rulings admitting or excluding evidence are governed by Federal Rule of Evidence 103. Any error that does not affect substantial rights must be disregarded, while a plain error that affects substantial rights may be considered even though it was not brought to the court's attention. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 51, 52]

Authorities: AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 → AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34, AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Removed the unsupported list of de novo, clear-error, and abuse-of-discretion standards and the 'must be identified before selecting' checklist; kept the issue-by-issue point using Rule 28(a)(8)(B), and stated Rules 51-52 in their own words, adding the no-opportunity-to-object protection and the FRE 103 referral both reviewers flagged.
  - AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34, Fed. R. App. P. 28(a)(8)(B): "for each issue, a concise statement of the applicable standard of review"
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52, Fed. R. Crim. P. 51(b): "of the action the party wishes the court to take, or the party's objection to the court's action and the grounds for that objection"
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52, Fed. R. Crim. P. 51(b): "If a party does not have an opportunity to object to a ruling or order, the absence of an objection does not later prejudice that party."
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52, Fed. R. Crim. P. 51(b): "A ruling or order that admits or excludes evidence is governed by Federal Rule of Evidence 103."
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52, Fed. R. Crim. P. 52(a): "Any error, defect, irregularity, or variance that does not affect substantial rights must be disregarded."
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52, Fed. R. Crim. P. 52(b): "A plain error that affects substantial rights may be considered even though it was not brought to the court's attention."

#### CLM-DR-033: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Appointed appellate counsel has no constitutional duty to raise every nonfrivolous issue requested by the defendant and may exercise professional judgment in selecting the strongest issues; that issue-selection rule does not permit counsel to abandon a wholly frivolous appeal without the required procedure. [*Jones v. Barnes*, 463 U.S. 745, 751–54 (1983)]

After:

> Appointed appellate counsel has no constitutional duty to raise every nonfrivolous issue requested by the defendant and may, as a matter of professional judgment, decide not to present those points. [*Jones v. Barnes*, 463 U.S. 745, 751–54 (1983)] If counsel finds the case wholly frivolous after a conscientious examination, counsel should so advise the court and request permission to withdraw, and that request must be accompanied by a brief referring to anything in the record that might arguably support the appeal. [*Anders v. California*, 386 U.S. 738, 744 (1967)]

Authorities: AUTH-SCOTUS-JONES-BARNES-463-US-745 → AUTH-SCOTUS-JONES-BARNES-463-US-745, AUTH-SCOTUS-ANDERS-386-US-738

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Kept the Jones v. Barnes issue-selection holding and moved the withdrawal-procedure limit to Anders, stated in Anders's own words, because Jones does not set out that procedure.
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-JONES-BARNES-463-US-745, 463 U.S. at 751: "the indigent defendant has a constitutional right to compel appointed counsel to press nonfrivolous points requested by the client, if counsel, as a matter of professional judgment, decides not to present those points"
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-ANDERS-386-US-738, 386 U.S. at 744: "if counsel finds his case to be wholly frivolous, after a conscientious examination of it, he should so advise the court and request permission to withdraw"
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-ANDERS-386-US-738, 386 U.S. at 744: "That request must, however, be accompanied by a brief referring to anything in the record that might arguably support the appeal."

#### CLM-DR-035: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> An appellee's brief generally follows Rule 28(a) except for specified matters the appellee may omit if satisfied with the appellant's statements, and a reply is limited to responding to the appellee's brief; the court's order and local rules control the actual filing. [Fed. R. App. P. 28(b), (c)]

After:

> An appellee's brief must conform to the requirements of Rule 28(a)(1)–(8) and (10), except that the jurisdictional statement, statement of the issues, statement of the case, and statement of the standard of review need not appear unless the appellee is dissatisfied with the appellant's statement. The appellant may file a brief in reply to the appellee's brief, which must contain a table of contents and a table of authorities; unless the court permits, no further briefs may be filed. [Fed. R. App. P. 28(b), (c)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Replaced the 'limited to responding' and local-rule glosses with Rule 28(c)'s text, adding the no-further-briefs rule and the reply's table requirements both reviewers noted. Quotes verified. Overstatement: 'An appellee's brief must conform to Rule 28(a)'s requirements' takes in all of Rule 28(a), but 28(b) incorporates only specified paragraphs: 'The appellee's brief must conform to the requirements of Rule 28(a)(1)(8) and (10)' (OCR of '(1)–(8) and (10)'), which leaves out 28(a)(9), the short conclusion. Name the paragraphs.
  - AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34, Fed. R. App. P. 28(b): "none of the following need appear unless the appellee is dissatisfied with the appellant's statement"
  - AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34, Fed. R. App. P. 28(c): "The appellant may file a brief in reply to the appellee's brief. Unless the court permits, no further briefs may be filed."
  - AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34, Fed. R. App. P. 28(c): "A reply brief must contain a table of contents, with page references, and a table of authorities"

#### CLM-DR-036: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Absent a different rule or court order, Rule 31(a) gives the appellant forty days after the record is filed, the appellee thirty days after service of the appellant's brief, and the appellant twenty-one days after service of the appellee's brief for a reply, which ordinarily must be filed at least seven days before argument. [Fed. R. App. P. 31(a)]

After:

> Under Rule 31(a)(1), the appellant must serve and file a brief within 40 days after the record is filed, and the appellee must serve and file a brief within 30 days after the appellant's brief is served. The appellant may serve and file a reply brief within 21 days after service of the appellee's brief, but a reply brief must be filed at least 7 days before argument, unless the court, for good cause, allows a later filing. Under Rule 31(a)(2), a court of appeals that routinely considers cases on the merits promptly after the briefs are filed may shorten the time to serve and file briefs, either by local rule or by order in a particular case. [Fed. R. App. P. 31(a)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-31 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-31: Wording conformed to the text of the cited law or policy; independent reviews agreed; owner approved 2026-10-31.** Restated in the cited source's own terms (owner directive: claims follow the law and policy text). Rule 31(a)(1) says a reply brief 'must be filed at least 7 days before argument, unless the court, for good cause, allows a later filing.' The claim replaces that specific good-cause exception with 'ordinarily'. 'Absent a different rule or court order' loosely tracks Rule 31(a)(2), which lets a court of appeals that routinely considers cases promptly after briefing shorten the time 'by local rule or by order in a particular case'. The rule says the appellant 'must serve and file' (a duty); 'gives the appellant forty days' recasts it as an allowance. The 40-, 30-, and 21-day periods are correct.
  - AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34, Fed. R. App. P. 31(a)(1): "The appellant must serve and file a brief within 40 days after the record is filed. The appellee must serve and file a brief within 30 days after the appellant’s brief is served. The appellant may serve and file a reply brief within 21 days after service of the appellee’s brief but a reply brief must be filed at least 7 days before argument, unless the court, for good cause, allows a later filing."
  - AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34, Fed. R. App. P. 31(a)(2): "A court of appeals that routinely considers cases on the merits promptly after the briefs are filed may shorten the time to serve and file briefs, either by local rule or by order in a particular case."

#### CLM-DR-037: rewritten
