#### CLM-TRIAL-002: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> An offense with a maximum authorized prison term of six months or less is presumed petty, but additional statutory penalties can overcome that presumption when they are so severe that they clearly reflect a legislative judgment that the offense is serious. [*Blanton v. City of North Las Vegas*, 489 U.S. 538, 541–43 (1989)]

After:

> An offense with a maximum authorized prison term of six months or less is presumed petty for Sixth Amendment purposes; the defendant is entitled to a jury trial only if he can demonstrate that any additional statutory penalties, viewed in conjunction with the maximum authorized period of incarceration, are so severe that they clearly reflect a legislative determination that the offense is serious, and only penalties resulting from state action, such as those mandated by statute or regulation, are considered. [*Blanton v. City of North Las Vegas*, 489 U.S. 538, 543 & n.8 (1989)]

#### CLM-TRIAL-004: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A federal criminal jury ordinarily consists of twelve jurors; before verdict the parties may stipulate in writing, with court approval, to fewer, and after deliberations begin the court may permit eleven jurors to return a verdict if it finds good cause to excuse a juror. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 23(b)]

After:

> A federal criminal jury consists of 12 persons unless Rule 23 provides otherwise. At any time before the verdict, the parties may, with the court's approval, stipulate in writing that the jury may consist of fewer than 12 persons, or that a jury of fewer than 12 persons may return a verdict if the court finds it necessary to excuse a juror for good cause after the trial begins. After the jury has retired to deliberate, the court may permit a jury of 11 persons to return a verdict, even without a stipulation by the parties, if the court finds good cause to excuse a juror. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 23(b)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-31 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-31: Wording conformed to the text of the cited law or policy; independent reviews agreed; owner approved 2026-10-31.** Restated in the cited source's own terms (owner directive: claims follow the law and policy text). Rule 23(b)(1) says 'A jury consists of 12 persons unless this rule provides otherwise.' 'Ordinarily consists' is looser: it suggests departures beyond those the rule itself provides. The stipulation clause covers only 23(b)(2)(A) ('fewer'). It leaves out 23(b)(2)(B), a stipulation that a jury of fewer than 12 may return a verdict if a juror is excused for good cause after trial begins. It also leaves out that the 23(b)(3) jury of 11 may be permitted 'even without a stipulation by the parties'.

#### CLM-TRIAL-013: rewritten

#### CLM-TRIAL-017: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Rule 30 permits written instruction requests, made at the close of the evidence or at any earlier time the court reasonably sets, with a copy furnished to every other party; requires the court to inform the parties before closing arguments how it intends to rule on the requested instructions; and requires a party who objects to any portion of the instructions or to a failure to give a requested instruction to inform the court of the specific objection and the grounds for the objection before the jury retires to deliberate.
