#### CLM-3582-001: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> An imposed term of imprisonment is final and may be modified only through the three doors in § 3582(c): subsection (c)(1)(A), subsection (c)(1)(B), or subsection (c)(2). Each door has different eligibility, procedure, and limits.

After:

> Under § 3582(c), the court "may not modify a term of imprisonment once it has been imposed" except through subsection (c)(1)(A) (with separate routes in clauses (i) and (ii)), subsection (c)(1)(B), or subsection (c)(2); § 3582(b) separately recognizes correction under Rule 35 and § 3742 and appeal and modification under § 3742 if the sentence is outside the guideline range, and makes the judgment final "for all other purposes." Each door has different eligibility, procedure, and limits. [18 U.S.C. § 3582(b)–(c)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Replaced the overstatement that a sentence may be modified "only through the three doors" with the statute's actual limit on the court's modification power, added the § 3582(b) correction and appeal routes and its "for all other purposes" finality language, noted the two (c)(1)(A) routes, and added the link.
  - AUTH-USC-18-3582, § 3582(c): "The court may not modify a term of imprisonment once it has been imposed except that"
  - AUTH-USC-18-3582, § 3582(b): "corrected pursuant to the provisions of rule 35 of the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure and section 3742; or (3) appealed and modified, if outside the guideline range, pursuant to the provisions of section 3742"
  - AUTH-USC-18-3582, § 3582(b): "a judgment of conviction that includes such a sentence constitutes a final judgment for all other purposes."

#### CLM-3582-005: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Rule 35(b) requires a government motion based on substantial assistance. A motion within one year is governed by Rule 35(b)(1); a later motion must fit one of Rule 35(b)(2)'s three information-timing categories. The court may consider presentence assistance and may reduce below a statutory minimum.

After:

> Rule 35(b) requires a government motion. A motion within one year of sentencing is governed by Rule 35(b)(1), which requires that the defendant, "after sentencing, provided substantial assistance in investigating or prosecuting another person"; a later motion must fit one of Rule 35(b)(2)'s three information-timing categories. Presentence assistance may be considered only in evaluating whether the defendant has provided substantial assistance, and the court "may" reduce the sentence, including to a level below a statutory minimum. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 35(b)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Added Rule 35(b)(1)'s post-sentencing assistance element, limited presentence assistance to the (b)(3) evaluation it serves, stated that reduction is discretionary ("may"), and added the link.
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-35-36, Rule 35(b)(1): "Upon the government's motion made within one year of sentencing, the court may reduce a sentence if the defendant, after sentencing, provided substantial assistance in investigating or prosecuting another person."
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-35-36, Rule 35(b)(3): "In evaluating whether the defendant has provided substantial assistance, the court may consider the defendant's presentence assistance."
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-35-36, Rule 35(b)(4): "When acting under Rule 35(b), the court may reduce the sentence to a level below the minimum sentence established by statute."
