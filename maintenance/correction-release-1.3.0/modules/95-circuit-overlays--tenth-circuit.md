#### CLM-CIR10-006: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> In the Tenth Circuit, a federal prisoner cannot use audita querela to challenge a conviction or sentence when the claim is cognizable under § 2255; the substance of the filing controls and a custody-based collateral attack may be treated as a § 2255 motion subject to the applicable authorization rules. [*United States v. Torres*, 282 F.3d 1241, 1245–46 & n.6 (10th Cir. 2002)]

After:

> In the Tenth Circuit, *Torres*, assuming without deciding that audita querela survives, held as one of two alternative grounds that the writ is "not available to a petitioner when other remedies exist, such as a motion to vacate sentence under 28 U.S.C. § 2255." As the other ground, it held that a prisoner who had already filed a § 2255 motion could not avoid the successive-motion bar "by simply styling a petition under a different name," so the filing was properly treated as a successive § 2255 motion requiring authorization. *Torres* distinguished circuit precedent limiting recharacterization of a filing that would be a first § 2255 motion; that question is now governed by *Castro*, under which a court cannot recharacterize a pro se filing as a first § 2255 motion unless it informs the litigant of its intent, warns that later § 2255 motions will be subject to the second-or-successive restrictions, and provides an opportunity to withdraw or amend the filing. [*United States v. Torres*, 282 F.3d 1241, 1245–46 & n.6 (10th Cir. 2002)]; [*Castro v. United States*, 540 U.S. 375, 377, 383 (2003)]; [28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)]
