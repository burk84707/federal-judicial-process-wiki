#### CLM-CIR4-004: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> In the Fourth Circuit, § 3583(e)(1) ordinarily authorizes a conduct-based inquiry and permits consideration of a broad range of interests-of-justice factors, but it is not a mechanism for collaterally attacking an original, unappealed sentence. [*United States v. Pregent*, 190 F.3d 279, 282–83 (4th Cir. 1999)]

After:

> In the Fourth Circuit, § 3583(e)(1), in the typical case, allows a conduct-based inquiry into the continued necessity for supervision after the individual has served one full year on supervised release, and its interest-of-justice language permits consideration of a broad range of factors; the court must conclude that termination is warranted both by the individual’s conduct and by the interest of justice. The statute is not a mechanism for collaterally attacking an original, unappealed sentence. [*United States v. Pregent*, 190 F.3d 279, slip op. at 6–7 (4th Cir. 1999)]
