#### CLM-R33-008: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Rule 45(b)(1) permits an extension on a timely motion for good cause and permits a post-deadline extension when the failure to act resulted from excusable neglect; Rule 45(b)(2)'s current exception applies to Rule 35, not Rule 33.

After:

> Rule 45(b)(1) lets the court extend a time on its own, or for good cause on a party's motion made before the time expires or, after it expires, if the party failed to act because of excusable neglect; Rule 45(b)(2)'s exception, which bars extending the time to take any action under Rule 35 except as stated in that rule, applies to Rule 35, not Rule 33. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 45(b) (Dec. 1, 2025 ed.)]

#### CLM-R33-010: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A court may not use Rule 33 to order a new trial on its own initiative after the applicable motion period has expired.

After:

> Rule 33(a) authorizes a new trial only "[u]pon the defendant's motion," and the 1966 Advisory Committee Note states that a judge has no power to order a new trial on his own motion, while leaving intact the court's power in certain circumstances, before verdict or finding of guilty, to declare a mistrial and order a new trial on its own motion. Construing the earlier rule, *Smith* held that the Government was entitled to writs vacating a new-trial order the district judge entered on his own initiative after the conviction had been affirmed and the defendant had been imprisoned, reasoning that "expiration of the time within which relief can openly be asked of the judge, terminates the time within which it can properly be granted on the court's own initiative." [Fed. R. Crim. P. 33(a) & advisory committee's note to 1966 amendment]; [*United States v. Smith*, 331 U.S. 469, 470–71, 475, 477 (1947)]

Verification date: 2026-08-24 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** States that current Rule 33 permits no sua sponte new trial at any time (not only after the motion period), preserves the separate pre-verdict mistrial power, and confines Smith's after-the-deadline rule to the earlier rule it construed; no links added because the current claim carries none. Rule 33(a) and 1966 Note sentences are supported. But the Smith sentence presents the opinion's reasoning as a broad holding ("Smith held that expiration of the time ... terminates the time"). #### CLM-R33-011: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Rule 33 operates as a motion in the federal criminal proceeding and is decided by the district court that conducted the trial.

After:

> A Rule 33 new trial is sought by the defendant's motion under the Criminal Rules, which govern the procedure in all criminal proceedings in the United States district courts; on that motion, "the court may vacate any judgment and grant a new trial if the interest of justice so requires." [Fed. R. Crim. P. 1(a)(1), 33(a) (Dec. 1, 2025 ed.)]
