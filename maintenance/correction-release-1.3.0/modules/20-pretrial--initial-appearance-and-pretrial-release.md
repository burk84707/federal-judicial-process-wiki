#### CLM-PRETRIAL-003: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> For a federal confession, 18 U.S.C. § 3501(c) supplies a six-hour safe harbor against exclusion solely for presentment delay when the confession is voluntary. If the confession falls outside that window, allowing for reasonable transportation and distance, the court applies the McNabb–Mallory inquiry into whether the delay was unnecessary or unreasonable.

After:

> For a federal confession, 18 U.S.C. § 3501(c) provides that a confession made within six hours of arrest (or a longer period reasonable considering the means of transportation and the distance to the nearest available magistrate judge) is not inadmissible solely because of presentment delay; within that window it is admissible, subject to the other Rules of Evidence, if made voluntarily. If the confession occurred before presentment and beyond six hours, the court must decide whether the delay was unreasonable or unnecessary under the McNabb–Mallory cases, and if it was, the confession is to be suppressed. [Corley v. United States, 556 U.S. 303, 322 (2009)]

#### CLM-PRETRIAL-008: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A preliminary hearing is conditional, not an unqualified right in every case. Rule 5.1 ordinarily requires one for a nonpetty offense, but lists exceptions including waiver, indictment, specified informations, and a consenting misdemeanor defendant; absent an extension, the outside date is 14 days after initial appearance for a defendant in custody and 21 days for a released defendant.

After:

> A preliminary hearing is not required in every case. If a defendant is charged with an offense other than a petty offense, Rule 5.1(a) requires a magistrate judge to conduct a preliminary hearing unless the defendant waives the hearing; the defendant is indicted; the government files an information under Rule 7(b) charging the defendant with a felony; the government files an information charging the defendant with a misdemeanor; or the defendant is charged with a misdemeanor and consents to trial before a magistrate judge. Unless a magistrate judge extends the time limits under Rule 5.1(d), the magistrate judge must hold the preliminary hearing within a reasonable time, but no later than 14 days after the initial appearance if the defendant is in custody and no later than 21 days if not in custody. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 5.1(a), (c)–(d) (Dec. 1, 2025 ed.)]

#### CLM-PRETRIAL-016: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A § 3142(f) category triggers a hearing, not mandatory detention. The hearing ordinarily occurs immediately at first appearance, subject to statutory continuances; the person has counsel and the specified opportunities to testify, present and cross-examine witnesses, and present information by proffer or otherwise.

After:

> A § 3142(f) category triggers a hearing to determine whether any condition or combination of conditions will reasonably assure appearance and safety, not mandatory detention. The hearing shall be held immediately upon the person's first appearance unless the person or the government seeks a continuance; except for good cause, a defense continuance may not exceed five days and a government continuance three days (not including any intermediate Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday), and the person shall be detained during a continuance. The person has the right to counsel, appointed if financially unable to obtain adequate representation, and an opportunity to testify, present witnesses, cross-examine witnesses who appear, and present information by proffer or otherwise; the rules concerning admissibility of evidence in criminal trials do not apply. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(e)(1), (f)]

#### CLM-PRETRIAL-022: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> The § 3142(c) conditions menu includes third-party custody and restrictions on travel, associations, intoxicants, and controlled substances, among other options; it also permits any other reasonably necessary condition. The judicial officer must select conditions under the least-restrictive rule rather than treat the legacy chart's short list as mandatory or exhaustive.

After:

> The § 3142(c) conditions menu includes third-party custody and restrictions on travel, associations, intoxicants, and controlled substances, among other options; it also permits any other reasonably necessary condition. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(c)(1)]
