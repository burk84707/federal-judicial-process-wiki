#### CLM-FSA404-015: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A § 404 matter remains a sentence-modification proceeding, not a categorical plenary resentencing. Its information-consideration rule is broad, but the statutory authorization, the § 404(c) bars, and the benchmark-range limitation still control.

After:

> *Concepcion* did not equate a § 404 proceeding with an original sentencing: the majority said only that "there are differences between the two," while holding that the only limits on the information a court may consider are those set by Congress in a statute or by the Constitution. That information rule is broad, but § 404(b)'s authorization, § 404(c)'s two explicit limitations, and the rule that the benchmark Guidelines range may be recalculated only to reflect the Fair Sentencing Act still control. [*Concepcion v. United States*, 597 U.S. 481 (2022)]; [First Step Act of 2018, Pub. L. No. 115-391, § 404, 132 Stat. 5194, 5222]

Authorities: AUTH-USC-18-3582, AUTH-SCOTUS-CONCEPCION-597-US-481 → AUTH-SCOTUS-CONCEPCION-597-US-481, AUTH-PL-115-391-SEC-404

#### CLM-FSA404-016: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A prior § 3582(c)(2) Guidelines reduction is not automatically a § 404(c) bar. The prior order and sentence must be examined to determine whether the sentence was imposed or reduced in accordance with Fair Sentencing Act §§ 2 and 3 or whether a prior § 404 merits denial supplies the actual bar.

After:

> Section 404(c) provides that no court shall entertain a motion made under § 404 to reduce a sentence if the sentence was previously imposed or previously reduced in accordance with the amendments made by sections 2 and 3 of the Fair Sentencing Act of 2010, or if a previous § 404 motion to reduce the sentence was, after the date of enactment of the First Step Act, denied after a complete review of the motion on the merits. Section 404 does not list a prior § 3582(c)(2) Guidelines reduction as a limitation; whether the first limitation applies depends on whether the sentence was previously imposed or previously reduced in accordance with the amendments made by sections 2 and 3 of the Fair Sentencing Act of 2010. [First Step Act of 2018, Pub. L. No. 115-391, § 404, 132 Stat. 5194, 5222]

#### CLM-FSA404-018: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Section 404 does not erase an amended statutory minimum or make an unrelated legal rule retroactive. Any requested sentence must be authorized under the Fair Sentencing Act's amended statutory framework or under a separately established legal basis.

After:

> Section 404(b) authorizes a reduced sentence only "as if sections 2 and 3 of the Fair Sentencing Act of 2010 ... were in effect at the time the covered offense was committed," so a requested sentence must fit the penalties as those sections amended them or rest on a separately established legal basis; this follows from the "as if" text, since neither § 404 nor *Concepcion* addresses statutory minimums expressly. Section 404 does not make unrelated legal changes retroactive: the court need not recalculate the Guidelines "in any respect other than applying the Fair Sentencing Act," and postsentencing conduct or nonretroactive changes may inform only the choice of sentence, with the properly calculated range as the benchmark. [First Step Act of 2018, Pub. L. No. 115-391, § 404, 132 Stat. 5194, 5222]; [*Concepcion v. United States*, 597 U.S. 481 (2022)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Labeled the statutory-minimum point as an inference from the "as if" clause rather than a stated rule, tied the unrelated-rule point to Concepcion's footnotes 6 and 8, and added links.
  - AUTH-PL-115-391-SEC-404, § 404(b): "impose a reduced sentence as if sections 2 and 3 of the Fair Sentencing Act of 2010 (Public Law 111–220; 124 Stat. 2372) were in effect at the time the covered offense was committed."
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-CONCEPCION-597-US-481, slip op. n.8: "the First Step Act does not require a district court to recalculate a movant's Guidelines in any respect other than applying the Fair Sentencing Act."
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-CONCEPCION-597-US-481, slip op. n.6: "The district court may then consider postsentencing conduct or nonretroactive changes in selecting or rejecting an appropriate sentence, with the properly calculated Guidelines range as the benchmark."
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-CONCEPCION-597-US-481, slip op. Part III-A: "the "as if" clause requires a district court to apply the Fair Sentencing Act as if it applied at the time of the commission of the offense"
