#### CLM-CLEM-021: rewritten

#### CLM-CLEM-026: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> The current Pardon Attorney FAQ states that a full and unconditional presidential pardon restores federal firearm rights lost solely because of the pardoned federal conviction, but state, territorial, and tribal law and any independent disqualification remain separate. After:

> DOJ's current guidance states that a full presidential pardon removes penalties and disabilities, including a firearm disability, resulting from the federal conviction, but that a person who also has a separate, independent basis for firearm prohibition, like a state conviction or a prohibiting status, may need to seek federal firearms relief. The Attorney General may also restore federal firearms rights under 18 U.S.C. § 925(c); DOJ states that relief under that process removes only applicable federal firearms disabilities and does not override independent restrictions imposed by state law. A person whose federal firearms rights have been restored should consult state, territorial, or tribal authorities about whether he or she can possess firearms under the laws where he or she lives. [Office of the Pardon Attorney FAQ]; [DOJ Federal Firearm Rights Restoration FAQ]; [DOJ, Attorney General Blanche Launches New Process to Restore Federal Firearm Rights (Aug. 17, 2026)]

#### CLM-CLEM-028: rewritten
