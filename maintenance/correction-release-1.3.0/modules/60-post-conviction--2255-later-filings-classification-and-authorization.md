#### CLM-SOS-035: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Authorization forms, claim limits, attachments, word limits, electronic or paper filing, rehearing practice, and service requirements are circuit-specific and must be taken from the appropriate court of appeals' current rules and forms. [28 U.S.C. §§ 2244(b)(3), 2255(h)]

After:

> Section 2255(h) requires that a second or successive § 2255 motion be "certified as provided in section 2244 by a panel of the appropriate court of appeals" to contain one of the two showings listed in § 2255(h). *Bowe v. United States* explained that this cross-reference incorporates § 2244(b)(3)(A)–(D), which require a motion in the appropriate court of appeals for an order authorizing the district court to consider the filing, determination of that motion by a three-judge panel, a prima facie showing, and a grant or denial "not later than 30 days after the filing of the motion." *Bowe* observed in a parenthetical, without deciding the point, that "most Courts of Appeals view that requirement as advisory." Section 2244(b)(3)(E) provides that the grant or denial of an authorization "shall not be appealable and shall not be the subject of a petition for rehearing or for a writ of certiorari." *Bowe* held that § 2255(h)'s cross-reference does not clearly incorporate § 2244(b)(3)(E)'s certiorari bar, so a federal prisoner is not prohibited from seeking certiorari from a panel's denial of authorization to file a second or successive § 2255 motion. *Bowe* did not decide whether § 2244(b)(3)(E)'s panel-rehearing bar applies to federal prisoners, so rehearing practice must be checked under current controlling circuit law. The statutes do not prescribe the motion's form, attachments, length limits, filing method, or service; those matters require the current rules and forms of the particular court of appeals. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(3)]; [28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)]; [*Bowe v. United States*, 607 U.S. 13, 18, 28–36 (2026)]
