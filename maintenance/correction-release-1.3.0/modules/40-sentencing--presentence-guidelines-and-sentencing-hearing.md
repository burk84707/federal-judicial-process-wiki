#### CLM-SENT-018: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> The court ordinarily uses one Guidelines Manual in effect on the sentencing date, but the Ex Post Facto Clause forbids using a later manual that creates a significant risk of a higher sentence than the manual in effect when the offense was committed. [U.S.S.G. § 1B1.11]; [*Peugh v. United States*, 569 U.S. 530, 533, 544–50 (2013)]

After:

> The court uses the Guidelines Manual in effect on the sentencing date unless doing so would violate the ex post facto clause, in which case it uses the Manual in effect on the date the offense of conviction was committed, with the last date of the offense of conviction controlling. The Manual in effect on a particular date is applied in its entirety, but a court applying an earlier edition must consider later amendments to the extent they are clarifying rather than substantive; and if the defendant is convicted of two offenses, the first committed before and the second after a revised edition took effect, the revised edition applies to both. [U.S.S.G. § 1B1.11(a), (b)(1)–(3) & comment. (n.2) (Nov. 1, 2026)] There is an ex post facto violation when a defendant is sentenced under Guidelines promulgated after the offense and the new version provides a higher applicable Guidelines sentencing range than the version in place at the time of the offense. [*Peugh v. United States*, 569 U.S. 530, 533, 550 (2013)]
