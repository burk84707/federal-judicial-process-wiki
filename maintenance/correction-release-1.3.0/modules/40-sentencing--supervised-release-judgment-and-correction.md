#### CLM-SENT-053: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Unless another statute provides otherwise, the maximum supervised-release term is five years for a Class A or B felony, three years for a Class C or D felony, and one year for a Class E felony or a nonpetty misdemeanor. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(b)]

After:

> Except as otherwise provided, the maximum supervised-release term is five years for a Class A or B felony, three years for a Class C or D felony, and one year for a Class E felony or a nonpetty misdemeanor; § 3583 itself authorizes any term of years or life for listed terrorism offenses and any term of years not less than five, or life, for listed sex and child offenses. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(b), (j), (k)]

#### CLM-SENT-064: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A party preserves a sentencing issue by informing the court of the requested action or objection and grounds when the ruling is made or sought; requesting a shorter sentence preserves substantive unreasonableness, but a distinct procedural error ordinarily requires a specific objection. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 51(b), 52(b)]; [*Holguin-Hernandez v. United States*, 589 U.S. 169, 173–75 (2020)]

After:

> A party preserves a claim of error by informing the court, when the ruling or order is made or sought, of the action it wishes the court to take, or of its objection and the grounds for it. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 51(b)] Advocating a shorter sentence preserves a claim that a longer sentence is unreasonable, but the Supreme Court expressly declined to decide what is sufficient to preserve a claim that the trial court used improper procedures. [*Holguin-Hernandez v. United States*, 589 U.S. 169 (2020)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Removed the statement that a procedural error ordinarily requires a specific objection, which the majority declined to decide (only a concurrence addressed it), dropped Rule 52(b) which no longer supports the text, and dropped the unverifiable U.S. Reports pinpoint.
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52, Rule 51(b): "informing the court—when the court ruling or order is made or sought—of the action the party wishes the court to take, or the party’s objection to the court’s action and the grounds for that objection."
