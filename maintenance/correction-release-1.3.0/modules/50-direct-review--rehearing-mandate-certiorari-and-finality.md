#### CLM-DR-040: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Current Rule 40 places panel rehearing and rehearing en banc in one rule: a party may seek panel rehearing, rehearing en banc, or both in one document unless a local rule provides otherwise, while en banc rehearing remains not favored. [Fed. R. App. P. 40]

After:

> Current Rule 40 places panel rehearing and rehearing en banc in one rule: a party may seek panel rehearing, rehearing en banc, or both, and unless a local rule provides otherwise, a party seeking both must file the petitions as a single document. Panel rehearing is the ordinary means of reconsidering a panel decision; rehearing en banc is not favored. [Fed. R. App. P. 40(a)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Changed the permissive 'may seek ... both in one document' to the rule's mandatory single-document requirement and narrowed the pinpoint to 40(a).
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(a): "A party may seek rehearing of a decision through a petition for panel rehearing, a petition for rehearing en banc, or both."
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(a): "Unless a local rule provides otherwise, a party seeking both forms of rehearing must file the petitions as a single document."
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(a): "Panel rehearing is the ordinary means of reconsidering a panel decision; rehearing en banc is not favored."

#### CLM-DR-042: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Rehearing en banc ordinarily requires that the proceeding conflict with a Supreme Court or circuit decision and consideration by the full court is needed for uniformity, or that the proceeding involves a question of exceptional importance. [Fed. R. App. P. 40(b)(2)]

After:

> A petition for rehearing en banc must begin with a statement that the panel decision conflicts with a decision of the court to which the petition is addressed (with citation to the conflicting case or cases) and the full court's consideration is therefore necessary to secure or maintain uniformity of the court's decisions; that the panel decision conflicts with a decision of the United States Supreme Court (with citation to the conflicting case or cases); that the panel decision conflicts with an authoritative decision of another United States court of appeals (with citation to the conflicting case or cases); or that the proceeding involves one or more questions of exceptional importance, each concisely stated. Rehearing en banc is not favored and ordinarily will be allowed only if one of those criteria is met. [Fed. R. App. P. 40(b)(2), (c)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-31 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Listed the four separate grounds, confining the uniformity requirement to intra-circuit conflicts, adding the inter-circuit-conflict ground, and citing 40(c) for the 'ordinarily' standard.
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(b)(2)(A): "and the full court's consideration is therefore necessary to secure or maintain uniformity of the court's decisions"
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(b)(2)(B): "the panel decision conflicts with a decision of the United States Supreme Court"
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(b)(2)(C): "the panel decision conflicts with an authoritative decision of another United States court of appeals"
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(b)(2)(D): "the proceeding involves one or more questions of exceptional importance, each concisely stated"
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(c): "Rehearing en banc is not favored and ordinarily will be allowed only if one of the criteria in Rule 40(b)(2)(A)-(D) is met."
- **2026-10-31: Wording conformed to the text of the cited law or policy; independent reviews agreed; owner approved 2026-10-31.** Restated in the cited source's own terms (owner directive: claims follow the law and policy text). The 'not favored ... ordinarily will be allowed only if' sentence quotes Rule 40(c) exactly, so that qualifier is supported. The deviation is in the fourth criterion. Rule 40(b)(2)(D) says 'the proceeding involves one or more questions of exceptional importance, each concisely stated'. In the claim's list the subject of 'involves' is 'the panel decision'. The claim also drops the rule's '(with citation to the conflicting case or cases)' requirement for criteria (A)–(C) and 'each concisely stated' for (D). Both are part of what the petition 'must begin with'. This is a small wording deviation, not a hedge.
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R.   - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(b)(2)(B)–(D): "(B) the panel decision conflicts with a decision of the United States Supreme Court (with citation to the con- flicting case or cases); (C) the panel decision conflicts with an authoritative de- cision of another United States court of appeals (with cita- tion to the conflicting case or cases); or (D) the proceeding involves one or more questions of ex- ceptional importance, each concisely stated."
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(c): "Rehearing en banc is not fa- vored and ordinarily will be allowed only if one of the criteria in Rule 40(b)(2)(A)-(D) is met."

#### CLM-DR-043: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> In a criminal case, a rehearing petition ordinarily must be filed within fourteen days after entry of judgment, but a local rule or court order may shorten or extend that time and Rule 40 supplies a longer government period in specified cases. [Fed. R. App. P. 40(d)(1)]

After:

> Unless the time is shortened or extended by order or local rule, a rehearing petition must be filed within 14 days after judgment is entered or, if the panel later amends its decision, within 14 days after the amended decision is entered. Rule 40's 45-day period for cases in which the United States or its agency, officer, or employee is a party applies only in a civil case. [Fed. R. App. P. 40(d)(1)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Removed the incorrect statement that Rule 40 gives the government a longer period in criminal cases (the 45-day period is limited to civil cases) and added the amended-decision trigger.
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(d)(1): "Unless the time is shortened or extended by order or local rule, any petition for panel rehearing or rehearing en banc must be filed within 14 days after judgment is entered"
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(d)(1): "if the panel later amends its decision (on rehearing or otherwise), within 14 days after the amended decision is entered"
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 40(d)(1): "But in a civil case, unless an order shortens or extends the time, the petition may be filed by any party within 45 days after entry of judgment"

#### CLM-DR-046: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> A motion to stay the mandate pending certiorari must show that the petition would present a substantial question and that there is good cause for a stay; a stay ordinarily may not exceed ninety days unless extended for good cause or a timely certiorari filing is shown. [Fed. R. App. P. 41(d)]

After:

> A motion to stay the mandate pending certiorari must be served on all parties and must show that the petition would present a substantial question and that there is good cause for a stay, and the court may require a bond or other security. The stay must not exceed 90 days unless the period is extended for good cause or the party who obtained the stay notifies the circuit clerk in writing, within the period of the stay, that the time to file the petition has been extended or that the petition has been filed. [Fed. R. App. P. 41(d)(1)–(3)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Restored the mandatory 'must not exceed 90 days,' added the written-notice-within-the-stay requirement and the extension-of-time route, and added service and possible security as the reviewers noted.
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 41(d)(1): "The motion must be served on all parties and must show that the petition would present a substantial question and that there is good cause for a stay."
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 41(d)(2): "The stay must not exceed 90 days, unless: (A) the period is extended for good cause; or"
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 41(d)(2)(B): "the party who obtained the stay notifies the circuit clerk in writing within the period of the stay"
  - AUTH-FRAP-40-41, Fed. R. App. P. 41(d)(3): "The court may require a bond or other security as a condition to granting or continuing a stay of the mandate."

#### CLM-DR-049: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> For good cause, a Justice may extend the certiorari filing time for no more than sixty days; an application ordinarily must be submitted at least ten days before the due date absent extraordinary circumstances, and an extension is not automatic. [Sup. Ct. R. 13.5]

After:

> For good cause, a Justice may extend the certiorari filing time for no more than sixty days; the application must be filed with the Clerk at least ten days before the petition is due, except in extraordinary circumstances, must set out specific reasons why an extension is justified along with the other required contents, and any extension applies solely to the parties named in it. An application to extend the time is not favored. [Sup. Ct. R. 13.5]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Replaced 'not automatic' with the rule's 'not favored,' restored the mandatory filing deadline wording, and added the required-contents and named-parties-only limits.
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-RULES-10-12-13-14-39, Sup. Ct. R. 13.5: "For good cause, a Justice may extend the time to file a petition for a writ of certiorari for a period not exceeding 60 days."
  - AUTH-SCOTUS-RULES-10-12-13-14-39, Sup. Ct. R. 13.5: "The application must be filed with the Clerk at least 10 days before the date the petition is due, except in extraordinary circumstances."
