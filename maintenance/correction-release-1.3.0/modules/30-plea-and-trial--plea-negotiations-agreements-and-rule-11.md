#### CLM-PLEA-002: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> An unexecuted plea proposal, standing alone, is an executory arrangement without constitutional significance; a defendant has no federal constitutional right to specific performance of an offer withdrawn before the guilty plea was entered. [*Mabry v. Johnson*, 467 U.S. 504, 507–11 (1984)]

After:

> A plea bargain standing alone is without constitutional significance; in itself it is a mere executory agreement which, until embodied in the judgment of a court, does not deprive an accused of liberty or any other constitutionally protected interest. In *Mabry*, the defendant's acceptance of an offer that the prosecutor then withdrew created no constitutional right to specific performance, because his later guilty plea was in no sense induced by the withdrawn offer and his inability to enforce that offer did not impair the voluntariness or intelligence of the plea; as *Mabry* explained, under *Santobello* when a plea rests in any significant degree on a promise or agreement of the prosecutor, so that it can be said to be part of the inducement or consideration, such promise must be fulfilled, although *Santobello* declined to hold that the Constitution compels specific performance of a broken prosecutorial promise as the remedy.
