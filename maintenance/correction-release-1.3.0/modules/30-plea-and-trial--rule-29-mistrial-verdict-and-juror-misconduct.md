#### CLM-TRIAL-020: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> When the court reserves decision on a Rule 29 motion made before deliberations or before discharge in a bench trial, it must decide the reserved motion on the evidence as it stood when the ruling was reserved. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 29(b)]

After:

> The court may reserve decision on a Rule 29 motion, proceed with the trial (where the motion is made before the close of all the evidence), submit the case to the jury, and decide the motion before the jury returns a verdict or after it returns a guilty verdict or is discharged without having returned a verdict; if the court reserves decision, it must decide the motion on the basis of the evidence at the time the ruling was reserved. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 29(b)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Removes the invented bench-trial and before-deliberations scope and states Rule 29(b)'s actual timing, keeping its evidence-at-reservation command.
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-26-3-29-31-45, Fed. R. Crim. P. 29(b): "The court may reserve decision on the motion, proceed with the trial (where the motion is made before the close of all the evidence), submit the case to the jury, and de- cide the motion"
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-26-3-29-31-45, Fed. R. Crim. P. 29(b): "either before the jury returns a verdict or after it returns a verdict of guilty or is discharged without having re- turned a verdict."
  - AUTH-FRCRIM-P-26-3-29-31-45, Fed. R. Crim. P. 29(b): "If the court reserves decision, it must decide the motion on the basis of the evidence at the time the ruling was re- served."

#### CLM-TRIAL-022: rewritten

#### CLM-TRIAL-024: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> Sufficiency is measured against the elements of the charged crime, not against an erroneously heightened jury instruction, and the reviewing court does not reweigh the evidence or assess witness credibility. [*Musacchio v. United States*, 577 U.S. 237, 243–44 (2016)]

After:

> When a jury instruction sets forth all the elements of the charged crime but incorrectly adds one more element, sufficiency is assessed against the elements of the charged crime, not the erroneously heightened instruction, and that limited review does not intrude on the jury's role to resolve conflicts in the testimony and to weigh the evidence. The Court left open several matters: it expressed no view on whether sufficiency must be judged by reference to the elements charged in the indictment when the indictment charges one or more elements not required by statute; it did not suggest that the Government adds an element for sufficiency purposes when the indictment charges different means of committing a crime in the conjunctive; and it did not suggest that an erroneous jury instruction cannot result in reversible error just because the evidence was sufficient to support a conviction. [*Musacchio v. United States*, 577 U.S. 237, 243–44 & n.2 (2016)]

#### CLM-TRIAL-026: rewritten

Before (public 1.2.1):

> If the court grants postverdict Rule 29 relief, it must conditionally decide any new-trial motion and state its reasons; the conditional ruling does not affect finality of the acquittal judgment. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 29(d)]

After:

> If the court enters a judgment of acquittal after a guilty verdict, it must also conditionally determine whether any motion for a new trial should be granted if the judgment of acquittal is later vacated or reversed, and must specify the reasons for that determination; an order conditionally granting a new trial does not affect the finality of the judgment of acquittal. If the court conditionally grants a new trial and an appellate court later reverses the judgment of acquittal, the trial court must proceed with the new trial unless the appellate court orders otherwise. If the court conditionally denies a new trial, an appellee may assert that the denial was erroneous, and if the appellate court later reverses the judgment of acquittal, the trial court must proceed as the appellate court directs. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 29(d)]

Verification date: 2026-08-25 → 2026-10-30 (date of the actual review)

- **2026-10-30: Dual independent source review, with second and third independent checks; owner approved 2026-10-30.** Narrows the finality statement to an order conditionally granting a new trial, as Rule 29(d)(2) says, and adds (d)(3)(B)'s right of an appellee to contest a conditional denial. Only part of the earlier reviewer's point was fixed. An independent reviewer flagged that the claim omitted Rule 29(d)(3). The draft adds only the first sentence of (d)(3)(B). It omits (d)(3)(A): "If the court conditionally grants a motion for a new trial and an appellate court later reverses the judgment of acquittal, the trial court must proceed with the new trial unless the appellate court orders otherwise.
