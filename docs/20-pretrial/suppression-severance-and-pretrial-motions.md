# Suppression, Severance, and Pretrial Evidentiary Motions

## Verification boundary

This module supplies the nationwide procedural framework and selected controlling Supreme Court limits. It does not decide whether a particular search, seizure, interrogation, joinder, or item of evidence was lawful; replace the district's motion deadline; resolve a disputed record; or supply circuit-specific burdens, hearing thresholds, standards for unaddressed evidentiary doctrines, conditional-plea requirements, or harmless-error applications.

## Rule 12 motion procedure

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-001 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Rule 12 permits a pretrial motion for a defense, objection, or request that can be decided without a trial on the merits, and Rule 47 governs the motion's form and supporting grounds. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 12(b)(1), 47](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-002 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52 status:verified_current -->
A suppression request and a Rule 14 severance request must be raised before trial when the basis is then reasonably available and the motion can be decided without a trial on the merits. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 12(b)(3)(C)–(D)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-003 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52 status:verified_current -->
At arraignment or as soon afterward as practicable, a defendant may request notice of the government's intent to use in its case-in-chief evidence discoverable under Rule 16 so the defendant has an opportunity to move to suppress; the government's voluntary notice provision is distinct. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 12(b)(4)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-004 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The court may set, extend, or reset a pretrial-motion deadline; if it sets none, the deadline is the start of trial. A late Rule 12(b)(3) motion is untimely, but the court may consider it on a showing of good cause. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 12(c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-005 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The court must decide every pretrial motion before trial unless good cause supports deferral, may not defer if doing so will adversely affect a party's appeal right, and must state essential factual findings on the record. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 12(d)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-006 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52 status:verified_current -->
All suppression-hearing proceedings and oral findings and conclusions must be recorded, and Rule 26.2 applies at the hearing with a law-enforcement officer treated as a government witness. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 12(f), (h)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-007 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Rule 41 distinguishes a motion to return property, filed in the seizure district with evidence received on necessary factual issues, from a defendant's suppression motion, which is filed in the trial court as Rule 12 provides. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 41(g)–(h)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Suppression theories and exclusion limits

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-008 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-RAKAS-439-US-128 status:verified_current -->
Fourth Amendment rights are personal: a defendant must show that the challenged search or seizure violated the defendant's own protected interest rather than rely solely on evidence obtained through a violation of someone else's rights. [*Rakas v. Illinois*, 439 U.S. 128, 133–40 (1978)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-439/pdf/USREPORTS-439-128.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-009 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-RAKAS-439-US-128 status:verified_current -->
For privacy-based search claims, *Rakas* directs the inquiry to whether the defendant had a legitimate expectation of privacy in the place searched or thing invaded; mere presence or a target label is not enough. [*Rakas*, 439 U.S. at 140–50](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-439/pdf/USREPORTS-439-128.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-010 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-FRANKS-438-US-154 status:verified_current -->
A *Franks* evidentiary hearing requires a substantial preliminary showing that a warrant-affidavit false statement was made knowingly and intentionally or with reckless disregard for truth and was necessary to probable cause; conclusory allegations or a mere desire to cross-examine do not suffice. [*Franks v. Delaware*, 438 U.S. 154, 155–56, 170–72 (1978)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-438/pdf/USREPORTS-438-154.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-011 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-FRANKS-438-US-154 status:verified_current -->
If the defendant proves the qualifying falsity by a preponderance at the *Franks* hearing and the affidavit without that material cannot establish probable cause, the warrant is void and the search fruits are excluded to the same extent as if probable cause had been absent on the affidavit's face. [*Franks*, 438 U.S. at 156, 171–72](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-438/pdf/USREPORTS-438-154.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-012 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-LEON-468-US-897 status:verified_current -->
The Fourth Amendment exclusionary rule is a judicial remedy aimed at deterrence, not an automatic personal right to exclusion for every constitutional violation. [*United States v. Leon*, 468 U.S. 897, 906–13 (1984)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-468/pdf/USREPORTS-468-897.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-013 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-LEON-468-US-897 status:verified_current -->
*Leon* ordinarily permits use of evidence obtained in objectively reasonable reliance on a later-invalidated warrant, but identifies limits including a knowingly or recklessly misleading affidavit, abandonment of the judicial role, an affidavit so lacking in probable-cause indicia that reliance is unreasonable, and a facially deficient warrant. [*Leon*, 468 U.S. at 922–23](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-468/pdf/USREPORTS-468-897.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-014 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-WONG-SUN-371-US-471 status:verified_current -->
Evidence is not excluded merely because it would not have come to light but for unlawful police action; the question is whether it was obtained by exploitation of the illegality or instead by means sufficiently distinguishable to purge the primary taint. [*Wong Sun v. United States*, 371 U.S. 471, 487–88 (1963)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-371/pdf/USREPORTS-371-471.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-015 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MIRANDA-384-US-436 status:verified_current -->
*Miranda* addresses statements stemming from custodial interrogation and requires the specified warnings and a valid waiver before the prosecution may use such a statement in its case-in-chief, subject to later Supreme Court refinements and exceptions. [*Miranda v. Arizona*, 384 U.S. 436, 444–45, 467–79 (1966)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-384/pdf/USREPORTS-384-436.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-016 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-DICKERSON-530-US-428 status:verified_current -->
*Dickerson* holds that *Miranda* announced a constitutional rule that Congress could not supersede through 18 U.S.C. § 3501's voluntariness-only approach; voluntariness and *Miranda* compliance remain analytically distinct. [*Dickerson v. United States*, 530 U.S. 428, 432, 437–44 (2000)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-530/pdf/USREPORTS-530-428.pdf)

Search-and-seizure, *Franks*, *Miranda*, voluntariness, and Sixth Amendment statement claims have different elements and remedial limits. They must be classified independently before deciding whether a statement, physical evidence, derivative evidence, identification, or other item is excludable.

## Motions in limine and evidentiary preservation

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-017 authorities:AUTH-FRE-103-104 status:verified_current -->
The Federal Rules do not create one universal criminal “motion in limine” standard; Rules 103 and 104 provide the core evidentiary-ruling and preliminary-question framework, while the particular evidence rule and controlling precedent supply the merits. [Fed. R. Evid. 103–104](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-evidence.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-018 authorities:AUTH-FRE-103-104 status:verified_current -->
To claim erroneous admission, Rule 103 ordinarily requires a timely on-record objection or motion to strike stating the specific ground unless apparent; to claim erroneous exclusion, it ordinarily requires an offer of proof unless the substance was apparent. The ruling must affect a substantial right. [Fed. R. Evid. 103(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-evidence.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-019 authorities:AUTH-FRE-103-104 status:verified_current -->
Once the court definitively rules on the record before or at trial, Rule 103(b) does not require renewal of the objection or offer of proof; whether a pretrial ruling was definitive must be determined from the actual record. [Fed. R. Evid. 103(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-evidence.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-020 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-LUCE-469-US-38 status:verified_current -->
Under *Luce*, a defendant must testify to preserve appellate review of an in-limine ruling allowing impeachment by a prior conviction under Rule 609(a), because otherwise the reviewing court cannot know the testimony, whether impeachment occurred, or its effect. [*Luce v. United States*, 469 U.S. 38, 41–43 (1984)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-469/pdf/USREPORTS-469-38.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-021 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-OHLER-529-US-753 status:verified_current -->
When a defendant preemptively introduces the conviction on direct examination, *Ohler* applies the general rule that a party introducing evidence cannot complain on appeal that the evidence was erroneously admitted. [*Ohler v. United States*, 529 U.S. 753, 755–60 (2000)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-753.pdf)

## Severance

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-022 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52 status:verified_current -->
If joinder of offenses or defendants appears prejudicial, Rule 14 permits separate trials of counts, severance of defendants, or other relief justice requires; before ruling, the court may inspect in camera a defendant's statement the government intends to use. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 14](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-023 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ZAFIRO-506-US-534 status:verified_current -->
Rule 14 does not require severance merely because codefendants present mutually antagonistic or mutually exclusive defenses. [*Zafiro v. United States*, 506 U.S. 534, 538–39 (1993)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-506/pdf/USREPORTS-506-534.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-024 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ZAFIRO-506-US-534 status:verified_current -->
*Zafiro* directs relief only when a joint trial creates a serious risk of compromising a specific trial right or preventing a reliable guilt-or-innocence judgment, and even then the remedy need not be severance because limiting instructions or other measures may suffice. [*Zafiro*, 506 U.S. at 539–41](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-506/pdf/USREPORTS-506-534.pdf)

## Interlocutory review and preservation

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-025 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3731 status:verified_current -->
Before jeopardy and verdict, 18 U.S.C. § 3731 authorizes a government appeal from an order suppressing or excluding evidence or requiring return of property if the United States attorney certifies that the appeal is not for delay and the evidence is substantial proof of a material fact. [18 U.S.C. § 3731](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A3731+edition%3Aprelim%29)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-026 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3731 status:verified_current -->
A § 3731 appeal must be taken within 30 days and diligently prosecuted, remains subject to the Double Jeopardy Clause, and is governed by Congress's direction to construe the section liberally. [18 U.S.C. § 3731](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A3731+edition%3Aprelim%29)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-027 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ABNEY-431-US-651 status:verified_current -->
Criminal defendants ordinarily obtain appellate review after final judgment; *Abney* recognizes immediate collateral-order review for a colorable denial of a motion to dismiss on Double Jeopardy grounds, not a general interlocutory route for accompanying suppression, severance, or indictment arguments. [*Abney v. United States*, 431 U.S. 651, 656–63 (1977)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-431/pdf/USREPORTS-431-651.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-028 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52,AUTH-FRE-103-104 status:verified_current -->
Preservation is issue-specific: Rule 12(c)(3) governs an untimely required pretrial motion, Rule 51 requires the requested action or a timely specific objection when the ruling is made, and Evidence Rule 103 governs evidentiary objections, offers of proof, and definitive rulings. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 12(c)(3), 51](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [Fed. R. Evid. 103](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-evidence.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-029 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-12-14-41-47-51-52,AUTH-FRE-103-104 status:verified_current -->
Rule 52 distinguishes harmless preserved error from plain error affecting substantial rights, but neither Rule 52 nor Evidence Rule 103(e) guarantees relief for an unpreserved claim; the governing elements and record still control. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 52](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [Fed. R. Evid. 103(e)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-evidence.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-MOTION-030 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ORNELAS-517-US-690 status:verified_current -->
On appeal after a suppression ruling, *Ornelas* requires independent review of ultimate reasonable-suspicion and probable-cause determinations, while historical facts receive clear-error review and due weight is given to inferences drawn by resident judges and local officers. [*Ornelas v. United States*, 517 U.S. 690, 696–700 (1996)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-517/pdf/USREPORTS-517-690.pdf)

The notice of appeal, plea, reservation of issues, trial record, renewed objections, offers of proof, and circuit doctrine must be checked before representing any pretrial ruling as reviewable or preserved.
