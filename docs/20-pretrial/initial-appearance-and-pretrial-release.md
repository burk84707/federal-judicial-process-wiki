# Initial Appearance and Pretrial Release or Detention

## Verification boundary

This module replaces the legacy pretrial chart with the nationwide Rule 5, Rule 5.1, and 18 U.S.C. § 3142 framework. It separates presentment delay, warrantless-arrest probable-cause review, and confession admissibility; they are related events with different legal tests. It does not decide a particular defendant's release, a district's local practice, a circuit-specific flight burden, review under § 3145, or whether counsel must be present at a particular proceeding beyond the attachment rule decided in *Rothgery*.

## Three clocks that must not be merged

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-001 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-5-5-1 status:verified_current -->
For an arrest within the United States, Rule 5(a)(1)(A) requires the arresting person to take the defendant without unnecessary delay before a magistrate judge, or a qualifying state or local judicial officer under Rule 5(c), unless a statute provides otherwise. Rule 5(a)(2) excepts certain arrests on a complaint charging solely a violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1073, and provides that Rule 32.1 applies to an arrest for violating probation or supervised release and Rule 40 to an arrest for failing to appear in another district. The rule does not establish a 48-hour presentment threshold. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 5(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-002 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MCLAUGHLIN-500-US-44 status:verified_current -->
The 48-hour framework in County of Riverside v. McLaughlin concerns the Fourth Amendment requirement of a prompt judicial probable-cause determination after a warrantless arrest. A determination within 48 hours generally satisfies promptness, although an arrested person may prove unreasonable delay; after 48 hours the government must show a bona fide emergency or other extraordinary circumstance. [*County of Riverside v. McLaughlin*, 500 U.S. 44, 56–57 (1991)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-500/pdf/USREPORTS-500-44.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-003 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-CORLEY-556-US-303 status:verified_current -->
For a federal confession, 18 U.S.C. § 3501(c) provides that a confession made within six hours of arrest (or a longer period reasonable considering the means of transportation and the distance to the nearest available magistrate judge) is not inadmissible solely because of presentment delay; within that window it is admissible, subject to the other Rules of Evidence, if made voluntarily. If the confession occurred before presentment and beyond six hours, the court must decide whether the delay was unreasonable or unnecessary under the McNabb–Mallory cases, and if it was, the confession is to be suppressed. [Corley v. United States, 556 U.S. 303, 322 (2009)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/556bv.pdf)

These are not interchangeable rules: Rule 5 asks whether presentment was unnecessarily delayed; *McLaughlin* addresses prompt probable-cause review of a warrantless arrest; and *Corley* addresses whether a federal confession may be excluded because of presentment delay.

## Rule 5 initial appearance

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-004 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-5-5-1 status:verified_current -->
After a warrantless arrest, a complaint satisfying Rule 4(a)'s probable-cause requirement must be promptly filed in the district where the offense allegedly occurred. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 5(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-005 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-5-5-1 status:verified_current -->
Rule 5(c) determines the place and transfer procedure for the initial appearance. The ordinary location depends on whether the arrest occurred in the charging district, another district, or after extradition; the chart therefore cannot assume every arrest goes immediately before a magistrate in the charging district. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 5(c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-006 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-5-5-1 status:verified_current -->
In a felony case, Rule 5(d)(1) requires advice concerning: the complaint and any filed affidavit; retained or appointed counsel; circumstances for pretrial release; any preliminary-hearing right; the right not to make a statement and the possible use of a statement; and consular notification for a noncitizen. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 5(d)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-007 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-5-5-1 status:verified_current -->
The judge must allow a reasonable opportunity to consult counsel and must release or detain the defendant as statutes and rules provide. Rule 5 itself does not make Sixth Amendment attachment a statutory trigger for a detention hearing. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 5(d)(2)–(3)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-008 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-2025-PAMPHLET status:verified_current -->
A preliminary hearing is not required in every case. If a defendant is charged with an offense other than a petty offense, Rule 5.1(a) requires a magistrate judge to conduct a preliminary hearing unless the defendant waives the hearing; the defendant is indicted; the government files an information under Rule 7(b) charging the defendant with a felony; the government files an information charging the defendant with a misdemeanor; or the defendant is charged with a misdemeanor and consents to trial before a magistrate judge. Unless a magistrate judge extends the time limits under Rule 5.1(d), the magistrate judge must hold the preliminary hearing within a reasonable time, but no later than 14 days after the initial appearance if the defendant is in custody and no later than 21 days if not in custody. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 5.1(a), (c)–(d) (Dec. 1, 2025 ed.)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Sixth Amendment attachment is a separate question

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-009 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ROTHGERY-554-US-191 status:verified_current -->
Rothgery held that an initial appearance before a judicial officer at which the defendant learns the formal accusation and liberty is restricted initiates adversary judicial proceedings and triggers attachment of the Sixth Amendment right to counsel; prosecutor awareness or participation is not required. [*Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191, 194–95, 213 (2008)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/554bv.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-010 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ROTHGERY-554-US-191,AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
Attachment and critical-stage analysis are distinct. Rothgery did not hold that attachment itself creates a § 3142 detention hearing, nor did it decide the scope of counsel's required presence at every postattachment proceeding; § 3142(f), not attachment, defines when a detention hearing is held. [*Rothgery*, 554 U.S. at 212 & nn.15–17](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/554bv.pdf); [18 U.S.C. § 3142(f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

## Release and detention structure

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-011 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
Section 3142(a) supplies four possible pretrial orders: release on personal recognizance or unsecured appearance bond; release on conditions; temporary detention under subsection (d); or detention under subsection (e). [18 U.S.C. § 3142(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-012 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
Section 3142(b) makes recognizance or unsecured-bond release the starting rule unless the judicial officer determines that it will not reasonably assure appearance or will endanger another person or the community. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-013 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
If subsection (b) is insufficient, subsection (c) requires the least restrictive further condition or combination that will reasonably assure appearance and safety. A financial condition may not be imposed if it results in pretrial detention. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(c)(1)–(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

## The § 3142(f) hearing gate

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-014 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
On the government's motion, § 3142(f)(1) requires a detention hearing in a case that involves: a crime of violence, a § 1591 violation, or a listed terrorism offense with a maximum term of 10 years or more; an offense with a maximum sentence of life imprisonment or death; a qualifying drug offense with a maximum term of ten years or more; any felony if the person has been convicted of two or more offenses described in (A) through (C), or state or local equivalents, or a combination of such offenses; or any felony that is not otherwise a crime of violence that involves a minor victim, the possession or use of a firearm, destructive device, or any other dangerous weapon, or a § 2250 failure to register. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(f)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-015 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
On the government's motion or the court's own motion, § 3142(f)(2) requires a hearing in a case involving a serious risk of flight or a serious risk that the person will obstruct or attempt to obstruct justice, or threaten, injure, or intimidate a prospective witness or juror. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(f)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-016 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
A § 3142(f) category triggers a hearing to determine whether any condition or combination of conditions will reasonably assure appearance and safety, not mandatory detention. The hearing shall be held immediately upon the person's first appearance unless the person or the government seeks a continuance; except for good cause, a defense continuance may not exceed five days and a government continuance three days (not including any intermediate Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday), and the person shall be detained during a continuance. The person has the right to counsel, appointed if financially unable to obtain adequate representation, and an opportunity to testify, present witnesses, cross-examine witnesses who appear, and present information by proffer or otherwise; the rules concerning admissibility of evidence in criminal trials do not apply. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(e)(1), (f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

## Decision, burdens, and presumptions

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-017 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
After the subsection (f) hearing, § 3142(e)(1) requires detention only if the judicial officer finds that no condition or combination will reasonably assure the person's appearance and the safety of another person and the community. Subsection (e)(1) is the detention rule; it is not a rebuttable-presumption category. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(e)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-018 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
The facts supporting a detention finding based on inability to reasonably assure safety must be established by clear and convincing evidence. Section 3142(f) does not state the burden of proof for a flight-based detention finding, so this nationwide module does not assign one without controlling circuit authority. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-019 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
Section 3142(e)(2) creates a safety-only rebuttable presumption in a subsection (f)(1) case only when the person has a described prior conviction, committed that prior offense while on pretrial release, and is within five years of the later of that conviction or release from imprisonment. It is not the ten-year-drug-offense presumption. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(e)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-020 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
Section 3142(e)(3) creates a rebuttable presumption concerning both appearance and safety upon probable cause for the specifically listed drug, firearm, terrorism, trafficking, and minor-victim offenses. A § 924(c) offense appears in this subsection, but the complete statutory list and thresholds control. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(e)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-021 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
Both statutory presumptions are subject to rebuttal. Their application does not itself produce an automatic detention order; the judicial officer must apply the statute's hearing, factors, and no-adequate-condition framework. The nationwide effect of rebuttal on the burdens of production and persuasion is not resolved here. [18 U.S.C. § 3142(e)–(g)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

<!-- claim:CLM-PRETRIAL-022 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3142 status:verified_current -->
The § 3142(c) conditions menu includes third-party custody and restrictions on travel, associations, intoxicants, and controlled substances, among other options; it also permits any other reasonably necessary condition. Every release under § 3142(c) is subject to the no-new-crime condition and, where authorized, DNA-sample collection, and the judicial officer must otherwise impose the least restrictive further condition or combination of conditions that will reasonably assure appearance and safety, except that in a listed minor-victim case or a § 2250 failure-to-register case any release order shall contain, at a minimum, electronic monitoring and the conditions in subparagraphs (iv) through (viii). [18 U.S.C. § 3142(c)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3142)

## Record-first checklist

1. Identify the arrest authority, warrant status, arrest time, district of arrest, charging district, complaint and affidavit, and initial-appearance time.
2. Keep Rule 5 presentment, *McLaughlin* probable-cause timing, and *Corley* confession admissibility on separate timelines.
3. Record every Rule 5(d) advisement and the opportunity to consult counsel.
4. Determine whether Rule 5.1 requires a preliminary hearing and calculate the applicable custody or release deadline.
5. Start with § 3142(b), then test § 3142(c) and any § 3142(d) issue before reaching detention.
6. For detention, identify the exact § 3142(f) trigger, the authorized movant, any § 3142(e) presumption, the § 3142(g) evidence, and the required findings and order.
7. Research the controlling circuit and local rules for the flight burden, evidentiary practice, reopening, review under § 3145, and counsel-presence questions not decided by *Rothgery*.

## Matters not yet verified in this module

- A nationwide burden of proof for flight-based detention; § 3142 states clear and convincing evidence only for the safety finding.
- Circuit-specific operation of a rebutted presumption, permissible hearsay or proffers, reopening, and review under § 3145.
- Whether a particular initial appearance or detention hearing is a critical stage requiring counsel's presence under the Sixth Amendment.
- Any individual presentment remedy, confession ruling, probable-cause violation, release condition, detention finding, or appellate remedy.
- District-specific forms, standing orders, filing practices, and calendars.

Those applications remain open in `data/coverage_matrix.yaml`; the module verifies the corrected nationwide structure, not every pretrial subject in the project specification.
