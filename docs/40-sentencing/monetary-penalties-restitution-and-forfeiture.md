# Monetary Penalties, Restitution, and Criminal Forfeiture

## Verification boundary

This module verifies general nationwide fine, special-assessment, restitution, and criminal-forfeiture structure. It does not establish that a particular offense triggers restitution or forfeiture, identify a victim or property nexus, calculate loss or proceeds, determine ownership, select a payment schedule, or resolve offense-specific statutes, plea terms, limitations, enforcement, remission, or circuit doctrines.

## Fines and special assessments

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-032 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3013-3571-3572 status:verified_current -->
Section 3571 authorizes a fine and generally sets the maximum as the greatest of the offense-specific amount, the alternative gain-or-loss amount when usable, or the class-based amount, subject to the statute's specific lower-fine exception. [18 U.S.C. § 3571](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3571)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-033 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3572 status:verified_current -->
In determining whether to impose a fine, and its amount, time for payment, and method of payment, the court shall consider § 3572(a)'s factors in addition to the § 3553(a) factors; if the defendant must make restitution to a victim of the offense other than the United States, the court shall impose a fine or other monetary penalty only to the extent it will not impair the defendant's ability to make restitution. [18 U.S.C. § 3572(a), (b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3572)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-034 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-SOUTHERN-UNION-567-US-343 status:verified_current -->
The *Apprendi* rule applies to criminal fines, so a fact necessary to raise the statutory maximum fine must be admitted or found by a jury beyond a reasonable doubt; where a fine is so insubstantial that the underlying offense is considered petty, the Sixth Amendment jury-trial right is not triggered and no *Apprendi* issue arises. [*Southern Union Co. v. United States*, 567 U.S. 343, 346, 350–60 (2012)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-567/pdf/USREPORTS-567-343.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-035 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3013-3571-3572 status:verified_current -->
Section 3013 requires a separate assessment for every conviction against the United States: for an individual, $5 for an infraction or Class C misdemeanor, $10 for a Class B misdemeanor, $25 for a Class A misdemeanor, and $100 for a felony; for a nonindividual, the corresponding amounts are $25, $50, $125, and $400. [18 U.S.C. § 3013(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3013)

## Restitution authority and procedure

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-036 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3663,AUTH-USC-18-3663A status:verified_current -->
Section 3663 provides that the court may order restitution for its covered convictions other than § 3663A(c) offenses, may decline to the extent the complication and prolongation of sentencing outweighs the need for restitution, and may order restitution in any criminal case to the extent agreed in a plea agreement. [18 U.S.C. § 3663(a)(1), (a)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3663?link-type=html) Section 3663A provides that the court shall order restitution for its listed offense categories in which an identifiable victim has suffered a physical injury or pecuniary loss, and applies to a plea agreement that does not result in conviction of a listed offense only if the plea specifically states that a listed offense gave rise to the plea agreement. [18 U.S.C. § 3663A(a)(1), (c)(1)–(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3663A?link-type=html) Section 3663A does not apply to its listed property or anti-doping offenses if the court finds, from facts on the record, that the number of identifiable victims makes restitution impracticable or that complex issues of fact would burden sentencing so much that the need for restitution is outweighed. [18 U.S.C. § 3663A(c)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3663A?link-type=html)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-037 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3663A status:verified_current -->
For the Mandatory Victims Restitution Act, a victim is a person directly and proximately harmed as a result of the commission of an offense for which restitution may be ordered, including, for an offense that involves as an element a scheme, conspiracy, or pattern of criminal activity, any person directly harmed by the defendant's criminal conduct in the course of it; the court shall also order restitution to persons other than the victim if the parties agreed to it in a plea agreement. [18 U.S.C. § 3663A(a)(2)–(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3663A?link-type=html)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-038 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3663-3663A-3664 status:verified_current -->
For mandatory restitution governed by § 3664, the court determines the full loss amount without considering the defendant's economic circumstances, then considers resources, projected income, and obligations when setting the manner and schedule of payment. [18 U.S.C. § 3664(f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3664)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-039 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3663-3663A-3664 status:verified_current -->
Restitution disputes are decided by a preponderance: the government bears loss amount, the defendant bears resources and dependent needs, and the court assigns other matters as justice requires. [18 U.S.C. § 3664(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3664)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-040 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3663-3663A-3664 status:verified_current -->
If victim losses are not ascertainable ten days before sentencing, the government or probation must notify the court and the court must set a final-loss date no later than ninety days after sentencing; a victim later discovering additional losses has sixty days from discovery to seek amendment for good cause. [18 U.S.C. § 3664(d)(5)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3664)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-041 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-DOLAN-560-US-605 status:verified_current -->
Missing § 3664(d)(5)'s ninety-day date does not eliminate restitution power when the sentencing court made clear before the deadline that restitution would be ordered and left only the amount open. [*Dolan v. United States*, 560 U.S. 605, 608, 611–12 (2010)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-560/pdf/USREPORTS-560-605.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-042 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3663-3663A-3664 status:verified_current -->
When multiple defendants contributed to one victim's loss, § 3664(h) permits full liability for each or apportionment reflecting contribution and economic circumstances; the statute does not impose one universal allocation. [18 U.S.C. § 3664(h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3664)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-043 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3663-3663A-3664 status:verified_current -->
A restitution sentence is a final judgment despite § 3664(o)'s enumerated correction, appeal, amendment, adjustment, and resentencing routes; later modification requires one of those authorized routes rather than general equitable power. [18 U.S.C. § 3664(o)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3664)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-044 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-LAGOS-584-US-577 status:verified_current -->
The Mandatory Victims Restitution Act's § 3663A(b)(4) “investigation” and “proceedings” expense language covers government investigations and criminal proceedings, not civil or bankruptcy proceedings or a private investigation the victim chooses on its own to conduct; the Court did not decide whether it covers similar expenses of a private investigation pursued at the government's invitation or request. [*Lagos v. United States*, 584 U.S. 577, 579–85 (2018)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-584/pdf/USREPORTS-584-577.pdf)

## Criminal forfeiture

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-045 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36 status:verified_current -->
A criminal forfeiture judgment requires notice in the indictment or information that the government will seek forfeiture as part of the sentence, but the charging paper need not identify the property or the amount of a money judgment. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.2(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-046 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36 status:verified_current -->
As soon as practical after a verdict or finding of guilty, or after a plea of guilty or nolo contendere is accepted, the court must determine the required property nexus or money judgment under the applicable statute; the determination may be based on evidence already in the record, including any written plea agreement, and on additional evidence the parties submit that the court accepts as relevant and reliable. If the forfeiture is contested, on either party's request the court must conduct a hearing after the verdict or finding of guilty. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.2(b)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-047 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36,AUTH-SCOTUS-LIBRETTI-516-US-29 status:verified_current -->
In a jury case, Rule 32.2 gives either party a right—if requested before deliberations—to have the jury decide whether the government proved the required nexus for specific property, even though *Libretti* holds that criminal forfeiture itself is not constitutionally protected by the Sixth Amendment jury right. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.2(b)(5)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [*Libretti v. United States*, 516 U.S. 29, 48–49 (1995)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-29.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-048 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36 status:verified_current -->
Unless doing so is impractical, the court must enter a preliminary forfeiture order sufficiently in advance of sentencing to allow the parties to suggest revisions; the order becomes final as to the defendant at sentencing, or at any time before sentencing if the defendant consents, but an order for specific property remains preliminary as to third parties until the ancillary proceeding is concluded. The court must include the forfeiture when orally announcing the sentence or otherwise ensure the defendant knows of it at sentencing, and must include the forfeiture order, directly or by reference, in the judgment. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.2(b)(2)(B), (b)(4)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-049 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36 status:verified_current -->
Failure to include forfeiture in the judgment may be corrected at any time under Rule 36, but only when the court failed to include an already ordered forfeiture; Rule 36 does not create an unentered substantive forfeiture judgment. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.2(b)(4)(B), 36](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-050 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36 status:verified_current -->
Third-party interests are determined in Rule 32.2(c)'s ancillary proceeding, which the court must conduct if a third party files a petition as prescribed by statute, except to the extent the forfeiture consists of a money judgment; the defendant may not object to entry of the final order on the ground that the property belongs, in whole or in part, to a codefendant or third party. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.2(b)(2)(A), (c)(1)–(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-051 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-982,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36 status:verified_current -->
Section 982 requires forfeiture only for the offenses and property categories it specifies, and the forfeiture, including any seizure, disposition, and related judicial or administrative proceeding, is governed by 21 U.S.C. § 853 other than § 853(d); substitute assets may not be ordered against a money-laundering defendant who acted merely as an intermediary who handled but did not retain the property, unless the defendant conducted three or more separate transactions totaling $100,000 or more in any twelve-month period. [18 U.S.C. § 982(a), (b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/982) When the government seeks specific property, the court must determine whether it has established the requisite nexus between the property and the offense. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.2(b)(1)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Application checklist

1. Separate the fine, special assessment, restitution, and forfeiture; each has a different source, trigger, calculation, and amendment route.
2. Identify the statute of conviction, offense date, victim definition, causal boundary, covered losses, forfeiture provision, and property category.
3. Preserve loss evidence, offsets, insurance, resources, dependents, payment schedule, government notice, and every deadline.
4. Request a forfeiture jury determination before deliberations when specific property nexus will be disputed.
5. Compare the oral sentence, preliminary forfeiture order, final order, restitution findings, Statement of Reasons, and written judgment.

## Open items requiring individualized or circuit research

- Offense-specific restitution and forfeiture statutes, plea expansions, proximate cause, loss methodologies, substitute assets, money judgments, tracing, and statutes of limitation.
- Victim and third-party standing, ownership, notice, ancillary petitions, enforcement, offsets, liens, default, remission, and collection.
- Review standards, preservation, harmlessness, delegation of payment schedules, joint liability after later payments, and finality of the actual judgment.
