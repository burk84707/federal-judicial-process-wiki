# Presentence Investigation, Guidelines, and Sentencing Hearing

## Verification boundary

This module verifies current nationwide Rule 32 procedure, the general statutory sentencing framework, the 2025 Guidelines calculation sequence, the advisory-Guidelines system, constitutional statutory-range rules, and bounded preservation principles. It does not calculate an offense-specific range or penalty, resolve disputed facts, choose a sentence, construe a plea agreement, or supply circuit-specific burdens, departures, preservation applications, or reasonableness outcomes.

## Presentence investigation and disclosure

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-001 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The court must impose sentence without unnecessary delay and may change Rule 32's time limits for good cause. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-002 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52,AUTH-USC-18-3552 status:verified_current -->
Before the court imposes sentence, the probation officer must conduct a presentence investigation and submit a report unless 18 U.S.C. § 3593(c) or another statute requires otherwise, or the court finds that the information in the record enables it to meaningfully exercise its sentencing authority under § 3553 and explains that finding on the record. Separately, if the law permits restitution, the probation officer must conduct an investigation and submit a report that contains sufficient information for the court to order restitution. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(c)(1)(A)–(B)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [18 U.S.C. § 3552(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3552)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-003 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
A probation officer who interviews the defendant as part of a presentence investigation must, on request, give defense counsel notice and a reasonable opportunity to attend the interview. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(c)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-004 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52,AUTH-USC-18-3552 status:verified_current -->
Unless the defendant waives the minimum period, the probation officer must give the presentence report to the defendant, defense counsel, and the government at least 35 days before sentencing, but the court may, for good cause, change any time limit in Rule 32. Section 3552(d) separately directs the court to assure disclosure at least ten days before the date set for sentencing, unless the defendant waives that minimum period. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(e)(2), (b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [18 U.S.C. § 3552(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3552)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-005 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Within 14 days after receiving the presentence report, the parties must state in writing any objections, including objections to material information, sentencing guideline ranges, and policy statements contained in or omitted from the report, and an objecting party must provide a copy to the opposing party and the probation officer; the court may change the time for good cause. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(f)(1)–(2), (b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-006 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
At least seven days before sentencing, the probation officer must submit the report and an addendum identifying unresolved objections, grounds for those objections, and the officer's comments, unless the court changes the period for good cause. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(g), (b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Sentencing-hearing procedure and preservation

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-007 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Before imposing sentence, the court must verify that the defendant and defense counsel read and discussed the presentence report and any addendum. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(1)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-008 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The court must allow the parties' attorneys to comment on probation determinations and other sentencing matters and, for each disputed portion of the report or controverted matter, must rule on the dispute or determine that a ruling is unnecessary because it will not affect sentencing or will not be considered; the written determinations must accompany copies of the report made available to the Bureau of Prisons. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(1)(C), (i)(3)(B)–(C)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-009 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Before sentence, the court must address the defendant personally and permit the defendant to speak or present information in mitigation; permitting counsel to speak does not replace the defendant's personal allocution. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(4)(A)(ii)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-010 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Before imposing sentence, the court must also give defense counsel an opportunity to speak on the defendant's behalf and give the government an opportunity to speak equivalent to that of defense counsel, and it must address any victim of the crime who is present at sentencing and permit the victim to be reasonably heard. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(4)(A)(i), (iii), (B)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-011 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
For good cause, the court may allow a party to make a new objection at any time before sentence is imposed. A party preserves a claim of error by informing the court, when the ruling is made or sought, of the action it wishes the court to take or its objection and the grounds; a party that has no opportunity to object is not later prejudiced by the absence of an objection, and a plain error that affects substantial rights may be considered even though it was not brought to the court's attention. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(1)(D), 51(b), 52(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-012 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
If the court contemplates a Guidelines departure on a ground not identified in the presentence report or a party's prehearing submission, it must give reasonable notice specifying that ground. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(h)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-013 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-IRIZARRY-553-US-708 status:verified_current -->
Rule 32(h)'s notice command applies to Guidelines departures, not to a variance selected under § 3553(a); *Irizarry* therefore rejected a categorical Rule 32(h) notice requirement for variances. [*Irizarry v. United States*, 553 U.S. 708, 713–16 (2008)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-553/pdf/USREPORTS-553-708.pdf)

## Statutory sentencing framework and reasons

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-014 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
Section 3553(a) requires a sentence sufficient but not greater than necessary to serve its listed purposes after consideration of: the offense and defendant; the permitted purposes; available sentences; the kinds of sentence and the Guidelines range in effect on the date the defendant is sentenced (except as provided in § 3742(g)) or, for a probation or supervised-release violation, the Commission's applicable guidelines or policy statements; any pertinent policy statement in effect on the sentencing date (subject to the same § 3742(g) exception); unwarranted disparities; and victim restitution. [18 U.S.C. § 3553(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3553)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-015 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
At sentencing the court must state its reasons in open court; § 3553(c) adds point-within-range reasons when the range exceeds 24 months, and the specific reason for a different kind of sentence or a sentence outside the range, which must also be stated with specificity in a statement of reasons form except to the extent the court relies on statements received in camera under Rule 32. If the court relies on such in-camera statements, it must state that they were so received and that it relied on their content. If the court does not order restitution, or orders only partial restitution, it must include the reason in the statement. [18 U.S.C. § 3553(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3553)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-016 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BOOKER-543-US-220 status:verified_current -->
*Booker* rendered the federal Guidelines advisory by severing and excising § 3553(b)(1) and § 3742(e), while requiring the sentencing court to calculate and consider the Guidelines with the other § 3553(a) factors. [*United States v. Booker*, 543 U.S. 220, 245, 259–65 (2005)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-543/pdf/USREPORTS-543-220.pdf)

## Guidelines calculation, departures, and variances

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-017 authorities:AUTH-USSG-2026-SENTENCING-CALCULATION,AUTH-USSG-2026-MANUAL,AUTH-FR-91-24088 status:verified_current -->
The Guidelines direct a sequenced calculation: select the offense guideline, determine the base offense level and specific offense characteristics, apply Chapter Three victim, role, and obstruction adjustments, determine the combined offense level for multiple counts, apply acceptance of responsibility, determine the criminal-history category, determine the guideline range and the Chapter Five sentencing requirements and options, and apply Part K of Chapter Five as appropriate; the court then considers the other applicable § 3553(a) factors. For multiple counts, the amendments effective Nov. 1, 2026 replace the five guidelines in Chapter Three, Part D with a single guideline, § 3D1.1, which determines the combined offense level applicable to all counts; earlier editions grouped the counts under Part D. [U.S.S.G. § 1B1.1(a)–(b) (2026)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2026-guidelines-manual/annotated-2026-chapter-1); [U.S.S.G. § 3D1.1 (Nov. 1, 2026)](https://www.ussc.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/guidelines-manual/2026/GLMFull.pdf); [91 Fed. Reg. 24,088, 24,096, 24,108 (May 4, 2026)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-05-04/html/2026-08647.htm)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-018 authorities:AUTH-USSG-2026-SENTENCING-CALCULATION,AUTH-SCOTUS-PEUGH-569-US-530 status:verified_current -->
The court uses the Guidelines Manual in effect on the sentencing date unless doing so would violate the ex post facto clause, in which case it uses the Manual in effect on the date the offense of conviction was committed, with the last date of the offense of conviction controlling. The Manual in effect on a particular date is applied in its entirety, but a court applying an earlier edition must consider later amendments to the extent they are clarifying rather than substantive; and if the defendant is convicted of two offenses, the first committed before and the second after a revised edition took effect, the revised edition applies to both. [U.S.S.G. § 1B1.11(a), (b)(1)–(3) & comment. (n.2) (Nov. 1, 2026)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2026-guidelines-manual/annotated-2026-chapter-1) There is an ex post facto violation when a defendant is sentenced under Guidelines promulgated after the offense and the new version provides a higher applicable Guidelines sentencing range than the version in place at the time of the offense. [*Peugh v. United States*, 569 U.S. 530, 533, 550 (2013)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-569/pdf/USREPORTS-569-530.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-019 authorities:AUTH-USSG-2025-SENTENCING-CALCULATION status:verified_current -->
The 2025 Guidelines Manual removed departures. The Commission states that it framed the change to be outcome neutral, intending that judges who would have relied on facts previously identified as a basis for a departure would continue to have the authority to rely on those facts to impose a sentence outside the applicable guideline range as a variance under 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a); it adds that the removal does not limit the information courts may consider in imposing a sentence, and that Appendix B compiles the departure provisions as they were last provided in the 2024 edition. [U.S.S.G. ch. 1, pt. A, intro. comment. (Nov. 1, 2025)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2025-guidelines-manual/annotated-2025-chapter-1)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-020 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-GALL-552-US-38 status:verified_current -->
The court must begin with a correct Guidelines calculation, permit arguments, consider § 3553(a), avoid clearly erroneous facts, explain the sentence, and justify any variance; appellate review of every sentence is for abuse of discretion, without a proportional mathematical rule or extraordinary-circumstances requirement. [*Gall v. United States*, 552 U.S. 38, 49–51 (2007)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-552/pdf/USREPORTS-552-38.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-021 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-RITA-551-US-338 status:verified_current -->
An appellate court may apply a nonbinding presumption of reasonableness to a properly calculated within-Guidelines sentence, but the sentencing court may not presume that the Guidelines sentence is reasonable. [*Rita v. United States*, 551 U.S. 338, 347–51 (2007)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-551/pdf/USREPORTS-551-338.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-022 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-KIMBROUGH-552-US-85 status:verified_current -->
Because the Guidelines are advisory, a court may conclude that the crack-cocaine guideline produces a sentence greater than necessary even in an ordinary case; *Kimbrough* permits a variance based on that policy disagreement within statutory limits. [*Kimbrough v. United States*, 552 U.S. 85, 101–10 (2007)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-552/pdf/USREPORTS-552-85.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-023 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-PEPPER-562-US-476 status:verified_current -->
When a defendant's sentence has been set aside on appeal, the district court at resentencing may consider evidence of the defendant's postsentencing rehabilitation, and that evidence may, in appropriate cases, support a downward variance from the advisory Guidelines range. [*Pepper v. United States*, 562 U.S. 476, 481, 490–93 (2011)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-562/pdf/USREPORTS-562-476.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-024 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-TAPIA-564-US-319 status:verified_current -->
A court may discuss treatment and recommend programs, but it may not impose or lengthen imprisonment to promote rehabilitation. [*Tapia v. United States*, 564 U.S. 319, 326–35 (2011)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-564/pdf/USREPORTS-564-319.pdf)

## Statutory-range facts and mandatory-minimum routes

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-025 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-APPRENDI-530-US-466,AUTH-SCOTUS-BOOKER-543-US-220 status:verified_current -->
Other than the fact of a prior conviction, any fact that increases the penalty for a crime beyond the prescribed statutory maximum must be submitted to a jury and proved beyond a reasonable doubt; *Apprendi* also quotes *Jones*'s statement, construing a federal statute, that such a fact must be charged in an indictment. [*Apprendi v. New Jersey*, 530 U.S. 466, 476, 490 (2000)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-530/pdf/USREPORTS-530-466.pdf) *Booker* reaffirmed the rule as requiring that such a fact be admitted by the defendant or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. [*United States v. Booker*, 543 U.S. 220, 244 (2005)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-543/pdf/USREPORTS-543-220.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-026 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ALLEYNE-570-US-99 status:verified_current -->
A fact that increases a statutory mandatory minimum is an element that must be submitted to the jury and found beyond a reasonable doubt; *Alleyne* did not revisit the narrow exception for the fact of a prior conviction. [*Alleyne v. United States*, 570 U.S. 99, 103, 111 & n.1, 116 (2013)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-570/pdf/USREPORTS-570-99.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-027 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
On a government motion, § 3553(e) gives the court limited authority to impose a sentence below a statutory minimum so as to reflect a defendant's substantial assistance in the investigation or prosecution of another person who has committed an offense; such a sentence must be imposed in accordance with the Sentencing Commission's guidelines and policy statements. [18 U.S.C. § 3553(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3553)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-028 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553,AUTH-SCOTUS-PULSIFER-601-US-124 status:verified_current -->
For an offense under 21 U.S.C. § 841, 844, 846, 960, or 963 or 46 U.S.C. § 70503 or 70506, § 3553(f) directs that the court shall impose a sentence pursuant to the guidelines without regard to any statutory minimum if it finds at sentencing, after the government has been afforded the opportunity to make a recommendation, that all five statutory criteria are met. Under *Pulsifer*, a defendant who has any one of the criminal-history features in § 3553(f)(1)(A), (B), or (C) fails that criterion. Section 3553(f)(1)(A) excludes criminal-history points resulting from 1-point offenses, so those offenses cannot, either alone or in combination, prevent safety-valve relief. [18 U.S.C. § 3553(f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3553); [*Pulsifer v. United States*, 601 U.S. 124, 127–30 (2024)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/601us1r07_19m1.pdf)

## Appellate-error and preservation boundaries

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-029 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MOLINA-MARTINEZ-578-US-189 status:verified_current -->
When an unpreserved Guidelines error produces the wrong range, the error ordinarily shows a reasonable probability of a different outcome and thus affects substantial rights without additional evidence, though the record can show otherwise. [*Molina-Martinez v. United States*, 578 U.S. 189, 198–204 (2016)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-578/pdf/USREPORTS-578-189.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-030 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ROSALES-MIRELES-585-US-129 status:verified_current -->
An unpreserved Guidelines miscalculation satisfying the first three plain-error requirements ordinarily seriously affects the fairness, integrity, or public reputation of judicial proceedings because it risks unnecessary loss of liberty. [*Rosales-Mireles v. United States*, 585 U.S. 129, 137–45 (2018)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-585/pdf/USREPORTS-585-129.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-031 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-HOLGUIN-HERNANDEZ-589-US-169,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
By advocating a particular shorter sentence, a defendant preserves a claim that a longer sentence is substantively unreasonable, but *Holguin-Hernandez* expressly declined to decide what is sufficient to preserve a claim that the trial court used improper procedures. The Court restated that errors not brought to the court's attention in one of the two ways Rule 51(b) provides are subject to review only insofar as they are plain under Rule 52(b), and a two-Justice concurrence added that failing to object at all to a procedural error will subject a procedural challenge to plain-error review. [*Holguin-Hernandez v. United States*, 589 U.S. 169 (2020)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/18-7739_9q7h.pdf); [Fed. R. Crim. P. 51(b), 52(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Application checklist

1. Obtain the indictment, verdict or plea, agreement, PSR and addenda, objections, sentencing submissions, transcript, Statement of Reasons, judgment, and docket.
2. Identify every statute of conviction, offense class, maximum, minimum, consecutive term, supervised-release term, fine, restitution, forfeiture, and assessment.
3. Use one correct Guidelines Manual, document any ex post facto comparison, and reproduce every § 1B1.1 step and disputed fact.
4. File specific written PSR objections within fourteen days and renew or clarify unresolved legal and factual issues at sentencing.
5. If a sentence outside the guideline range is sought, identify whether it rests on a government motion under § 5K1.1 (substantial assistance), which the 2026 Manual retains, or on a § 3553(a) variance, and request the sentence and reasons expressly. The 2025 Manual deleted the other departure provisions (Amendment 836); for a sentencing under an earlier edition, its departure provisions apply.
6. Preserve allocution, disputed-fact rulings, any Rule 32(h) notice issue, statutory-range facts, the selected range, and the court's reasons.

## Open items requiring individualized or circuit research

- Offense elements, statutory penalties, guideline selection, relevant conduct, enhancements, grouping, acceptance, criminal history, any § 5K1.1 motion, safety-valve proof, and plea stipulations.
- Evidentiary reliability and burdens for disputed sentencing facts beyond the nationwide Rule 32 commands.
- The controlling circuit's procedural-preservation rules, review components, departure law for sentencings under pre-2025 editions of the Manual, variance methodology, and explanation requirements.
- Whether a later proceeding is a full resentencing or a limited modification and which § 3553(a) factors its authorizing statute permits.
