# Presentence Investigation, Guidelines, and Sentencing Hearing

## Verification boundary

This module verifies current nationwide Rule 32 procedure, the general statutory sentencing framework, the 2025 Guidelines calculation sequence, the advisory-Guidelines system, constitutional statutory-range rules, and bounded preservation principles. It does not calculate an offense-specific range or penalty, resolve disputed facts, choose a sentence, construe a plea agreement, or supply circuit-specific burdens, departures, preservation applications, or reasonableness outcomes.

## Presentence investigation and disclosure

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-001 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The court must impose sentence without unnecessary delay and may change Rule 32's time limits for good cause. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-002 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52,AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
A probation officer must conduct a presentence investigation and submit a report unless a statute supplies an exception or the court finds that the record contains enough information to exercise sentencing authority meaningfully and explains that finding on the record. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(c)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [18 U.S.C. § 3552(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3552)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-003 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The probation officer may interview the defendant only after giving defense counsel notice and a reasonable opportunity to attend, and counsel must be present if the defendant so requests. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(c)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-004 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52,AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
Unless the defendant waives the minimum period, the probation officer must provide the presentence report to the defendant, defense counsel, and government at least thirty-five days before sentencing; § 3552(d)'s ten-day statutory floor does not shorten the current Rule 32 period. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(e)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [18 U.S.C. § 3552(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3552)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-005 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Within fourteen days after receiving the presentence report, the parties must state in writing any objection to material information, sentencing ranges, or policy statements in or omitted from the report, unless the court changes the time for good cause. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(f), (b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-006 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
At least seven days before sentencing, the probation officer must submit the report and an addendum identifying unresolved objections, grounds for those objections, and the officer's comments, unless the court changes the period for good cause. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(g), (b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Sentencing-hearing procedure and preservation

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-007 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Before imposing sentence, the court must verify that the defendant and defense counsel read and discussed the presentence report and any addendum. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(1)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-008 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The court must allow the parties' attorneys to comment on probation determinations and other sentencing matters and, for each disputed portion of the report or controverted matter, must rule on the dispute or determine that a ruling is unnecessary because it will not affect sentencing or will not be considered; the written determinations must accompany copies of the report made available to the Bureau of Prisons. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(1)(C), (i)(3)(B)–(C)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-009 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Before sentence, the court must address the defendant personally and permit the defendant to speak or present information in mitigation; permitting counsel to speak does not replace the defendant's personal allocution. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(4)(A)(ii)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-010 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The court must also allow defense counsel and the government an opportunity to speak and must address any victim of a qualifying violent or sexual offense who is present and permit the victim to be reasonably heard. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(4)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-011 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
For good cause, the court may allow a new Rule 32 objection at any time before sentence is imposed, but that discretion does not make a specific timely objection unnecessary when the ground is known. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(i)(1)(D), 51(b), 52(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-012 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
If the court contemplates a Guidelines departure on a ground not identified in the presentence report or a party's prehearing submission, it must give reasonable notice specifying that ground. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(h)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-013 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-IRIZARRY-553-US-708 status:verified_current -->
Rule 32(h)'s notice command applies to Guidelines departures, not to a variance selected under § 3553(a); *Irizarry* therefore rejected a categorical Rule 32(h) notice requirement for variances. [*Irizarry v. United States*, 553 U.S. 708, 713–16 (2008)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-553/pdf/USREPORTS-553-708.pdf)

## Statutory sentencing framework and reasons

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-014 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
Section 3553(a) requires a sentence sufficient but not greater than necessary to serve its listed purposes after consideration of the offense and defendant, the permitted purposes, available sentences, current Guidelines and policy statements, unwarranted disparities, and victim restitution. [18 U.S.C. § 3553(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3553)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-015 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
At sentencing the court must state its reasons in open court; § 3553(c) adds point-within-range reasons when the range exceeds twenty-four months and particularized reasons for a different kind of sentence or a sentence outside the range. [18 U.S.C. § 3553(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3553)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-016 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BOOKER-543-US-220 status:verified_current -->
*Booker* rendered the federal Guidelines advisory by severing and excising § 3553(b)(1) and § 3742(e), while requiring the sentencing court to calculate and consider the Guidelines with the other § 3553(a) factors. [*United States v. Booker*, 543 U.S. 220, 245, 259–65 (2005)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-543/pdf/USREPORTS-543-220.pdf)

## Guidelines calculation, departures, and variances

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-017 authorities:AUTH-USSG-2025-SENTENCING-CALCULATION status:verified_current -->
The 2025 Guidelines require a sequenced calculation: select the offense guideline, determine the base level and specific characteristics, apply Chapter Three adjustments, address multiple counts, apply acceptance of responsibility, determine criminal-history category, and then determine the range and sentencing options before considering departures and the remaining § 3553(a) factors. [U.S.S.G. § 1B1.1 (Nov. 1, 2025)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2025-guidelines-manual/annotated-2025-chapter-1)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-018 authorities:AUTH-USSG-2025-SENTENCING-CALCULATION,AUTH-SCOTUS-PEUGH-569-US-530 status:verified_current -->
The court ordinarily uses one Guidelines Manual in effect on the sentencing date, but the Ex Post Facto Clause forbids using a later manual that creates a significant risk of a higher sentence than the manual in effect when the offense was committed. [U.S.S.G. § 1B1.11](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2025-guidelines-manual/annotated-2025-chapter-1); [*Peugh v. United States*, 569 U.S. 530, 533, 544–50 (2013)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-569/pdf/USREPORTS-569-530.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-019 authorities:AUTH-USSG-2025-SENTENCING-CALCULATION,AUTH-SCOTUS-BOOKER-543-US-220 status:verified_current -->
A departure is an adjustment authorized within the Guidelines system; a variance is a sentence selected outside the advisory range through the independent § 3553(a) analysis, and the two should be identified separately in the record. [U.S.S.G. § 1B1.1 commentary](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2025-guidelines-manual/annotated-2025-chapter-1); [*Booker*, 543 U.S. at 259–65](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-543/pdf/USREPORTS-543-220.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-020 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-GALL-552-US-38 status:verified_current -->
The court must begin with a correct Guidelines calculation, permit arguments, consider § 3553(a), avoid clearly erroneous facts, explain the sentence, and justify any variance; appellate review of every sentence is for abuse of discretion, without a proportional mathematical rule or extraordinary-circumstances requirement. [*Gall v. United States*, 552 U.S. 38, 49–51 (2007)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-552/pdf/USREPORTS-552-38.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-021 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-RITA-551-US-338 status:verified_current -->
An appellate court may apply a nonbinding presumption of reasonableness to a properly calculated within-Guidelines sentence, but the sentencing court may not presume that the Guidelines sentence is reasonable. [*Rita v. United States*, 551 U.S. 338, 347–51 (2007)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-551/pdf/USREPORTS-551-338.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-022 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-KIMBROUGH-552-US-85 status:verified_current -->
Because the Guidelines are advisory, a court may conclude that the crack-cocaine guideline produces a sentence greater than necessary even in an ordinary case; *Kimbrough* permits a variance based on that policy disagreement within statutory limits. [*Kimbrough v. United States*, 552 U.S. 85, 101–10 (2007)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-552/pdf/USREPORTS-552-85.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-023 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-PEPPER-562-US-476 status:verified_current -->
At a full resentencing after a sentence has been set aside, the court may consider evidence of the defendant's postsentencing rehabilitation and may use it to support a downward variance. [*Pepper v. United States*, 562 U.S. 476, 481, 490–93 (2011)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-562/pdf/USREPORTS-562-476.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-024 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-TAPIA-564-US-319 status:verified_current -->
A court may discuss treatment and recommend programs, but it may not impose or lengthen imprisonment to promote rehabilitation. [*Tapia v. United States*, 564 U.S. 319, 326–35 (2011)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-564/pdf/USREPORTS-564-319.pdf)

## Statutory-range facts and mandatory-minimum routes

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-025 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-APPRENDI-530-US-466 status:verified_current -->
Other than the prior-conviction exception preserved in *Apprendi*, a fact that increases the statutory maximum must be admitted by the defendant or submitted to a jury and proved beyond a reasonable doubt. [*Apprendi v. New Jersey*, 530 U.S. 466, 476, 490 (2000)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-530/pdf/USREPORTS-530-466.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-026 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ALLEYNE-570-US-99 status:verified_current -->
A fact that increases a statutory mandatory minimum is an element that must be submitted to a jury and found beyond a reasonable doubt, unless admitted through a valid plea. [*Alleyne v. United States*, 570 U.S. 99, 103, 111–17 (2013)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-570/pdf/USREPORTS-570-99.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-027 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
On a government motion, § 3553(e) authorizes a sentence below a statutory minimum to reflect substantial assistance; the motion and assistance-based authority do not convert § 3553(a) alone into power to disregard the floor. [18 U.S.C. § 3553(e)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3553)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-028 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553,AUTH-SCOTUS-PULSIFER-601-US-124 status:verified_current -->
Section 3553(f) permits sentencing without regard to a covered drug-offense minimum when all five statutory criteria are met without a government motion; under *Pulsifer*, any one of § 3553(f)(1)(A), (B), or (C)'s listed criminal-history features disqualifies the defendant. Section 3553(f)(1)(A) excludes criminal-history points resulting from one-point offenses, not only a single one-point offense; satisfying the safety valve removes the statutory floor but does not make the advisory Guidelines mandatory. [18 U.S.C. § 3553(f)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3553); [*Pulsifer v. United States*, 601 U.S. 124, 132–53 (2024)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/601us1r07_19m1.pdf)

## Appellate-error and preservation boundaries

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-029 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MOLINA-MARTINEZ-578-US-189 status:verified_current -->
When an unpreserved Guidelines error produces the wrong range, the error ordinarily shows a reasonable probability of a different outcome and thus affects substantial rights without additional evidence, though the record can show otherwise. [*Molina-Martinez v. United States*, 578 U.S. 189, 198–204 (2016)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-578/pdf/USREPORTS-578-189.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-030 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ROSALES-MIRELES-585-US-129 status:verified_current -->
An unpreserved Guidelines miscalculation satisfying the first three plain-error requirements ordinarily seriously affects the fairness, integrity, or public reputation of judicial proceedings because it risks unnecessary loss of liberty. [*Rosales-Mireles v. United States*, 585 U.S. 129, 137–45 (2018)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-585/pdf/USREPORTS-585-129.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-031 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-HOLGUIN-HERNANDEZ-589-US-169,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
By advocating a particular shorter sentence, a defendant preserves a claim that a longer sentence is substantively unreasonable, but *Holguin-Hernandez* does not dispense with a specific objection needed to preserve a distinct procedural error. [*Holguin-Hernandez v. United States*, 589 U.S. 169, 173–75 (2020)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/18-7739_9q7h.pdf); [Fed. R. Crim. P. 51(b), 52(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Application checklist

1. Obtain the indictment, verdict or plea, agreement, PSR and addenda, objections, sentencing submissions, transcript, Statement of Reasons, judgment, and docket.
2. Identify every statute of conviction, offense class, maximum, minimum, consecutive term, supervised-release term, fine, restitution, forfeiture, and assessment.
3. Use one correct Guidelines Manual, document any ex post facto comparison, and reproduce every § 1B1.1 step and disputed fact.
4. File specific written PSR objections within fourteen days and renew or clarify unresolved legal and factual issues at sentencing.
5. Separate a Guidelines departure from a § 3553(a) variance and request the sentence and reasons expressly.
6. Preserve allocution, disputed-fact rulings, departure notice, statutory-range facts, the selected range, and the court's reasons.

## Open items requiring individualized or circuit research

- Offense elements, statutory penalties, guideline selection, relevant conduct, enhancements, grouping, acceptance, criminal history, departures, safety-valve proof, and plea stipulations.
- Evidentiary reliability and burdens for disputed sentencing facts beyond the nationwide Rule 32 commands.
- The controlling circuit's procedural-preservation rules, review components, departure law, variance methodology, and explanation requirements.
- Whether a later proceeding is a full resentencing or a limited modification and which § 3553(a) factors its authorizing statute permits.
