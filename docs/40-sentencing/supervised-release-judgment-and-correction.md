# Supervised Release, Judgment, and Sentence Correction

## Verification boundary

This module verifies initial imposition and conditions of supervised release, judgment entry, immediate Rule 35(a) correction, Rule 36 clerical correction, appeal advice, and bounded issue preservation. It does not cover modification, early termination, revocation, delayed revocation, tolling, reentry practice, or an individual sentence-modification route, which require their own modules and records.

## Initial supervised-release term

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-052 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
When imposing imprisonment for a felony or misdemeanor, the court may include supervised release after imprisonment and must include it when another statute requires it or for a first domestic-violence conviction as § 3561(b) defines; § 3583(a) does not impose a general mandate based solely on an imprisonment term exceeding one year. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-053 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Unless another statute provides otherwise, the maximum supervised-release term is five years for a Class A or B felony, three years for a Class C or D felony, and one year for a Class E felony or a nonpetty misdemeanor. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-054 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
In selecting the initial supervised-release term and conditions, § 3583(c) incorporates § 3553(a)(1), (a)(2)(B)–(D), and (a)(4)–(7), but not § 3553(a)(2)(A)'s retributive purposes. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-055 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Section 3583(d) imposes its listed mandatory conditions, including no new crime, no unlawful controlled-substance possession, authorized DNA collection, and required sex-offender registration, while allowing qualifying discretionary conditions drawn from § 3563(b) and other appropriate conditions. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-056 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
A discretionary supervised-release condition must be reasonably related to the incorporated sentencing factors, involve no greater deprivation of liberty than reasonably necessary for deterrence, public protection, and effective treatment, and be consistent with Sentencing Commission policy statements. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-057 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-TAPIA-564-US-319 status:verified_current -->
Although treatment needs may inform supervised-release conditions, the court may not impose or lengthen imprisonment to promote rehabilitation. [*Tapia v. United States*, 564 U.S. 319, 326–35 (2011)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-564/pdf/USREPORTS-564-319.pdf)

## Judgment and appeal advice

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-058 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The judgment of conviction must set out the plea, jury verdict or court findings, adjudication, and sentence; the judge must sign it and the clerk must enter it. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(k)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-059 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
After sentencing, Rule 32(j) requires the court to advise a defendant who went to trial of the right to appeal and advise any defendant of a right to appeal the sentence, subject to the rule's exceptions, and to advise about applying for leave to proceed in forma pauperis. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32(j)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Immediate correction and clerical error

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-060 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36 status:verified_current -->
Within fourteen days after the oral announcement of sentence, Rule 35(a) permits correction only of an arithmetical, technical, or other clear error; the narrow period is not a general opportunity to reconsider the sentence. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 35(a), (c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-061 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36 status:verified_current -->
Rule 35(b) permits a post-sentencing reduction only on a government substantial-assistance motion and under its timing and information conditions; it is distinct from § 3553(e) authority at the original sentencing. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 35(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-062 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-2-35-36 status:verified_current -->
After any appropriate notice, Rule 36 permits correction at any time of a clerical error in a judgment, order, or record or an error arising from oversight or omission, but it does not authorize a substantive resentencing. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 36](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-063 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
A term of imprisonment is a final judgment and may be modified only through an express route listed in § 3582(c) or another applicable statute or rule; § 3553(a) alone does not create sentence-modification jurisdiction. [18 U.S.C. § 3582(b)–(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3582)

## Preservation checklist

<!-- claim:CLM-SENT-064 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52,AUTH-SCOTUS-HOLGUIN-HERNANDEZ-589-US-169 status:verified_current -->
A party preserves a sentencing issue by informing the court of the requested action or objection and grounds when the ruling is made or sought; requesting a shorter sentence preserves substantive unreasonableness, but a distinct procedural error ordinarily requires a specific objection. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 51(b), 52(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [*Holguin-Hernandez v. United States*, 589 U.S. 169, 173–75 (2020)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/18-7739_9q7h.pdf)

## Application checklist

1. Identify the statute of conviction, offense class, any offense-specific supervised-release term, and every mandatory or proposed discretionary condition.
2. Compare oral pronouncement, written judgment, Statement of Reasons, forfeiture and restitution orders, and docket entry.
3. Calendar Rule 35(a) from the oral announcement of sentence, not entry of judgment.
4. Classify a claimed mistake as clear sentencing error, government substantial assistance, clerical omission, appeal issue, or a separate statutory modification route before filing.
5. Make specific contemporaneous objections to disputed calculations, facts, procedures, conditions, and reasons, while expressly requesting the desired sentence.

## Open items requiring individualized or circuit research

- Offense-specific supervised-release terms, mandatory minima, oral-pronouncement doctrine, vagueness, delegation, occupational and technology restrictions, and appellate review of particular conditions.
- Modification, early termination, tolling, revocation, delayed jurisdiction, Chapter Seven, and reentry practice.
- Whether an actual discrepancy is clerical or substantive and which court has authority while an appeal is pending.
