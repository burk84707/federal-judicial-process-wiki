# Supervised Release Commencement, Conditions, Modification, Termination, and Transfer

Supervised release is a distinct post-imprisonment sentence component. Before choosing a procedure, identify the original offense and judgment, statutory maximum and any offense-specific term, actual release and custody dates, every condition, the district holding jurisdiction, and whether the requested relief is correction, modification, early termination, transfer, or a later revocation question.

## Imposition and authorized term

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-001 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
A court may include supervised release after imprisonment and must do so when a statute requires it; the domestic-violence provision in § 3583(a) supplies an additional mandatory circumstance. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-002 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Unless another statute provides otherwise, § 3583(b) sets default maximum terms of five years for a Class A or B felony, three years for a Class C or D felony, one year for a Class E felony or misdemeanor other than a petty offense, and no term for a petty offense. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-003 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
At initial imposition, § 3583(c) directs consideration of only the listed § 3553(a) factors; it does not incorporate every subsection of § 3553(a). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-004 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
The applicable offense statute can require a minimum, authorize a different maximum, or otherwise displace § 3583(b)'s defaults, so the statute of conviction and offense date must be checked before calculating the permissible term. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

## Commencement and tolling

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-005 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
A supervised-release term begins on the day the person is released from imprisonment, and it runs concurrently with federal, state, or local probation, supervised release, or parole for another offense. [18 U.S.C. § 3624(e)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A3624+edition%3Aprelim%29)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-006 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
Section 3624(e) excludes from the supervised-release term a period of imprisonment exceeding thirty consecutive days in connection with a federal, state, or local conviction. [18 U.S.C. § 3624(e)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A3624+edition%3Aprelim%29)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-007 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MONT-587-US-514,AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
Pretrial detention later credited to a new conviction qualifies as imprisonment in connection with that conviction and tolls supervised release under § 3624(e). [*Mont v. United States*, 587 U.S. 514, 518–24 (2019)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-587/pdf/USREPORTS-587-514.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-008 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-JOHNSON-529-US-53,AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
Excess time served in prison does not shorten the supervised-release term because § 3624(e) starts that term on actual release; equitable relief, if warranted, lies through § 3583(e)'s modification or termination tools. [*United States v. Johnson*, 529 U.S. 53, 57–60 (2000)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-53.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-009 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-RICO-607-US-SLIP,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
The Sentencing Reform Act does not automatically stop or extend a supervised-release term merely because a person absconds; *Rico* requires use of the enacted tolling, modification, and delayed-revocation provisions rather than a judge-made fugitive-tolling rule. [*Rico v. United States*, 607 U.S. ___, slip op. 1–16 (2026)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1056_qn12.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-010 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-RICO-607-US-SLIP,AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
The actual release, detention, conviction-credit, warrant, summons, transfer, and expiration records control the term calculation; labels such as “absconded,” “inactive,” or “tolled” cannot replace that chronology. [*Rico*, 607 U.S. ___, slip op. 8–16](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1056_qn12.pdf)

## Conditions

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-011 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Section 3583(d) imposes listed mandatory conditions and authorizes discretionary conditions drawn from § 3563(b) plus other appropriate conditions. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-012 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
A discretionary condition must be reasonably related to the specified history, deterrence, protection, and treatment factors, involve no greater deprivation of liberty than reasonably necessary for the specified purposes, and be consistent with Sentencing Commission policy statements. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)(1)–(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-013 authorities:AUTH-USSC-2025-5D1 status:verified_current -->
Current Guidelines § 5D1.3 distinguishes mandatory, standard, and special conditions and directs individualized selection within the statutory limits; a guideline condition does not override § 3583(d). [U.S.S.G. § 5D1.3 (2025)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2025-guidelines-manual/annotated-2025-chapter-5)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-014 authorities:AUTH-USSC-2025-5D1 status:verified_current -->
An occupational restriction under § 5F1.5 requires a reasonably direct relationship between the occupation and offense conduct, necessity to protect the public from similar unlawful conduct, and the minimum time and extent needed. [U.S.S.G. § 5F1.5 (2025)](https://guidelines.ussc.gov/apex/r/ussc_apex/guidelinesapp/guidelines?APP_GL_ID=%C2%A75F1.5)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-015 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
For a person required to register under SORNA, § 3583(d) authorizes the stated search condition only on reasonable suspicion and at a reasonable time and manner, with a probation-officer search also permitted when necessary for the probation officer's duties. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-016 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
The court imposes the condition of supervised release; probation may administer implementation details only within the lawful condition and the controlling circuit's nondelegation boundaries. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-017 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Whether an internet, treatment, search, association, travel, residence, employment, financial, or third-party-notification condition is valid depends on its precise text, the sentencing record, statutory fit, constitutional burden, and controlling circuit law. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

## Modification, extension, and early termination

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-018 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1 status:verified_current -->
Before the term expires or is terminated, the court may modify, reduce, or enlarge conditions under § 3583(e)(2), subject to Rule 32.1(c). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(2)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583); [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-019 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1,AUTH-USC-18-3006A status:verified_current -->
Rule 32.1(c) ordinarily requires a modification hearing, counsel, and an opportunity to make a statement and present mitigation. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(c)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf); [18 U.S.C. § 3006A(a)(1)(E)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A3006A+edition%3Aprelim%29)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-020 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1 status:verified_current -->
The court may dispense with a Rule 32.1(c) hearing if the person waives it, or if the proposed change is favorable and does not extend the term and the government has notice, an opportunity to object, and does not object. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(c)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-021 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
If the original term was shorter than the maximum authorized term, § 3583(e)(2) permits extension before expiration or termination; it does not authorize revival of an already expired term outside § 3583(i). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(2), (i)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-022 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
After one year of felony supervised release, the court may terminate the remaining term if the person's conduct and the interest of justice warrant it after considering the factors listed in § 3583(e). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-023 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Early termination is discretionary rather than automatic; the statute does not make government or probation consent a prerequisite. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-024 authorities:AUTH-USSC-2025-5D1,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Effective November 1, 2025, Guidelines § 5D1.4 recommends an individualized assessment for modification, early termination after one year, and extension; its consultation recommendation does not add a consent requirement to § 3583(e). [U.S.S.G. § 5D1.4 (2025)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2025-guidelines-manual/annotated-2025-chapter-5)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-025 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-JOHNSON-529-US-53,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Section 3583(e) permits a court to consider excess imprisonment and other verified post-sentencing circumstances when deciding modification or termination, but *Johnson* does not require a particular equitable result. [*Johnson*, 529 U.S. at 60](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-53.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-026 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
For modification, termination, extension, and revocation decisions under § 3583(e), the court considers only the factors that subsection expressly lists, including § 3553(a)(1), (a)(2)(B)–(D), (a)(4)–(7), but not § 3553(a)(2)(A). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

## Transfer and record controls

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-027 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3605 status:verified_current -->
The sentencing court may transfer jurisdiction over supervised release to another district only with that district court's concurrence; after transfer, the receiving court may exercise the statutory powers specified in § 3605. [18 U.S.C. § 3605](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3605)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-028 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3605 status:verified_current -->
Supervision in another district is not by itself a transfer of judicial jurisdiction; the transfer order, concurrence, docket, and probation arrangements must be distinguished. [18 U.S.C. § 3605](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3605)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-029 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-USC-18-3605 status:verified_current -->
A filing should identify the court holding jurisdiction, requested statutory action, current term calculation, exact condition or relief, supporting record, and any pending violation or transfer proceeding. [18 U.S.C. §§ 3583(e), 3605](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=1&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-030 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1 status:verified_current -->
Rule 32.1(d) contains only a cross-reference to the governing disposition statutes; it does not itself require written or oral findings, so any findings or reasons requirement must come from another verified authority. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(d)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

## Practical audit

1. Identify the statute of conviction, offense class, offense date, and offense-specific supervised-release provision.
2. Obtain the judgment, amended judgments, conditions, release date, custody chronology, and current probation record.
3. Calculate commencement and only enacted tolling; do not insert automatic absconding tolling.
4. Confirm which district holds jurisdiction and whether a § 3605 transfer was completed.
5. Select correction, modification, early termination, extension, or a revocation route and apply its distinct hearing and timing rules.
