# Supervised Release Commencement, Conditions, Modification, Termination, and Transfer

Supervised release is a distinct post-imprisonment sentence component. Before choosing a procedure, identify the original offense and judgment, statutory maximum and any offense-specific term, actual release and custody dates, every condition, the district holding jurisdiction, and whether the requested relief is correction, modification, early termination, transfer, or a later revocation question.

## Imposition and authorized term

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-001 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
When imposing imprisonment for a felony or a misdemeanor, a court may include a term of supervised release after imprisonment, and it must do so if such a term is required by statute or if the defendant has been convicted for the first time of a domestic violence crime as defined in § 3561(b). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-002 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Except as otherwise provided, § 3583(b) authorizes supervised-release terms of not more than five years for a Class A or B felony, three years for a Class C or D felony, and one year for a Class E felony or a misdemeanor (other than a petty offense). Subsections (j) and (k) displace those limits for the offenses they list, authorizing any term of years or life under (j) and any term of years not less than 5, or life, under (k). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(b), (j), (k)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-003 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-USC-18-3551-3552-3553 status:verified_current -->
At initial imposition, § 3583(c) directs consideration of only the listed § 3553(a) factors; it does not incorporate every subsection of § 3553(a). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-004 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
The applicable offense statute can require a minimum, authorize a different maximum, or otherwise displace § 3583(b)'s defaults, so the statute of conviction and offense date must be checked before calculating the permissible term. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

## Commencement and tolling

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-005 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
A supervised-release term begins on the day the person is released from imprisonment, and it runs concurrently with federal, state, or local probation, supervised release, or parole for another offense. [18 U.S.C. § 3624(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3624)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-006 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
Under § 3624(e), a supervised-release term does not run during any period in which the person is imprisoned in connection with a conviction for a federal, state, or local crime, unless the imprisonment is for a period of less than 30 consecutive days. [18 U.S.C. § 3624(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3624)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-007 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MONT-587-US-514,AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
Pretrial detention later credited to a new conviction qualifies as imprisonment in connection with that conviction and tolls supervised release under § 3624(e). [*Mont v. United States*, 587 U.S. 514, 518–24 (2019)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-587/pdf/USREPORTS-587-514.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-008 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-JOHNSON-529-US-53,AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
Excess time served in prison does not shorten the supervised-release term because § 3624(e) starts that term on actual release; equitable relief, if warranted, lies through § 3583(e)'s modification or termination tools. [*United States v. Johnson*, 529 U.S. 53, 57–60 (2000)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-53.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-009 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-RICO-607-US-SLIP status:verified_current -->
*Rico* holds that the Sentencing Reform Act does not authorize a rule automatically extending a supervised-release term when the defendant absconds; the Court pointed instead to the Act's enacted tools, including revocation under § 3583(e)(3) and (g), extension under § 3583(e)(2), delayed revocation under § 3583(i), and the § 3624(e) tolling rule for imprisonment. [*Rico v. United States*, 607 U.S. ___, slip op. 4–12 (2026)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1056_qn12.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-010 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3624,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-SCOTUS-RICO-607-US-609 status:verified_current -->
The term starts on "the day the person is released from imprisonment" and does not run during any period in which the person is imprisoned in connection with a conviction for a Federal, State, or local crime unless the imprisonment is for less than 30 consecutive days. After expiration, § 3583(i) extends the court's power to revoke "for any period reasonably necessary for the adjudication of matters arising before its expiration if, before its expiration, a warrant or summons has been issued on the basis of an allegation of such a violation." *Rico* called the lower court's "tolling" label for an absconder's term "a misnomer" and held that abscondment does not automatically extend the court-ordered term. [18 U.S.C. § 3624(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3624); [18 U.S.C. § 3583(i)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583); [*Rico v. United States*, 607 U.S. 609, 614–617, 622–623 (2026)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/607us2r23_4g15.pdf)

## Conditions

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-011 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Section 3583(d) requires listed explicit conditions and permits the court to order any discretionary condition of probation in § 3563(b) and any other condition it considers appropriate, but only to the extent the condition is reasonably related to the factors in § 3553(a)(1), (a)(2)(B)–(D), involves no greater deprivation of liberty than is reasonably necessary for the purposes in § 3553(a)(2)(B)–(D), and is consistent with pertinent Sentencing Commission policy statements; a § 3563(b)(10) condition may be imposed only for a violation of a condition of supervised release in accordance with § 3583(e)(2) and only when facilities are available. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-012 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
A discretionary condition must be reasonably related to the specified history, deterrence, protection, and treatment factors, involve no greater deprivation of liberty than reasonably necessary for the specified purposes, and be consistent with Sentencing Commission policy statements. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)(1)–(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-013 authorities:AUTH-USSC-2026-5D1 status:verified_current -->
Current Guidelines § 5D1.3 distinguishes mandatory, standard, and special conditions and directs individualized selection within the statutory limits; a guideline condition does not override § 3583(d). [U.S.S.G. § 5D1.3 (2026)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2026-guidelines-manual/annotated-2026-chapter-5)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-014 authorities:AUTH-USSC-2026-5D1 status:verified_current -->
An occupational restriction under § 5F1.5 requires a reasonably direct relationship between the occupation and offense conduct, a determination that the restriction is reasonably necessary to protect the public because there is reason to believe the defendant otherwise will continue similar unlawful conduct, and the minimum time and extent necessary to protect the public. [U.S.S.G. § 5F1.5 (2026)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2026-guidelines-manual/annotated-2026-chapter-5)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-015 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
For a person who is a felon and required to register under SORNA, § 3583(d) permits the court to order a condition that the person submit to search at any time, with or without a warrant, by any law enforcement or probation officer with reasonable suspicion concerning a supervised-release violation or unlawful conduct, and by any probation officer in the lawful discharge of the officer's supervision functions. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-016 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-USC-18-3624 status:verified_current -->
The court orders the conditions of supervised release, and the probation officer supervises the person "to the degree warranted by the conditions specified by the sentencing court." [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583); [18 U.S.C. § 3624(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3624)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-017 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Under § 3583(d), the court may order a discretionary condition, such as an internet, association, travel, residence, employment, financial, or third-party-notification condition, only to the extent it is reasonably related to the factors in § 3553(a)(1), (a)(2)(B)–(D), involves no greater deprivation of liberty than is reasonably necessary for the purposes in § 3553(a)(2)(B)–(D), and is consistent with pertinent Sentencing Commission policy statements. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)(1)–(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

## Modification, extension, and early termination

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-018 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1 status:verified_current -->
Before the term expires or is terminated, and after considering the § 3553(a) factors listed in § 3583(e), the court may modify, reduce, or enlarge conditions under § 3583(e)(2), following the Criminal Rules on modifying probation (Rule 32.1(c)) and the provisions that govern the initial setting of conditions. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583); [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-019 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1,AUTH-USC-18-3006A status:verified_current -->
Before modifying the conditions of probation or supervised release, Rule 32.1(c)(1) requires the court to hold a hearing, at which the person has the right to counsel and an opportunity to make a statement and present any information in mitigation. Under Rule 32.1(c)(2), a hearing is not required if: (A) the person waives the hearing; or (B) the relief sought is favorable to the person and does not extend the term of probation or of supervised release; and (C) an attorney for the government has received notice of the relief sought, has had a reasonable opportunity to object, and has not done so. Section 3006A(a)(1)(E) requires each district's plan to provide representation for any financially eligible person who faces modification, reduction, or enlargement of a condition of supervised release. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(c)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf); [18 U.S.C. § 3006A(a)(1)(E)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3006A)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-020 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1 status:verified_current -->
The court may dispense with a Rule 32.1(c) hearing if the person waives it, or if the proposed change is favorable and does not extend the term and the government has notice, an opportunity to object, and does not object. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(c)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-021 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-SCOTUS-RICO-607-US-609 status:verified_current -->
If less than the maximum authorized term was previously imposed, § 3583(e)(2) permits the court to extend the term at any time before it expires or is terminated; the court must generally hold a hearing and consider the listed sentencing factors, and it cannot extend beyond the statutory maximum or after the term has expired. After expiration, § 3583(i) preserves only the power to revoke, which "extends beyond the expiration of the term of supervised release for any period reasonably necessary for the adjudication of matters arising before its expiration if, before its expiration, a warrant or summons has been issued on the basis of an allegation of such a violation." [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(2), (i)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583); [*Rico v. United States*, 607 U.S. 609, 616 (2026)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/607us2r23_4g15.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-022 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
At any time after one year of supervised release, the court may terminate the term and discharge the person, following the Criminal Rules on modifying probation, if it is satisfied that the person's conduct and the interest of justice warrant it, after considering the factors listed in § 3583(e). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-023 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Early termination is discretionary rather than automatic; the statute does not make government or probation consent a prerequisite. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-024 authorities:AUTH-USSC-2026-5D1,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Effective November 1, 2025, Guidelines § 5D1.4 recommends an individualized assessment for modification, early termination after one year, and extension; its consultation recommendation does not add a consent requirement to § 3583(e). [U.S.S.G. § 5D1.4 (2026)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2026-guidelines-manual/annotated-2026-chapter-5)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-025 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-JOHNSON-529-US-53,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Under *Johnson*, excess imprisonment raises "equitable considerations of great weight"; the court, "as it sees fit," may modify conditions under § 3583(e)(2), and after one year of supervised release it may terminate the term under § 3583(e)(1) if it is satisfied that the person's conduct and the interest of justice warrant it. Under § 3583(e), the court exercises either power only after considering the listed § 3553(a) factors. [*United States v. Johnson*, 529 U.S. 53, 60 (2000)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-53.pdf); [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)–(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-026 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-SCOTUS-ESTERAS-606-US-SLIP status:verified_current -->
Before terminating, extending or modifying, or revoking under § 3583(e), the court considers the factors in § 3553(a)(1), (a)(2)(B)–(D), and (a)(4)–(7). For revocation, *Esteras* held that this itemized list is exhaustive and that district courts cannot consider § 3553(a)(2)(A). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583); [*Esteras v. United States*, 606 U.S. ___, slip op. 8, 11 (2025)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/23-7483_6k4c.pdf)

## Transfer and record controls

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-027 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3605 status:verified_current -->
After imposing sentence, the court may transfer jurisdiction over a person on supervised release to the district court for any other district to which the person is required to proceed as a condition of release, or is permitted to proceed, with that court's concurrence, and a later transfer may be made in the same manner; the receiving court may exercise the statutory powers specified in § 3605. [18 U.S.C. § 3605](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3605)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-028 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3605 status:verified_current -->
Supervision in another district is not by itself a transfer of judicial jurisdiction; the transfer order, concurrence, docket, and probation arrangements must be distinguished. [18 U.S.C. § 3605](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3605)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRTERM-030 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1 status:verified_current -->
Rule 32.1(d) contains only a cross-reference to the governing disposition statutes; it does not itself require written or oral findings, so any findings or reasons requirement must come from another verified authority. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(d)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

## Practical audit

1. Identify the statute of conviction, offense class, offense date, and offense-specific supervised-release provision.
2. Obtain the judgment, amended judgments, conditions, release date, custody chronology, and current probation record.
3. Calculate commencement and only enacted tolling; do not insert automatic absconding tolling.
4. Confirm which district holds jurisdiction and whether a § 3605 transfer was completed.
5. Select correction, modification, early termination, extension, or a revocation route and apply its distinct hearing and timing rules.
