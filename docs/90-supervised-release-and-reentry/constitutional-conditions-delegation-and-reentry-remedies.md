# Constitutional Conditions, Delegation, and Reentry Remedies

Reentry problems do not share one federal remedy. Begin with the exact condition, actor, jurisdiction, order, present injury, and requested relief. Judicial supervised-release tools can modify or terminate federal supervision, but they do not automatically remove state licensing, voting, registration, immigration, housing, employment, or private-background consequences.

## Condition challenge framework

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-001 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Every discretionary condition must satisfy § 3583(d)'s relationship, liberty, and policy-statement limits; the exact wording and record matter. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-002 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-USSC-2026-5D1 status:verified_current -->
A condition listed in the Guidelines remains subject to the statute and constitutional constraints; “standard” does not mean immune from individualized review. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583); [U.S.S.G. § 5D1.3 (2026)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2026-guidelines-manual/annotated-2026-chapter-5)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-003 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-PACKINGHAM-582-US-98 status:verified_current -->
*Packingham* invalidated a broad state post-sentence social-media ban under the First Amendment; it is constitutional background, not a holding deciding the validity of a particular federal supervised-release condition. [*Packingham v. North Carolina*, 582 U.S. 98, 104–10 (2017)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/16pdf/15-1194_08l1.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-004 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-PACKINGHAM-582-US-98,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
An internet or social-media condition requires review of its scope, duration, approval mechanism, offense relationship, less restrictive alternatives, record support, and controlling circuit law; *Packingham* does not dictate one nationwide supervised-release result. [*Packingham*, 582 U.S. at 104–10](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/16pdf/15-1194_08l1.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-005 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Beyond the conditions § 3583(d) makes mandatory, the court may order as a further condition any discretionary probation condition in § 3563(b) or any other condition it considers appropriate, such as treatment, search, monitoring, residence, travel, association, contact, employment, financial, and disclosure conditions, but only "to the extent that such condition" is reasonably related to the listed § 3553(a) factors, involves no greater deprivation of liberty than is reasonably necessary, and is consistent with pertinent Sentencing Commission policy statements. Section 3583(d) separately states that the court "shall order" certain conditions, including no new crime, no unlawful possession of a controlled substance, and refraining from unlawful use of a controlled substance with drug testing; that drug-use-and-testing condition may be ameliorated or suspended as provided in § 3563(a)(4). Section 3583(d) also separately authorizes, in their own sentences, a condition that an alien defendant subject to deportation be deported and remain outside the United States, and a search condition for a person who is a felon and required to register under the Sex Offender Registration and Notification Act. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-006 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Discretionary conditions affecting family, speech, religion, bodily integrity, occupation, or other substantial liberty interests, like other discretionary conditions, must involve "no greater deprivation of liberty than is reasonably necessary" for the purposes in § 3553(a)(2)(B), (C), and (D). [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

## Delegation and probation administration

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-007 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
The court must impose the sentence and its conditions; probation administers supervision within the judicially imposed condition and statutory authority. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-009 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1 status:verified_current -->
If probation's implementation effectively enlarges or changes a judicial condition, the matter can be presented to the court, which, after considering the listed § 3553(a) factors, may modify, reduce, or enlarge the conditions "at any time prior to the expiration or termination of the term of supervised release." [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583) Before modifying conditions, the court must hold a hearing unless an exception in Rule 32.1(c)(2) applies. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

## Timing and procedural vehicle

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-011 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1 status:verified_current -->
During an unexpired term, the court may, after considering the listed § 3553(a) factors, modify, reduce, or enlarge a condition "at any time prior to the expiration or termination of the term," under the Rules on modifying probation and the provisions that govern initially setting conditions. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583) Rule 32.1(c) requires a hearing before modification, at which the person has the right to counsel and an opportunity to make a statement and present mitigating information, unless the person waives it, or the relief is favorable, does not extend the term, and the government, after notice and a reasonable opportunity to object, has not objected. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-012 authorities:AUTH-CA11-CORDERO-7-F4TH-1058,AUTH-CA5-CAILLIER-79-F4TH-506 status:verified_current -->
The Eleventh Circuit, joining the Second, Fifth, and Ninth Circuits, holds that § 3583(e)(2) cannot be used to challenge the legality or constitutionality of supervised-release conditions; those arguments are "reserved properly for direct appeal or a 28 U.S.C. § 2255 motion." [*United States v. Cordero*, 7 F.4th 1058 (11th Cir. 2021)](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/201810837.pdf) The Fifth Circuit holds that a court cannot modify a condition under § 3583(e)(2) if its illegality is the basis for modification, whichever party moves, but it upheld a modification that was not sought or granted solely on that basis and rested on public safety and deterrence. [*United States v. Caillier*, 79 F.4th 506 (5th Cir. 2023)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/22/22-30383-CR0.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-013 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
After expiration, § 3583(e)(2) no longer supplies ordinary condition-modification jurisdiction, and § 3583(i) preserves only bounded delayed-revocation power meeting its express prerequisites. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(2), (i)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-014 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3605 status:verified_current -->
When supervision or residence spans districts, verify whether jurisdiction was formally transferred under § 3605 before filing a modification or termination motion. [18 U.S.C. § 3605](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3605)

## Early termination and reentry

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-016 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
At any time after the expiration of one year of supervised release, the court, after considering the listed § 3553(a) factors and under the Rules on modifying probation, may terminate the term and discharge the defendant if it is satisfied that such action is warranted by the defendant's conduct and the interest of justice. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-017 authorities:AUTH-USSC-2026-5D1,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Current § 5D1.4 identifies an individualized ongoing-supervision assessment and relevant conduct, risk, compliance, adjustment, and reentry considerations; it remains a policy statement operating within § 3583(e). [U.S.S.G. § 5D1.4 (2026)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2026-guidelines-manual/annotated-2026-chapter-5)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-018 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Compliance with conditions is relevant but does not create automatic entitlement to early termination; exceptional circumstances are not stated as a statutory prerequisite. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-019 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
A motion may document stable housing, lawful employment, treatment progress, restitution or support compliance, risk reduction, family responsibilities, travel or licensing burdens, and supervision history, but the court applies the statutory factors to verified evidence. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-022 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
There is no general federal judicial expungement remedy stated in § 3583(e); any sealing, expungement, certificate, pardon, statutory relief, or record-correction route requires its own jurisdictional authority. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-023 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
If a reentry barrier is caused by a supervised-release condition, § 3583(e) may be directly relevant: after considering the listed § 3553(a) factors, the court may modify, reduce, or enlarge the conditions at any time before the term expires or is terminated, or, after one year of supervised release, terminate the term and discharge the defendant if it is satisfied that the defendant's conduct and the interest of justice warrant it. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)–(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

## Record and remedy map

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-025 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-2025-PAMPHLET,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
Under Rule 32.1(c)(1), before modifying the conditions of probation or supervised release, the court must hold a hearing, at which the person has the right to counsel and an opportunity to make a statement and present any information in mitigation. Under Rule 32.1(c)(2), a hearing is not required if "(A) the person waives the hearing; or (B) the relief sought is favorable to the person and does not extend the term of probation or of supervised release; and (C) an attorney for the government has received notice of the relief sought, has had a reasonable opportunity to object, and has not done so." Section 3583(e)(2) provides that the court may, after considering the factors set forth in § 3553(a)(1), (a)(2)(B), (a)(2)(C), (a)(2)(D), (a)(4), (a)(5), (a)(6), and (a)(7), "modify, reduce, or enlarge the conditions of supervised release, at any time prior to the expiration or termination of the term of supervised release, pursuant to the provisions of the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure relating to the modification of probation and the provisions applicable to the initial setting of the terms and conditions of post-release supervision". [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(c) (Dec. 1, 2025 ed.)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-026 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-USSC-2026-5D1 status:verified_current -->
After the expiration of one year of supervised release, § 3583(e)(1) permits the court, after considering the § 3553(a) factors listed in § 3583(e), to terminate the term of supervised release and discharge the defendant if it is satisfied that termination is warranted by the defendant's conduct and the interest of justice. Section 5D1.4(b), a policy statement, provides for early termination after an individualized assessment of the need for ongoing supervision and following consultation with the government and the probation officer; its commentary lists factors the court may wish to consider and encourages the court, in coordination with the government, to ensure that any victim is reasonably, accurately, and timely notified and, to the extent practicable, given an opportunity to be reasonably heard, unless the victim previously requested not to be notified. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583); [U.S.S.G. § 5D1.4(b) & cmt. nn.1(B), 2 (2026)](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/2026-guidelines-manual/annotated-2026-chapter-5)

<!-- claim:CLM-SRCOND-027 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1,AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583 status:verified_current -->
If a condition is enforced through a violation proceeding, the person is entitled to written notice of the alleged violation and disclosure of the evidence, and the court's disposition is governed by § 3583. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.1(b)(2), (d)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure-dec-1-2024.pdf) Revocation requires a finding by a preponderance of the evidence that the defendant violated a condition. [18 U.S.C. § 3583(e)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3583)

## Practical sequence

1. Inventory every active condition and every separate collateral consequence.
2. Identify the source and decisionmaker for each burden.
3. Use direct appeal, modification, termination, transfer, revocation defense, agency review, state relief, or executive clemency only where its jurisdiction exists.
4. Build the actual factual record and request narrow, implementable relief.
5. Verify controlling circuit, local-rule, state, and consequence-specific law before filing.
