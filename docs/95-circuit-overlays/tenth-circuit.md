# Tenth Circuit Overlay

This page prevents nationwide rules from being mistaken for Tenth Circuit doctrine and prevents unresearched circuit law from being guessed. It is a bounded overlay, not an exhaustive treatise or a substitute for the current docket, published opinions, local rules, or citator.

- [Official Tenth Circuit rules and procedures](https://www.ca10.uscourts.gov/clerk/rules)
- Official 2026 rules repository, with local rules effective January 1, 2026, checked on 2026-08-25.

## Verified circuit holdings

<!-- claim:CLM-CIR10-001 authorities:AUTH-CA10-GARZA-596-F3D-1198 status:verified_current -->
- **CLM-CIR10-001:** In the Tenth Circuit, exhaustion of available BOP remedies is a prerequisite to § 2241 relief despite the absence of an express statutory clause; a narrow futility exception exists, but alleged local categorical denial is insufficient where regional and national review remain available. [*Garza v. Davis*, 596 F.3d 1198, 1203–04 (10th Cir. 2010)](https://www.ca10.uscourts.gov/sites/ca10/files/opinions/01018368252.pdf)

  Limitation: Binding only in the Tenth Circuit and limited to the BOP placement posture. A prisoner cannot establish futility merely by styling the claim as a facial regulatory challenge, but materially different facts require separate analysis.
<!-- claim:CLM-CIR10-002 authorities:AUTH-PL-115-391-SEC-404,AUTH-SCOTUS-CONCEPCION-597-US-481,AUTH-CA10-MANNIE-971-F3D-1145 status:verified_current -->
- **CLM-CIR10-002:** In the Tenth Circuit, a § 404 proceeding is a sentence modification rather than a plenary resentencing; no hearing or defendant presence is categorically required, and the discretionary disposition is reviewed for abuse of discretion with enough reasoning to permit review. [*United States v. Mannie*, 971 F.3d 1145, 1155–58 (10th Cir. 2020)](https://www.ca10.uscourts.gov/sites/ca10/files/opinions/010110393562.pdf)

  Limitation: Binding only in the Tenth Circuit. Mannie's surviving nonplenary, hearing, presence, and review holdings are used; any pre-Concepcion restriction on intervening information is not adopted.
<!-- claim:CLM-CIR10-003 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2253,AUTH-CA10-MCINTOSH-115-F3D-809 status:verified_current -->
- **CLM-CIR10-003:** In the Tenth Circuit, a federal prisoner appealing a final order in a properly classified § 2241 proceeding does not need a certificate of appealability. [*McIntosh v. U.S. Parole Commission*, 115 F.3d 809, 810 n.1 (10th Cir. 1997)](https://openjurist.org/115/f3d/809/mcintosh-v-united-states-parole-commission)

  Limitation: Binding only in the Tenth Circuit. McIntosh involved federal parole revocation; the proposition does not address state-process detention, § 2255, or whether a filing is properly classified under § 2241.
<!-- claim:CLM-CIR10-004 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-CA10-HEMMELGARN-15-F4TH-1027 status:verified_current -->
- **CLM-CIR10-004:** In the Tenth Circuit, § 3582(c)(1)(A)'s defendant-filing prerequisite is a nonjurisdictional claim-processing rule that the government can waive or forfeit; when the government abandoned its exhaustion argument on appeal, the court proceeded to the merits. [*United States v. Hemmelgarn*, 15 F.4th 1027, 1030–31 (10th Cir. 2021)](https://www.ca10.uscourts.gov/sites/ca10/files/opinions/010110588266.pdf)

  Limitation: Binding only in the Tenth Circuit. Hemmelgarn does not decide issue exhaustion or create a judicial exception when the government timely preserves the filing defense; only its durable procedural holdings are adopted.
<!-- claim:CLM-CIR10-005 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3582-3583,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-1,AUTH-CA10-BEGAY-631-F3D-1168 status:verified_current -->
- **CLM-CIR10-005:** In the Tenth Circuit, § 3583(e)(2) does not require changed circumstances before a district court may modify supervised-release conditions; the court must instead consider the listed factors, follow Rule 32.1, and ensure the new conditions satisfy the requirements applicable to supervised-release conditions. [*United States v. Begay*, 631 F.3d 1168, 1171–73 (10th Cir. 2011)](https://www.ca10.uscourts.gov/sites/ca10/files/opinions/01018581211.pdf)

  Limitation: Binding only in the Tenth Circuit. Begay addresses condition modification, not early termination, and does not dispense with factor consideration, Rule 32.1 procedure, or substantive limits on the modified condition.
<!-- claim:CLM-CIR10-006 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-1651,AUTH-USC-28-2255,AUTH-CA10-TORRES-282-F3D-1241 status:verified_current -->
- **CLM-CIR10-006:** In the Tenth Circuit, a federal prisoner cannot use audita querela to challenge a conviction or sentence when the claim is cognizable under § 2255; the substance of the filing controls and a custody-based collateral attack may be treated as a § 2255 motion subject to the applicable authorization rules. [*United States v. Torres*, 282 F.3d 1241, 1245–46 & n.6 (10th Cir. 2002)](https://openjurist.org/282/f3d/1241/united-states-v-torres)

  Limitation: Binding only in the Tenth Circuit. Torres assumed without deciding that audita querela might survive, distinguished coram nobis by custody, and tied notice before recharacterization to whether the filing would be the prisoner's first § 2255 motion.

## Application controls

1. Classify the judgment, custody source, motion, court, requested relief, and appellate posture before selecting an overlay row.
2. Use only a verified published circuit holding for a circuit proposition; do not promote a district opinion, unpublished disposition, dicta, or a party's characterization into binding law.
3. Run a current citator and check en banc, Supreme Court, statutory, rule, and local-rule changes before filing.
4. Preserve issue presentation, objections, the operative record, deadline trigger, and the governing standard of review.
5. If the issue register says `not_verified`, use the linked nationwide module only for its bounded nationwide proposition and obtain the missing controlling authority.
