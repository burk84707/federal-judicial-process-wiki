# Plea Negotiations, Plea Agreements, and Rule 11

## Verification boundary

This module supplies the nationwide federal framework and controlling Supreme Court limits. It does not determine whether a particular offer existed, construe a particular agreement or waiver, establish the defendant's understanding, decide whether counsel performed deficiently, select a breach remedy, calculate prejudice, or resolve circuit-specific enforcement, contract-construction, plain-error, factual-basis, withdrawal, or collateral-review questions. The signed agreement, offer history, plea and sentencing transcripts, charging papers, docket, and controlling circuit law remain indispensable.

## Negotiation and counsel

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-001 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-FRYE-566-US-134 status:verified_current -->
The Constitution does not create a right to receive a plea offer, but once the prosecution makes a formal offer the Sixth Amendment governs defense counsel's performance in advising about it. [*Missouri v. Frye*, 566 U.S. 134, 144–48 (2012)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-566/pdf/USREPORTS-566-134.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-002 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MABRY-467-US-504 status:verified_current -->
An unexecuted plea proposal, standing alone, is an executory arrangement without constitutional significance; a defendant has no federal constitutional right to specific performance of an offer withdrawn before the guilty plea was entered. [*Mabry v. Johnson*, 467 U.S. 504, 507–11 (1984)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-467/pdf/USREPORTS-467-504.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-003 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-FRYE-566-US-134 status:verified_current -->
Defense counsel has a general duty to communicate formal prosecution offers that may be favorable to the accused. [*Frye*, 566 U.S. at 145–47](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-566/pdf/USREPORTS-566-134.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-004 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-FRYE-566-US-134 status:verified_current -->
Prejudice from a formal offer that lapsed or was rejected through deficient performance requires reasonable probabilities that the defendant would have accepted it, it would have been entered without prosecutorial cancellation or judicial refusal where those actors had discretion, and the result would have been more favorable. [*Frye*, 566 U.S. at 147–49](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-566/pdf/USREPORTS-566-134.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-005 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-LAFLER-566-US-156 status:verified_current -->
The Sixth Amendment right to effective assistance applies when counsel advises a defendant whether to accept a plea offer, even if the defendant later receives a fair trial. [*Lafler v. Cooper*, 566 U.S. 156, 162–68 (2012)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-566/pdf/USREPORTS-566-156.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-006 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-LAFLER-566-US-156 status:verified_current -->
When deficient advice causes rejection followed by trial, prejudice requires reasonable probabilities that the defendant would have accepted, the prosecution would not have withdrawn for intervening reasons, the court would have accepted, and the offer would have produced a less severe conviction, sentence, or both. [*Lafler*, 566 U.S. at 163–64](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-566/pdf/USREPORTS-566-156.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-007 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-LAFLER-566-US-156 status:verified_current -->
The remedy for proven lost-offer ineffective assistance must neutralize the constitutional injury without creating a windfall; in *Lafler* the proper first step was reoffering, after which the trial court retained bounded discretion over the conviction and sentence. [*Lafler*, 566 U.S. at 170–75](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-566/pdf/USREPORTS-566-156.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-008 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-RUIZ-536-US-622 status:verified_current -->
The Constitution does not require the government to disclose material impeachment evidence before entering a plea agreement, and *Ruiz* likewise rejected a categorical constitutional right to pre-bargaining affirmative-defense information on the agreement before it. [*United States v. Ruiz*, 536 U.S. 622, 628–33 (2002)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-536/pdf/USREPORTS-536-622.pdf)

## Plea choices and conditional pleas

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-009 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Rule 11 permits pleas of not guilty, guilty, and—only with court consent—nolo contendere; a court considering nolo contendere must consider the parties' views and the public interest in effective justice. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(a)(1), (3)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-010 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
A conditional guilty or nolo plea requires both government consent and court approval, must reserve in writing review of a specified adverse pretrial ruling, and permits withdrawal if the defendant prevails on appeal. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(a)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-011 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-CLASS-583-US-174 status:verified_current -->
Rule 11(a)(2) is not the exclusive route for every post-plea claim: a guilty plea by itself did not bar *Class* from directly challenging the constitutionality of the statute of conviction where the claim did not contradict the indictment, plea terms, or admitted facts and challenged the government's power to convict. [*Class v. United States*, 583 U.S. 174, 180–86 (2018)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-583/pdf/USREPORTS-583-174.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-012 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-CLASS-583-US-174,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
*Class* does not convert an unconditional plea into a general reservation of pretrial rulings: the claim's substance, the plea and agreement, admitted facts, preservation, and any written Rule 11(a)(2) reservation must be classified separately. [*Class*, 583 U.S. at 181–87](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-583/pdf/USREPORTS-583-174.pdf); [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(a)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Colloquy, understanding, voluntariness, and factual basis

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-013 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Before accepting a guilty or nolo plea, the court must address the defendant personally in open court and determine that the defendant understands the matters Rule 11(b)(1) enumerates. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(b)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-014 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
The required colloquy includes the nature of each charge, possible perjury use of sworn answers, the right to counsel, and the right to plead not guilty or persist in that plea. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(b)(1)(A)–(D)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-015 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
The court must explain the trial rights to a jury, to confront and cross-examine, to protection from compelled self-incrimination, to testify and present evidence, and to compel witnesses, and that a guilty or nolo plea waives those trial rights. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(b)(1)(C), (E)–(F)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-016 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Rule 11 requires advice about maximum punishment, mandatory minimum, applicable forfeiture, restitution authority, the special assessment, and the court's duty to calculate and consider the advisory Guidelines and 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a) factors. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(b)(1)(G)–(M)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-017 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
If the agreement contains a provision waiving the right to appeal or collaterally attack the sentence, the court must determine that the defendant understands its terms; this colloquy requirement does not itself decide the provision's scope or enforceability. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(b)(1)(N)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-018 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
The court must warn a noncitizen that conviction may result in removal, denial of citizenship, and denial of future admission to the United States. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(b)(1)(O)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-019 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Before accepting the plea, the court must determine that it is voluntary and did not result from force, threats, or promises outside the plea agreement. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-020 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BRADY-397-US-742 status:verified_current -->
A guilty plea must be a voluntary and intelligent choice made with sufficient awareness of relevant circumstances and likely direct consequences; a plea is not involuntary merely because it was motivated by a lawful prospect of a lesser penalty than after trial. [*Brady v. United States*, 397 U.S. 742, 747–58 (1970)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-397/pdf/USREPORTS-397-742.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-021 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Before entering judgment on a guilty plea, the court must determine that a factual basis exists; Rule 11(b)(3) is a federal procedural command distinct from proof at trial and from every constitutional claim concerning innocence or offense elements. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(b)(3)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-022 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-PADILLA-559-US-356 status:verified_current -->
Defense counsel must advise a noncitizen about immigration consequences: when removal law is clear, correct advice is required; when it is unclear, counsel must at least advise that the charge may carry adverse immigration consequences. [*Padilla v. Kentucky*, 559 U.S. 356, 366–74 (2010)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-559/pdf/USREPORTS-559-356.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-023 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-HILL-474-US-52 status:verified_current -->
For ineffective assistance that allegedly caused an accepted guilty plea, *Hill* applies both *Strickland* prongs and ordinarily requires a reasonable probability that, absent counsel's error, the defendant would not have pleaded guilty and would have insisted on trial. [*Hill v. Lockhart*, 474 U.S. 52, 56–60 (1985)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-474/pdf/USREPORTS-474-52.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-024 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-LEE-582-US-357 status:verified_current -->
The accepted-plea prejudice inquiry is not defeated categorically by a weak trial defense; it compares the consequences and asks what this defendant rationally would have done, using contemporaneous evidence rather than post hoc assertion alone. [*Lee v. United States*, 582 U.S. 357, 364–71 (2017)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-582/pdf/USREPORTS-582-357.pdf)

## Agreements, acceptance, and breach

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-025 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
The government and defense may discuss a plea agreement, but the court must not participate in those discussions. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(c)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-026 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Rule 11(c)(1)(A) permits government promises not to bring or to dismiss other charges; Rule 11(c)(1)(B) permits a sentencing recommendation or nonopposition that does not bind the court; and Rule 11(c)(1)(C) permits agreement that a specified sentence, range, Guidelines provision, policy statement, or sentencing factor is appropriate and binds the court once accepted. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(c)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-027 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
The parties must disclose the plea agreement in open court when the plea is offered unless good cause supports in-camera disclosure. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(c)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-028 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
For a Rule 11(c)(1)(A) or (C) agreement the court may accept, reject, or defer until reviewing the presentence report; for a (B) agreement it must advise that the defendant cannot withdraw merely because the court does not follow the recommendation or request. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(c)(3)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-029 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Acceptance of a Rule 11(c)(1)(A) or (C) agreement makes the agreed disposition binding, while rejection requires on-record notice, an opportunity to withdraw, and a warning that a persisting plea may produce a less favorable disposition. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(c)(4)–(5)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-030 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-HYDE-520-US-670,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Acceptance of the guilty plea and acceptance of the plea agreement are distinct judicial acts; deferring the latter does not make an already accepted plea freely retractable. [*United States v. Hyde*, 520 U.S. 670, 674–80 (1997)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-520/pdf/USREPORTS-520-670.pdf); [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(c)(3), (d)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-031 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-SANTOBELLO-404-US-257 status:verified_current -->
When a guilty plea rests in significant part on a prosecution promise that induced it, the promise must be fulfilled; an inadvertent breach by a different prosecutor is not immaterial. [*Santobello v. New York*, 404 U.S. 257, 261–63 (1971)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-404/pdf/USREPORTS-404-257.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-032 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-SANTOBELLO-404-US-257 status:verified_current -->
For the proven breach in *Santobello*, the state court—not the defendant—was to choose between specific performance through resentencing before a different judge and permission to withdraw the plea. [*Santobello*, 404 U.S. at 262–63](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-404/pdf/USREPORTS-404-257.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-033 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-PUCKETT-556-US-129 status:verified_current -->
An unpreserved claim that the government breached a plea agreement receives ordinary Rule 52(b) plain-error review. [*Puckett v. United States*, 556 U.S. 129, 133–43 (2009)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-556/pdf/USREPORTS-556-129.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-034 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-PUCKETT-556-US-129 status:verified_current -->
A later government breach does not retroactively render a knowing and voluntary plea unknowing or involuntary; breach and the available remedy are analytically distinct from plea validity at entry. [*Puckett*, 556 U.S. at 137–39](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-556/pdf/USREPORTS-556-129.pdf)

## Plea-discussion evidence and waivers

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-035 authorities:AUTH-FRE-410 status:verified_current -->
Against the defendant who made the plea or participated in discussions, Evidence Rule 410 generally excludes a later-withdrawn guilty plea, a nolo plea, qualifying Rule 11 plea-proceeding statements, and qualifying statements to a prosecuting attorney during plea discussions that produced no guilty plea or a later-withdrawn plea. [Fed. R. Evid. 410(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-evidence.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-036 authorities:AUTH-FRE-410 status:verified_current -->
Rule 410 permits a qualifying plea-discussion statement when fairness requires considering it with another statement introduced from the same discussion, and in a perjury or false-statement prosecution if the defendant made it under oath, on the record, and with counsel present. [Fed. R. Evid. 410(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-evidence.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-037 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MEZZANATTO-513-US-196 status:verified_current -->
Absent an affirmative indication that the agreement was unknowing or involuntary, *Mezzanatto* enforced a defendant's agreement allowing impeachment use of plea-discussion statements; its holding does not by itself validate every broader case-in-chief or rebuttal waiver. [*United States v. Mezzanatto*, 513 U.S. 196, 200–10 (1995)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-513/pdf/USREPORTS-513-196.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-038 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52,AUTH-SCOTUS-GARZA-586-US-232 status:verified_current -->
An appellate or collateral-review waiver is an express agreement term, not a synonym for every consequence of pleading guilty; its text, colloquy, validity, scope, exceptions, and controlling circuit law must be examined claim by claim. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(b)(1)(N)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [*Garza v. Idaho*, 586 U.S. 232, 238–44 (2019)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/586bv.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-039 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-GARZA-586-US-232 status:verified_current -->
Because no appeal waiver bars all possible appellate claims, counsel who disregards a defendant's express instruction to file a notice of appeal performs deficiently, and the *Flores-Ortega* presumption of prejudice applies despite the waiver. [*Garza*, 586 U.S. at 238–49](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/586bv.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-040 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-TOLLETT-411-US-258 status:verified_current -->
A counseled, voluntary, intelligent guilty plea ordinarily forecloses independent collateral inquiry into antecedent constitutional deprivations and focuses the attack on the plea's voluntary and intelligent character and counsel's advice. [*Tollett v. Henderson*, 411 U.S. 258, 261–69 (1973)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-411/pdf/USREPORTS-411-258.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-041 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-CLASS-583-US-174,AUTH-SCOTUS-TOLLETT-411-US-258 status:verified_current -->
The ordinary *Tollett* rule and the power-to-convict claim recognized in *Class* are different from an express appellate waiver; none should be treated as an automatic rule that all claims survive or that all claims disappear. [*Class*, 583 U.S. at 180–87](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-583/pdf/USREPORTS-583-174.pdf); [*Tollett*, 411 U.S. at 266–67](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-411/pdf/USREPORTS-411-258.pdf)

## Withdrawal, record, review, and collateral limits

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-042 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Before the court accepts a guilty or nolo plea, the defendant may withdraw it for any reason or no reason. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(d)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-043 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52,AUTH-SCOTUS-HYDE-520-US-670 status:verified_current -->
After acceptance but before sentence, withdrawal is permitted if the court rejects a Rule 11(c)(1)(A) or (C) agreement or the defendant shows a fair and just reason; a mere change of mind is not a free-withdrawal entitlement. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(c)(5), (d)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [*Hyde*, 520 U.S. at 674–80](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-520/pdf/USREPORTS-520-670.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-044 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
After sentence, a guilty or nolo plea may not be withdrawn; it may be set aside only on direct appeal or collateral attack. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(e)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-045 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
The plea proceedings must be recorded by a court reporter or suitable recording device and, for a guilty or nolo plea, the record must include the Rule 11 inquiries and advice and the plea-agreement disclosure. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(g)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-046 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52,AUTH-SCOTUS-VONN-535-US-55,AUTH-SCOTUS-DOMINGUEZ-BENITEZ-542-US-74 status:verified_current -->
A Rule 11 variance is harmless if it does not affect substantial rights; an unpreserved Rule 11 claim is reviewed under Rule 52(b) on the whole record, and the defendant must show a reasonable probability that, but for the error, the defendant would not have pleaded guilty. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 11(h), 52](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [*United States v. Vonn*, 535 U.S. 55, 58–59, 62–75 (2002)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-535/pdf/USREPORTS-535-55.pdf); [*United States v. Dominguez Benitez*, 542 U.S. 74, 76, 80–85 (2004)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-542/pdf/USREPORTS-542-74.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-047 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-DAVILA-569-US-597 status:verified_current -->
Judicial participation in plea discussions violates Rule 11(c)(1), but *Davila* rejects automatic vacatur and requires the Rule 11(h) prejudice inquiry on the full record. [*United States v. Davila*, 569 U.S. 597, 602–13 (2013)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/569bv.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-PLEA-048 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-TIMMRECK-441-US-780 status:verified_current -->
A formal Rule 11 violation that is neither constitutional nor jurisdictional does not alone support § 2255 relief; collateral relief requires the kind of fundamental defect or exceptional circumstance cognizable under the collateral standard. [*United States v. Timmreck*, 441 U.S. 780, 783–85 (1979)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-441/pdf/USREPORTS-441-780.pdf)

## Application checklist

1. Preserve every written offer, expiration, rejection, counteroffer, and communication record.
2. Obtain the charging papers, signed agreement, plea petition, Rule 11 transcript, sentencing transcript, judgment, and docket.
3. Identify the plea type and whether plea acceptance and agreement acceptance occurred on different dates.
4. Classify each promise under Rule 11(c)(1)(A), (B), or (C), including mixed agreements.
5. Compare the colloquy to every applicable Rule 11(b)(1) item, voluntariness, and factual basis without treating a variance as automatic relief.
6. Parse each appellate, collateral-review, evidence-use, and discovery waiver by its actual words and controlling circuit law.
7. Separate offer-stage ineffective assistance, accepted-plea ineffective assistance, prosecution breach, judicial participation, withdrawal, direct review, and collateral review; each has different elements and remedies.
8. Confirm preservation and apply the correct harmless-error, plain-error, ineffective-assistance, or collateral standard.

## Open items requiring individualized or circuit research

- Whether a particular communication was a formal offer or protected plea discussion.
- Agreement-construction rules, government-breach burdens, materiality, and circuit-specific remedies.
- Enforceability, scope, exceptions, and miscarriage-of-justice doctrines for appellate and collateral-review waivers.
- Circuit factual-basis methodology, withdrawal factors, review standards, and treatment of plea-discussion waivers beyond *Mezzanatto*'s impeachment holding.
- Offense elements, immigration classification, sentencing exposure, Guidelines consequences, restitution, forfeiture, supervised release, and collateral consequences in the actual case.
- Any pending Rule 11 amendment is nonbinding unless and until it takes effect; the current rule text controls this module.
