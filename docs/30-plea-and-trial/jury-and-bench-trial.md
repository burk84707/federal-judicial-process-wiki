# Federal Jury and Bench Trial

## Verification boundary

This module states the current nationwide constitutional, statutory, and Criminal Rules framework for entitlement to a jury, waiver, jury composition and selection, bench findings, evidence in open court, presence, instructions, burden of proof, and verdict unanimity. It does not decide offense elements, local jury plans, individual jury-selection facts, evidentiary rulings, circuit-specific preservation applications, or harmlessness.

## Entitlement to a jury and waiver

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-001 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BALDWIN-399-US-66 status:verified_current -->
No offense carrying an authorized prison term greater than six months may be treated as petty for Sixth Amendment jury-trial purposes. [*Baldwin v. New York*, 399 U.S. 66, 68–74 (1970)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-399/pdf/USREPORTS-399-66.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-002 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BLANTON-489-US-538 status:verified_current -->
An offense with a maximum authorized prison term of six months or less is presumed petty for Sixth Amendment purposes; the defendant is entitled to a jury trial only if he can demonstrate that any additional statutory penalties, viewed in conjunction with the maximum authorized period of incarceration, are so severe that they clearly reflect a legislative determination that the offense is serious, and only penalties resulting from state action, such as those mandated by statute or regulation, are considered. [*Blanton v. City of North Las Vegas*, 489 U.S. 538, 543 & n.8 (1989)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-489/pdf/USREPORTS-489-538.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-003 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-23-24-26-30-31-43,AUTH-SCOTUS-SINGER-380-US-24 status:verified_current -->
When a jury trial is required, Rule 23 permits a bench trial only if the defendant waives a jury in writing, the government consents, and the court approves; the Constitution does not give a defendant an absolute right to insist on a bench trial. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 23(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf); [*Singer v. United States*, 380 U.S. 24, 34–37 (1965)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-380/pdf/USREPORTS-380-24.pdf)

## Jury size and bench findings

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-004 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-23-24-26-30-31-43 status:verified_current -->
A federal criminal jury consists of 12 persons unless Rule 23 provides otherwise. At any time before the verdict, the parties may, with the court's approval, stipulate in writing that the jury may consist of fewer than 12 persons, or that a jury of fewer than 12 persons may return a verdict if the court finds it necessary to excuse a juror for good cause after the trial begins. After the jury has retired to deliberate, the court may permit a jury of 11 persons to return a verdict, even without a stipulation by the parties, if the court finds good cause to excuse a juror. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 23(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-005 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-23-24-26-30-31-43 status:verified_current -->
In a bench trial the court must return a guilty-or-not-guilty finding and, if a party requests before the finding, must state specific findings of fact in open court or in a written decision or opinion. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 23(c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Jury pool and selection

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-006 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-1861-1862-1867 status:verified_current -->
The Jury Selection and Service Act declares a federal policy of random selection from a fair cross section of the community and gives all qualified citizens an opportunity to be considered for service and an obligation to serve when summoned. [28 U.S.C. § 1861](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/1861)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-007 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-1861-1862-1867 status:verified_current -->
No citizen may be excluded from federal grand- or petit-jury service because of race, color, religion, sex, national origin, or economic status. [28 U.S.C. § 1862](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/1862)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-008 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-1861-1862-1867 status:verified_current -->
In a criminal case, before the voir dire examination begins, or within seven days after the defendant discovered or could have discovered, by the exercise of diligence, the grounds therefor, whichever is earlier, the defendant may move to dismiss the indictment or stay the proceedings on the ground of substantial failure to comply with the provisions of title 28 in selecting the grand or petit jury. A motion containing a sworn statement of facts which, if true, would constitute a substantial failure to comply entitles the moving party to present in support of the motion the testimony of the jury commission or clerk, if available, relevant records and papers used by the jury commissioner or clerk that are not public or otherwise available, and any other relevant evidence. Section 1867's procedures are the exclusive means by which a person accused of a federal crime may challenge a jury on the ground that it was not selected in conformity with the provisions of title 28, but they do not preclude any other remedy, civil or criminal, available to vindicate or enforce any law prohibiting discrimination on account of race, color, religion, sex, national origin, or economic status in the selection of grand or petit jurors. [28 U.S.C. § 1867(a), (d), (e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/1867)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-009 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-DUREN-439-US-357 status:verified_current -->
A prima facie Sixth Amendment fair-cross-section claim requires a distinctive group, representation in venires that is not fair and reasonable relative to the community, and underrepresentation caused by systematic exclusion in the selection process; the right does not guarantee that the seated petit jury mirrors the community. [*Duren v. Missouri*, 439 U.S. 357, 363–67 (1979)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-439/pdf/USREPORTS-439-357.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-010 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-23-24-26-30-31-43 status:verified_current -->
Under Rule 24(a), the court may examine prospective jurors itself or permit the attorneys to do so; if the court examines the jurors, it must permit the attorneys to ask further questions that the court considers proper or to submit further questions that the court may ask if it considers them proper. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 24(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-011 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BATSON-476-US-79 status:verified_current -->
Equal protection forbids a prosecutor from exercising peremptory challenges to exclude prospective jurors on account of race or on the assumption that jurors of the defendant's race cannot be impartial. [*Batson v. Kentucky*, 476 U.S. 79, 89, 96–98 (1986)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-476/pdf/USREPORTS-476-79.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-012 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-JEB-511-US-127 status:verified_current -->
Intentional discrimination on the basis of sex in jury selection through peremptory challenges violates equal protection. [*J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B.*, 511 U.S. 127, 129, 140–43 (1994)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-511/pdf/USREPORTS-511-127.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-013 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-23-24-26-30-31-43 status:verified_current -->
Rule 24(b) sets the number of peremptory challenges by case category, and the court may allow additional peremptory challenges to multiple defendants; Rule 24(c) permits up to six alternate jurors, with additional peremptory challenges that may be used only to remove alternates. The court must ensure that a retained alternate does not discuss the case with anyone until that alternate replaces a juror or is discharged, and if an alternate replaces a juror after deliberations have begun, the court must instruct the jury to begin its deliberations anew. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 24(b)–(c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Trial conduct, burden, instructions, and verdict

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-014 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-23-24-26-30-31-43 status:verified_current -->
Witness testimony in a federal criminal trial must be taken in open court unless otherwise provided by a statute or by rules adopted under 28 U.S.C. §§ 2072–2077. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 26](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-015 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-23-24-26-30-31-43 status:verified_current -->
Unless Rule 43, Rule 5, or Rule 10 provides otherwise, the defendant must be present at every trial stage, including jury impanelment and the return of the verdict, and Rule 43(b) lists the circumstances in which presence is not required. A defendant who was initially present at trial waives the right to be present when voluntarily absent after the trial has begun. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 43(a)(2), (b), (c)(1)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-016 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-WINSHIP-397-US-358 status:verified_current -->
Due process requires the prosecution to prove beyond a reasonable doubt every fact necessary to constitute the charged crime. [*In re Winship*, 397 U.S. 358, 364 (1970)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-397/pdf/USREPORTS-397-358.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-017 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-23-24-26-30-31-43,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-11-52 status:verified_current -->
Rule 30 permits written instruction requests, made at the close of the evidence or at any earlier time the court reasonably sets, with a copy furnished to every other party; requires the court to inform the parties before closing arguments how it intends to rule on the requested instructions; and requires a party who objects to any portion of the instructions or to a failure to give a requested instruction to inform the court of the specific objection and the grounds for the objection before the jury retires to deliberate. Failure to object in accordance with Rule 30 precludes appellate review, except as permitted under Rule 52(b). [Fed. R. Crim. P. 30(a), (b), (d)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf) [Fed. R. Crim. P. 52(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-TRIAL-018 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-23-24-26-30-31-43 status:verified_current -->
A federal jury verdict must be unanimous and returned in open court. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 31(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

## Application checklist

1. Identify every charged offense and its maximum authorized prison term and additional statutory penalties.
2. Preserve any written jury waiver, government consent, court approval, and timing of the waiver colloquy.
3. Obtain the district's jury plan, source-list records available under governing law, voir-dire transcript, strike lists, stated explanations, and rulings.
4. Calendar any § 1867 challenge before voir dire or the earlier statutory discovery deadline and prepare the required sworn factual submission.
5. Record jury size, alternates, any excusal, the finding of good cause, and any instruction to restart deliberations.
6. Preserve instruction requests and make a distinct on-record objection before deliberations.
7. For a bench trial, request specific findings before the general finding if they are needed.

## Open items requiring individualized or circuit research

- The district jury plan, access to jury-selection records, local voir-dire practice, and any local filing requirement.
- The complete comparative-juror and strike record for a Batson or J.E.B. claim and the controlling circuit's review rules.
- Offense elements, affirmative defenses, evidentiary rulings, instruction formulations, preservation, harmlessness, and plain-error application.
- Whether a particular absence, remote appearance, jury-size change, alternate substitution, or bench waiver was valid on the actual record.
