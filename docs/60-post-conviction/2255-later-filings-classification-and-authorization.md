# Later § 2255 Filings — Classification and Authorization

## Verification boundary

“Second or successive” is a legal classification, not a count of papers. Most Supreme Court classification decisions arose under § 2254 and § 2244(b); this module identifies their exact holdings and does not silently convert every state-prisoner application into a complete § 2255 rule. Before classifying a federal filing, controlling circuit precedent remains necessary for prior untimeliness dismissals, voluntary withdrawals, mixed Rule 60 motions, limited sentence modifications, transfer, and other unresolved postures.

## Classify before applying the gate

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-001 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-PANETTI-551-US-930,AUTH-SCOTUS-MAGWOOD-561-US-320 status:verified_current -->
The statutory phrase “second or successive” is not self-defining and does not cover every application filed second in time; classification depends on the judgment, claim, ripeness, and prior disposition. [*Panetti v. Quarterman*, 551 U.S. 930, 943–47 (2007)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-551/pdf/USREPORTS-551-930.pdf); [*Magwood v. Patterson*, 561 U.S. 320, 331–38 (2010)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-561/pdf/USREPORTS-561-320.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-002 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255,AUTH-SCOTUS-MAGWOOD-561-US-320 status:verified_current -->
A docket count alone does not decide whether a filing is second or successive: *Magwood*, a § 2254 case, recognized that it is well settled that the phrase, a term of art, does not simply refer to all § 2254 applications filed second or successively in time, and, reasoning from § 2254's reference to the state-court judgment, held that the phrase must be interpreted with respect to the judgment challenged, while addressing only an application challenging a new state-court judgment for the first time. For federal prisoners, the certification requirement comes from § 2255(h). [28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255); [*Magwood*, 561 U.S. at 332–33, 338 n.12](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-561/pdf/USREPORTS-561-320.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-003 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BURTON-549-US-147 status:verified_current -->
In *Burton*, a later § 2254 petition challenging the same state judgment was successive, and filing it without court-of-appeals authorization left the district court without jurisdiction to entertain it. [*Burton v. Stewart*, 549 U.S. 147, 152–57 (2007)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-549/pdf/USREPORTS-549-147.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-004 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
For a filing legally classified as a second or successive § 2255 motion, § 2255(h) requires court-of-appeals panel certification before the district court may consider it. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

## New judgment and later-ripening claims

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-005 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MAGWOOD-561-US-320 status:verified_current -->
The first habeas application challenging a new state-court judgment entered after a conditional writ and resentencing was not second or successive in *Magwood*. [*Magwood*, 561 U.S. at 323–42](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-561/pdf/USREPORTS-561-320.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-006 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MAGWOOD-561-US-320 status:verified_current -->
*Magwood* treated the application as challenging the new judgment even though the alleged sentencing error had also occurred in the earlier judgment; the Court rejected a claim-by-claim rule for that first challenge to the new judgment. [*Magwood*, 561 U.S. at 330–42](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-561/pdf/USREPORTS-561-320.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-007 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MAGWOOD-561-US-320 status:verified_current -->
*Magwood* expressly did not decide whether a petitioner resentenced under a new judgment may use the first application against that judgment to challenge an undisturbed conviction component; that issue cannot be presented as a settled nationwide Supreme Court holding. [*Magwood*, 561 U.S. at 342](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-561/pdf/USREPORTS-561-320.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-008 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MAGWOOD-561-US-320 status:verified_current -->
Whether a Rule 35 correction, Rule 36 correction, § 3582 reduction, First Step Act reduction, partial resentencing, or amended judgment creates a new judgment for successive-filing purposes is not answered categorically by *Magwood*; the actual order and controlling circuit law are required. [*Magwood*, 561 U.S. at 323–42](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-561/pdf/USREPORTS-561-320.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-009 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-PANETTI-551-US-930 status:verified_current -->
*Panetti* held that a state prisoner's Ford incompetency-to-be-executed claim filed when it first became ripe after an execution date was set was not a second or successive application. [*Panetti*, 551 U.S. at 943–47](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-551/pdf/USREPORTS-551-930.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-010 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MARTINEZ-VILLAREAL-523-US-637 status:verified_current -->
A *Ford* claim of incompetency to be executed, presented in an earlier federal habeas petition, dismissed there as premature, and re-presented when ripe was treated in *Martinez-Villareal* as part of the first application rather than a second or successive application. [*Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal*, 523 U.S. 637, 640, 643–45 (1998)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-523/pdf/USREPORTS-523-637.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-011 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-SLACK-529-US-473 status:verified_current -->
When the first federal habeas petition was dismissed without condition and without prejudice for failure to exhaust, without adjudicating any claim on the merits, *Slack* treated the later petition as not second or successive; it applied pre-AEDPA law, though it did not suggest the definition would be different under AEDPA. [*Slack v. McDaniel*, 529 U.S. 473, 485–89 (2000)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-473.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-012 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-SLACK-529-US-473,AUTH-SCOTUS-BURTON-549-US-147 status:verified_current -->
The effect of other prior dispositions—including untimeliness, procedural default, voluntary withdrawal, lack of jurisdiction, defective form, or partial merits adjudication—must be researched under controlling circuit law; *Slack*'s no-merits exhaustion holding and *Burton*'s same-judgment holding do not supply one universal answer for all dismissals. [*Slack*, 529 U.S. at 485–89](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-473.pdf); [*Burton*, 549 U.S. at 152–57](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-549/pdf/USREPORTS-549-147.pdf)

## Recharacterization and postjudgment motions

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-013 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-CASTRO-540-US-375 status:verified_current -->
When a court recharacterizes a pro se litigant's motion as a first § 2255 motion, *Castro* requires the court to notify the litigant, warn that later § 2255 motions will be subject to the second-or-successive restrictions, and provide an opportunity to withdraw or amend the motion; an unwarned recharacterization cannot count as a § 2255 motion for those later restrictions, whether or not the litigant appealed. [*Castro v. United States*, 540 U.S. 375, 383–84 (2003)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-540/pdf/USREPORTS-540-375.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-014 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BANISTER-590-US-504 status:verified_current -->
A timely Rule 59(e) motion in a first habeas proceeding is part of that proceeding and is not a second or successive application. [*Banister v. Davis*, 590 U.S. 504, 508–19 (2020)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/preliminaryprint/590US2PP_web.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-015 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-GONZALEZ-CROSBY-545-US-524 status:verified_current -->
In § 2254 cases, a purported Rule 60(b) motion that seeks to add a new ground for relief or attacks the federal court's previous resolution of a claim on the merits functions as a successive habeas claim, while a motion attacking some defect in the integrity of the federal habeas proceedings may be a true Rule 60(b) motion; an attack based on the movant's own conduct or habeas counsel's omissions ordinarily does not go to integrity. *Gonzalez* limited its consideration to § 2254 cases because § 2255's successive-motion provision is not identical. [*Gonzalez v. Crosby*, 545 U.S. 524, 529 n.3, 531–33 & nn.4–5 (2005)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-545/pdf/USREPORTS-545-524.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-016 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12,AUTH-SCOTUS-GONZALEZ-CROSBY-545-US-524 status:verified_current -->
A “Rule 60” caption cannot authorize district-court merits review of a successive § 2255 claim, because before presenting a second or successive motion the movant must obtain an order from the appropriate court of appeals authorizing the district court to consider it. [Rule 9, Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download) *Gonzalez*, which limited its consideration to § 2254 cases, asks whether a Rule 60(b) motion advances one or more “claims” and treats claims couched in the language of a true Rule 60(b) motion as successive. [*Gonzalez*, 545 U.S. at 529 n.3, 531–32](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-545/pdf/USREPORTS-545-524.pdf)

## Section 2255(h) substantive gates

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-017 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(h)(1) requires newly discovered evidence that, if proven and viewed with the evidence as a whole, would establish by clear and convincing evidence that no reasonable factfinder would have found the movant guilty of the offense. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-018 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(h)(2) requires a new rule of constitutional law, previously unavailable, that the Supreme Court has made retroactive to cases on collateral review. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-019 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
A new statutory-interpretation rule is not a new rule of constitutional law and therefore does not satisfy § 2255(h)(2); any separate saving-clause question must be analyzed under § 2255(e) and the current saving-clause module. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(e), (h)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-020 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-TYLER-533-US-656 status:verified_current -->
For the parallel language in § 2244(b)(2)(A), *Tyler* holds that only the Supreme Court can make a new rule retroactive and that the Court must do so through a holding; § 2255(h)(2) uses the same “made retroactive” formulation. [*Tyler v. Cain*, 533 U.S. 656, 662–68 (2001)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-656.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-021 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-TYLER-533-US-656 status:verified_current -->
*Tyler* permits multiple Supreme Court holdings to make a rule retroactive only when those holdings necessarily dictate retroactivity; lower-court belief that a rule should apply retroactively is insufficient. [*Tyler*, 533 U.S. at 666; id. at 668–69 (O'Connor, J., concurring)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-656.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-022 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-SCHRIRO-542-US-348 status:verified_current -->
New substantive rules generally apply retroactively because they alter the range of conduct or class of persons the law punishes; rules regulating only the manner of determining culpability are procedural. [*Schriro v. Summerlin*, 542 U.S. 348, 351–55 (2004)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-542/pdf/USREPORTS-542-348.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-023 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-EDWARDS-VANNOY-593-US-255 status:verified_current -->
New constitutional rules of criminal procedure generally do not apply retroactively on federal collateral review, and *Edwards v. Vannoy* eliminated the theoretical watershed-procedure exception. [*Edwards v. Vannoy*, 593 U.S. 255, 265–72 (2021)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-5807_086c.pdf)

## Authorization procedure and unauthorized filings

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-024 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244,AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Before filing a legally second or successive § 2255 motion in district court, the movant must seek certification from the appropriate court of appeals as provided in § 2244. [28 U.S.C. §§ 2244(b)(3)(A), 2255(h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-025 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(b)(3)(B) assigns the authorization motion to a three-judge court-of-appeals panel. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(3)(B)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-026 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244,AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
The panel applies a prima facie showing at the authorization stage, using § 2255(h)'s federal-prisoner substantive gate rather than substituting § 2244(b)(2)'s state-prisoner wording. [28 U.S.C. §§ 2244(b)(3)(C), 2255(h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-027 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(b)(3)(D) directs a grant or denial within 30 days after filing; this module does not call the command jurisdictional, promise decision by that date, or invent a remedy for noncompliance. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(3)(D)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-028 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244,AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Authorization permits the proposed filing but is not a merits judgment or a grant of § 2255 relief; the district court must still apply the governing procedural bars, § 2255 standards, and claim-specific merits law. [28 U.S.C. §§ 2244(b)(3)(C), 2255(b), (h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-029 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244,AUTH-SCOTUS-BOWE-607-US-13 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(b)(4) directs a district court to dismiss any claim in an authorized second or successive application unless the applicant shows that the claim satisfies § 2244's requirements. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(4)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244) *Bowe* reasoned that § 2244(b)(4) plainly falls outside § 2255(h)'s cross-reference because it has nothing to do with how a filing is certified by a court of appeals panel, and rejected reading § 2255(h) to incorporate every § 2244 provision not expressly limited to state prisoners. [*Bowe v. United States*, 607 U.S. 13, 31–32 (2026)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/607us1r04_n7ip.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-030 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255,AUTH-SCOTUS-BURTON-549-US-147 status:verified_current -->
The district court cannot adjudicate the merits of an unauthorized filing that is legally a second or successive motion; whether to dismiss, transfer, sever a true Rule 60 component, or allow withdrawal must follow controlling circuit procedure. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255); [*Burton*, 549 U.S. at 152–57](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-549/pdf/USREPORTS-549-147.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-031 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-1631 status:verified_current -->
When a court finds a want of jurisdiction over a civil action or appeal, § 1631 requires it, if it is in the interest of justice, to transfer the matter to a court in which it could have been brought when filed, and the matter then proceeds as if filed in the receiving court on the date it was actually filed; because of the interest-of-justice condition, the statute does not make transfer automatic for every unauthorized § 2255 filing. [28 U.S.C. § 1631](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/1631)

## Review after the panel decision

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-032 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BOWE-607-US-13 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(b)(3)(E) does not bar Supreme Court certiorari review of a federal prisoner's unsuccessful request to file a successive § 2255 motion. [*Bowe*, 607 U.S. at 19–32](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/607us1r04_n7ip.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-033 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BOWE-607-US-13 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(b)(1)'s automatic previously-presented-claim bar does not apply to federal § 2255(h) motions, but all claims, even repeat ones, must still meet § 2255(h)'s strictly limited threshold requirements and § 2255(f)'s statute of limitations, and AEDPA may not have displaced other practices courts can use to address repetitive filings by federal prisoners. [*Bowe*, 607 U.S. at 36–41 & n.10](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/607us1r04_n7ip.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-034 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BOWE-607-US-13 status:verified_current -->
*Bowe* did not decide whether § 2244(b)(3)(E)'s rehearing bar applies to a federal-prisoner authorization decision; current circuit rules and precedent must be checked. [*Bowe*, 607 U.S. at 29–30](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/607us1r04_n7ip.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-035 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244,AUTH-USC-28-2255,AUTH-SCOTUS-BOWE-607-US-13 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(h) requires that a second or successive § 2255 motion be "certified as provided in section 2244 by a panel of the appropriate court of appeals" to contain one of the two showings listed in § 2255(h). *Bowe v. United States* explained that this cross-reference incorporates § 2244(b)(3)(A)–(D), which require a motion in the appropriate court of appeals for an order authorizing the district court to consider the filing, determination of that motion by a three-judge panel, a prima facie showing, and a grant or denial "not later than 30 days after the filing of the motion." *Bowe* observed in a parenthetical, without deciding the point, that "most Courts of Appeals view that requirement as advisory." Section 2244(b)(3)(E) provides that the grant or denial of an authorization "shall not be appealable and shall not be the subject of a petition for rehearing or for a writ of certiorari." *Bowe* held that § 2255(h)'s cross-reference does not clearly incorporate § 2244(b)(3)(E)'s certiorari bar, so a federal prisoner is not prohibited from seeking certiorari from a panel's denial of authorization to file a second or successive § 2255 motion. *Bowe* did not decide whether § 2244(b)(3)(E)'s panel-rehearing bar applies to federal prisoners, so rehearing practice must be checked under current controlling circuit law. The statutes do not prescribe the motion's form, attachments, length limits, filing method, or service; those matters require the current rules and forms of the particular court of appeals. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244); [28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255); [*Bowe v. United States*, 607 U.S. 13, 18, 28–36 (2026)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/607us1r04_n7ip.pdf)

## Related § 2244 provisions that remain on the state-judgment track

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-036 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(a) states a federal-custody anti-relitigation rule for a habeas application whose detention legality a federal judge has already determined, but it expressly says “except as provided in section 2255”; it is not a substitute for § 2255(h)'s later-motion framework. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-037 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244,AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(c) makes a prior Supreme Court judgment, on an appeal or certiorari review sought by the state prisoner from the state-court decision, conclusive as to all issues of fact or law on an asserted denial of a federal right actually adjudicated by the Supreme Court, unless the applicant pleads and the court finds a material and controlling fact not in that record that the applicant could not have placed there by reasonable diligence. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244) It applies to persons in custody under state-court judgments and does not establish the federal-prisoner authorization gate, which comes from § 2255(h). [28 U.S.C. § 2255(h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-038 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244,AUTH-SCOTUS-BOWE-607-US-13 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(b)(1) requires dismissal of a claim presented in a second or successive § 2254 application when that claim appeared in a prior application; *Bowe* holds that this state-track text is not imported into § 2255(h). [28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244); [*Bowe*, 607 U.S. at 32–38](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/607us1r04_n7ip.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-039 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244 status:verified_current -->
For a new claim in a second or successive § 2254 application, § 2244(b)(2) requires either a previously unavailable Supreme-Court-made-retroactive new constitutional rule or a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence plus clear-and-convincing proof that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(b)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-040 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(d)(1) supplies a one-year limitations period and four latest-of triggers for applications by persons held under state-court judgments; it is not § 2255(f)'s federal-prisoner clock. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(d)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-041 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(d)(2) excludes from that state-track one-year period the time during which a properly filed state postconviction or other collateral-review application concerning the pertinent judgment or claim is pending; § 2255 contains no identical provision. [28 U.S.C. § 2244(d)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2244)

<!-- claim:CLM-SOS-042 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2244,AUTH-USC-28-2255,AUTH-SCOTUS-DODD-545-US-353 status:verified_current -->
Section 2244(b)(3) does not state a freestanding deadline by which a federal prisoner must first seek § 2255(h) authorization, but claim-specific timing—including § 2255(f), *Dodd*'s rule for § 2255(f)(3), availability, and current circuit procedure—can still make prompt filing decisive. [28 U.S.C. §§ 2244(b)(3), 2255(f), (h)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255); [*Dodd v. United States*, 545 U.S. 353, 357–60 (2005)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-545/pdf/USREPORTS-545-353.pdf)

## Classification and authorization checklist

1. Obtain every original and amended judgment and identify what changed.
2. Inventory every earlier collateral filing, its label, claims, warnings, and exact disposition.
3. Classify Rule 59 and Rule 60 papers by timing and substance.
4. Identify whether the proposed claim existed and was ripe during the first proceeding.
5. Apply *Magwood*, *Panetti*, *Martinez-Villareal*, *Slack*, *Castro*, *Banister*, and *Gonzalez* only within their exact postures.
6. If legally successive, identify the precise § 2255(h)(1) or (h)(2) gate and supporting record.
7. Use the current circuit form and rules; do not file merits papers first in district court.
8. After a panel decision, preserve *Bowe*'s certiorari holding and its unresolved federal rehearing issue.

## Matters requiring controlling-circuit work

- Whether a particular limited sentence change creates a new judgment.
- Treatment of challenges to undisturbed components after resentencing.
- Prior untimeliness, default, withdrawal, jurisdictional, and mixed dispositions.
- Mixed Rule 60 handling, certificate requirements, and transfer versus dismissal.
- Prima facie formulations, evidence rules, filing forms, panel claim scope, rehearing, and treatment of the 30-day command.
