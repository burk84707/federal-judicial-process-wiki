# Initial § 2255 Motion, Filing, and Limitations

## Verification boundary

This module states the nationwide statutory and national-rule framework for an initial § 2255 motion and separates the four limitation triggers. It does not calculate a deadline without the actual judgment, appellate docket, asserted right, impediment, supporting facts, prison-mail facts, amendments, and controlling circuit law. *Holland*, *McQuiggin*, *Mayle*, and *Day* arose under § 2254 or § 2244; their direct holdings and any § 2255 application are kept distinct.

## Remedy, custody, and court

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-001 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
A prisoner claiming the right to release under a sentence of a federal court may move the court that imposed the sentence for § 2255 relief; the statute does not direct an initial motion to the district of present confinement. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-002 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(a) identifies four statutory grounds: a sentence imposed in violation of the Constitution or federal law, lack of jurisdiction to impose it, a sentence above the maximum authorized by law, or a sentence otherwise subject to collateral attack. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-003 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
If § 2255(b)'s merits conditions are met, the court must vacate and set aside the judgment and select the appropriate statutory relief: discharge, resentencing, a new trial, or correction of sentence. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-004 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(e) ordinarily makes the sentencing-court remedy exclusive for a federal prisoner attacking detention under the sentence, subject to the saving-clause boundary addressed separately; inability to satisfy § 2255's gatekeeping provisions does not by itself establish that the remedy is inadequate or ineffective. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

## Motion and filing requirements

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-005 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-1-3 status:verified_current -->
Rule 2 requires the motion to specify every ground available, state supporting facts, identify requested relief, be legible, and be signed under penalty of perjury; it must substantially follow the prescribed or local form. [Rule 2(b), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-006 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-1-3 status:verified_current -->
A movant seeking relief from judgments of more than one court must file a separate motion for each judgment. [Rule 2(d), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-007 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-1-3 status:verified_current -->
The clerk must file a § 2255 motion and enter it on the criminal docket of the case in which the challenged judgment was entered, then serve a copy on the United States attorney for that district. [Rule 3(b), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-008 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-1-3 status:verified_current -->
For an inmate using an institution's legal-mail system, Rule 3(d) treats the motion as timely if deposited in that system on or before the last filing day with prepaid first-class postage and the specified declaration, notarized statement, or other permitted proof. [Rule 3(d), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-009 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-1-3,AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Rule 3 does not create a separate filing period: it expressly sends timeliness to § 2255, so form acceptance, docketing, and the § 2255(f) calculation remain separate questions. [Rule 3(a), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download); [28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

## The four statutory limitation triggers

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-010 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(f) supplies one year from the latest applicable date among four separately worded triggers; a movant must identify and prove the trigger actually relied on rather than treating all four as extensions of finality. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-011 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255,AUTH-SCOTUS-CLAY-537-US-522 status:verified_current -->
Under § 2255(f)(1), when the court of appeals affirms and no certiorari petition is filed, finality occurs when the time for seeking certiorari expires, not when the appellate mandate issues. [*Clay v. United States*, 537 U.S. 522, 527–32 (2003)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-537/pdf/USREPORTS-537-522.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-012 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255,AUTH-SCOTUS-CLAY-537-US-522 status:verified_current -->
*Clay* did not decide the § 2255(f)(1) finality date when no direct appeal is taken; that question requires the actual docket, current appellate rules, and controlling circuit authority rather than an invented nationwide Supreme Court rule. [*Clay*, 537 U.S. at 524–25 & n.3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-537/pdf/USREPORTS-537-522.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-013 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(f)(2) starts on removal of a governmental impediment only when the impediment violated the Constitution or federal law and actually prevented the movant from making the motion. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-014 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(f)(3) applies only to a right newly recognized by the Supreme Court and made retroactively applicable to cases on collateral review. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-015 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-DODD-545-US-353 status:verified_current -->
When § 2255(f)(3) applies, the year runs from the date the Supreme Court initially recognized the asserted right, not from a later decision establishing retroactivity. [*Dodd v. United States*, 545 U.S. 353, 357–60 (2005)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-545/pdf/USREPORTS-545-353.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-016 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(f)(4) runs from when the facts supporting the claim could have been discovered through due diligence; it is a facts trigger, not a general restart whenever a court later changes or clarifies law. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(f)(4)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-017 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-JOHNSON-544-US-295 status:verified_current -->
A state-court order vacating a predicate conviction can qualify as a fact under § 2255(f)(4), but *Johnson* required due diligence in seeking that order after the federal judgment and measured the renewed period from notice of the vacatur. [*Johnson v. United States*, 544 U.S. 295, 302–11 (2005)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-544/pdf/USREPORTS-544-295.pdf)

## Equitable tolling, innocence, amendment, and court-raised timeliness

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-018 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-HOLLAND-560-US-631 status:verified_current -->
*Holland* holds that § 2244(d)'s state-habeas limitation is subject to equitable tolling; it did not directly decide § 2255(f), so a federal movant must identify controlling-circuit authority applying equitable tolling to § 2255. [*Holland v. Florida*, 560 U.S. 631, 645–49 (2010)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-560/pdf/USREPORTS-560-631.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-019 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-HOLLAND-560-US-631 status:verified_current -->
Under *Holland*'s standard, the litigant must show diligent pursuit and an extraordinary circumstance that stood in the way and prevented timely filing; ordinary case labels do not replace the required causal and fact-specific analysis. [*Holland*, 560 U.S. at 649–54](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-560/pdf/USREPORTS-560-631.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-020 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MCQUIGGIN-569-US-383 status:verified_current -->
*McQuiggin* recognizes a demanding actual-innocence gateway through § 2244(d)'s state-prisoner time bar, not a freestanding innocence claim and not a direct nationwide holding about § 2255(f); § 2255 use therefore requires controlling-circuit authority and the actual evidence. [*McQuiggin v. Perkins*, 569 U.S. 383, 386–401 (2013)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/569bv.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-021 authorities:AUTH-FRCP-15-59-60,AUTH-RG-2255-12,AUTH-SCOTUS-MAYLE-545-US-644 status:verified_current -->
Where compatible Civil Rule 15 applies through § 2255 Rule 12, an otherwise late amendment relates back only when the new claim shares a common core of operative facts with a timely claim; arising from the same trial, conviction, or sentence is not enough. [*Mayle v. Felix*, 545 U.S. 644, 656–64 (2005)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-545/pdf/USREPORTS-545-644.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-022 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-DAY-547-US-198 status:verified_current -->
*Day* permits a habeas court to consider a nonjurisdictional time bar not correctly raised by the State only after fair notice and an opportunity to respond and without overriding a deliberate waiver; because *Day* concerned § 2254 and § 2244(d), a § 2255 court must also apply controlling-circuit law and the government's actual position. [*Day v. McDonough*, 547 U.S. 198, 205–11 (2006)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-547/pdf/USREPORTS-547-198.pdf)

## Deadline checklist

1. Obtain the criminal judgment and every later amended judgment.
2. Obtain the complete direct-review docket, including mandate, rehearing, certiorari, and notice dates.
3. Determine which § 2255(f) trigger supports each claim and whether different claims rely on different facts or rights.
4. For (f)(3), identify the Supreme Court decision, date of initial recognition, asserted right, and authority making it retroactive.
5. For (f)(4), identify each supporting fact, when it existed, when it could have been discovered, and diligence steps.
6. Preserve institution-mail proof under Rule 3(d).
7. Compare every amendment with the timely pleading's operative facts under Rule 15 and controlling circuit law.
8. Research equitable tolling, innocence, sua-sponte timeliness, and no-direct-appeal finality in the controlling circuit.

## Matters requiring record- or circuit-specific work

- The exact finality date when no direct appeal was filed.
- Whether a particular obstacle was extraordinary and causally prevented filing.
- Whether *McQuiggin* opens a § 2255 time bar in the controlling circuit and whether any sentencing-innocence theory qualifies.
- Whether the government's conduct is forfeiture, error, or deliberate waiver under the applicable court's precedent.
- Local forms, filing mechanics, amendment practice, sealing, and page limits.
