# § 2255 Screening, Hearing, Appeal, and Postjudgment Motions

## Verification boundary

This module states national screening, response, discovery, record, hearing, counsel, remedy, certificate, notice, and postjudgment rules. It does not decide disputed facts, local schedules, appealability of every interlocutory order, standards of review for every issue, or the circuit-specific application of state-habeas holdings to § 2255.

## Screening, response, discovery, and record

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-049 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12 status:verified_current -->
Rule 4 requires prompt examination by the sentencing judge or an assigned judge and dismissal when the motion, attached exhibits, and prior record plainly show no entitlement to relief; otherwise the judge must order the United States attorney to respond or take other action. [Rule 4, Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-050 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-CASTRO-540-US-375 status:verified_current -->
Before recharacterizing a pro se filing as the litigant's first § 2255 motion, a federal court must notify the litigant, warn about later second-or-successive restrictions, and allow withdrawal or amendment; without those protections, the recharacterized filing does not count as the first motion for applying those later restrictions. [*Castro v. United States*, 540 U.S. 375, 381–84 (2003)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-540/pdf/USREPORTS-540-375.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-051 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12 status:verified_current -->
The government need not answer unless ordered; when ordered, Rule 5 requires it to address the allegations and specified prior-remedy matters, and the court may require relevant transcripts and briefs. [Rule 5(a)–(c), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-052 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12 status:verified_current -->
The movant may submit a reply to the government's answer within a time fixed by the judge, unless a local rule supplies the time. [Rule 5(d), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-053 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12 status:verified_current -->
Discovery is not automatic: Rule 6 requires good cause, reasons for the request, and proposed discovery, and requires appointment of financially eligible counsel when needed for effective discovery. [Rule 6, Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-054 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12 status:verified_current -->
Rule 7 permits expansion of the record with relevant materials and requires a party opposing added material to have an opportunity to admit or deny its correctness. [Rule 7, Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

## Evidentiary hearing, counsel, and relief

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-055 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255,AUTH-RG-2255-4-12 status:verified_current -->
Section 2255(b) requires notice, a prompt hearing, factual findings, and legal conclusions unless the motion and case files and records conclusively show no entitlement to relief; Rule 8 requires the judge to determine the hearing question after reviewing the answer, transcripts, record, and submitted materials. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A28+section%3A2255+edition%3Aprelim%29); [Rule 8(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-056 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MACHIBRODA-368-US-487 status:verified_current -->
Specific, detailed factual allegations about off-record plea promises could not be resolved solely against the movant through the judge's personal recollection in *Machibroda*; the Court did not hold that every allegation requires a full courtroom hearing. [*Machibroda v. United States*, 368 U.S. 487, 493–96 (1962)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-368/pdf/USREPORTS-368-487.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-057 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BLACKLEDGE-ALLISON-431-US-63 status:verified_current -->
Plea-hearing declarations carry a strong presumption of verity, but they are not invariably insurmountable; a sufficiently specific collateral allegation and the full record determine whether summary dismissal or further proceedings are proper. [*Blackledge v. Allison*, 431 U.S. 63, 73–82 (1977)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-431/pdf/USREPORTS-431-63.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-058 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12 status:verified_current -->
If an evidentiary hearing is warranted, Rule 8(c) requires appointment of counsel for a financially eligible movant; outside mandatory rule-based circumstances, counsel is not automatically guaranteed by this rule. [Rule 8(c), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-059 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2255 status:verified_current -->
The remedy must match the established defect: § 2255(b) authorizes discharge, resentencing, a new trial, or correction of sentence after vacatur, but it does not make every listed remedy available for every successful claim. [28 U.S.C. § 2255(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A28+section%3A2255+edition%3Aprelim%29)

## Certificate of appealability and notice

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-060 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2253,AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
A prisoner may not appeal the final order in a § 2255 proceeding without a certificate of appealability from a circuit justice or judge, and the certificate requires a substantial showing of the denial of a constitutional right. [28 U.S.C. § 2253(c)(1)(B), (c)(2)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A28+section%3A2253+edition%3Aprelim%29); [Fed. R. App. P. 22(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-061 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12 status:verified_current -->
When entering a final order adverse to the movant, the district court must issue or deny a certificate and, if issuing one, state the specific issue or issues satisfying § 2253(c)(2). [Rule 11(a), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-062 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-SLACK-529-US-473 status:verified_current -->
For a merits denial, *Slack* asks whether reasonable jurists could debate the district court's resolution of the constitutional claim or conclude the issue deserves encouragement to proceed further. [*Slack v. McDaniel*, 529 U.S. 473, 483–84 (2000)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-473.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-063 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-SLACK-529-US-473 status:verified_current -->
For a procedural dismissal without reaching the constitutional claim, *Slack* requires both a debatable valid constitutional claim and a debatable correctness of the procedural ruling. [*Slack*, 529 U.S. at 484–85](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-473.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-064 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MILLER-EL-537-US-322,AUTH-SCOTUS-BUCK-580-US-100 status:verified_current -->
The certificate inquiry is a threshold debatability assessment, not full merits adjudication; a court may not first decide the merits against the applicant and then deny a certificate because the applicant lost that merits determination. [*Miller-El v. Cockrell*, 537 U.S. 322, 336–42 (2003)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-537/pdf/USREPORTS-537-322.pdf); [*Buck v. Davis*, 580 U.S. 100, 115–23 (2017)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-580/pdf/USREPORTS-580-100.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-065 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-2253,AUTH-SCOTUS-GONZALEZ-THALER-565-US-134 status:verified_current -->
Section 2253(c)(1)'s certificate requirement is jurisdictional, but *Gonzalez v. Thaler* holds that a certificate's failure to specify a constitutional issue as § 2253(c)(3) directs is mandatory but nonjurisdictional. [*Gonzalez v. Thaler*, 565 U.S. 134, 142–49 (2012)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-565/pdf/USREPORTS-565-134.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-066 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12,AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
Rule 11(b) makes Appellate Rule 4(a) govern a § 2255 appeal; because the United States is a party, Rule 4(a)(1)(B)'s ordinary notice period is 60 days after entry of the appealed judgment or order, subject to the rule's exact entry, tolling, inmate, extension, and reopening provisions. [Rule 11(b), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download); [Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-067 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12,AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
A timely notice of appeal remains necessary even when the district court denies a certificate; the notice can be treated as a request for a certificate under Appellate Rule 22(b)(2), but the certificate process does not replace notice filing. [Rule 11(b), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download); [Fed. R. App. P. 22(b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-068 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
Appellate Rule 4(a)(4)(A) identifies the timely district-court motions that reset the appeal period for all parties; classification, timeliness, and disposition of the actual postjudgment filing must be established before calculating the notice deadline. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(a)(4)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

## Rules 59 and 60 after a habeas judgment

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-069 authorities:AUTH-FRCP-15-59-60 status:verified_current -->
Civil Rule 59(e) requires a motion to alter or amend a judgment within 28 days after judgment entry, and Civil Rule 6(b)(2) bars extending that deadline. [Fed. R. Civ. P. 59(e), 6(b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-070 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BANISTER-590-US-504 status:verified_current -->
*Banister* holds that a timely Rule 59(e) motion in a first habeas proceeding is part of that proceeding rather than a second or successive application, while emphasizing Rule 59(e)'s narrow corrective function and strict 28-day limit. [*Banister v. Davis*, 590 U.S. 504, 508–19 (2020)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/590us2r39_fd9g.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-071 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-GONZALEZ-CROSBY-545-US-524 status:verified_current -->
Under *Gonzalez v. Crosby*, a Rule 60 motion that adds a merits ground or attacks the federal court's merits resolution functions as a successive habeas claim, while a motion attacking a defect in the integrity of the federal habeas proceeding may be a true Rule 60 motion; *Gonzalez* arose under § 2254, so § 2255 application must follow controlling-circuit law. [*Gonzalez v. Crosby*, 545 U.S. 524, 530–36 (2005)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-545/pdf/USREPORTS-545-524.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-072 authorities:AUTH-FRCP-15-59-60,AUTH-SCOTUS-KEMP-596-US-528 status:verified_current -->
Civil Rule 60(b)(1) includes a judge's legal error under *Kemp*, and a motion under Rule 60(b)(1), (2), or (3) must be made within one year after judgment entry as well as within a reasonable time. [*Kemp v. United States*, 596 U.S. 528, 533–39 (2022)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/21-5726_5iel.pdf); [Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(c)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-073 authorities:AUTH-FRCP-15-59-60,AUTH-SCOTUS-GONZALEZ-CROSBY-545-US-524 status:verified_current -->
Rule 60(b)(6) requires extraordinary circumstances and a motion within a reasonable time; it cannot be used to evade the one-year limits on clauses (1) through (3) or habeas successive-application controls. [Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b), (c)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf); [*Gonzalez*, 545 U.S. at 535–38](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-545/pdf/USREPORTS-545-524.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-074 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-12 status:verified_current -->
Civil and Criminal Rules may be applied to § 2255 proceedings only to the extent they are not inconsistent with a statute or the Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings. [Rule 12, Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

<!-- claim:CLM-2255-075 authorities:AUTH-RG-2255-4-12 status:verified_current -->
A motion to reconsider the district court's certificate ruling does not extend the time to appeal. [Rule 11(a), Rules Governing § 2255 Proceedings](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/27805/download)

## Procedure checklist

1. Determine whether Rule 4 permits summary dismissal or requires a response.
2. If the filing was labeled differently, apply *Castro* before treating it as a first § 2255 motion.
3. Identify what factual disputes remain after the answer, reply, transcripts, and expanded record.
4. Apply § 2255(b), Rule 8, *Machibroda*, and *Blackledge* to the actual allegations and record.
5. Match any relief to the proven violation and the governing remedial rule.
6. Obtain the final order and the district court's certificate ruling.
7. Independently calculate the notice deadline from entry and every Rule 4(a)(4) event.
8. Classify every Rule 59 or Rule 60 filing by timing and substance before treating it as ordinary civil postjudgment litigation.

## Matters requiring circuit- or record-specific work

- Standards of review for particular rulings and the appealability of interlocutory collateral orders.
- When a paper hearing, affidavit process, or live evidentiary hearing is required.
- Rule 60 classification, mixed motions, transfer versus dismissal, and certificate requirements on postjudgment appeals.
- Local response, reply, discovery, hearing, sealing, and certificate procedures.
