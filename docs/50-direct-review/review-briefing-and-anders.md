# Review Standards, Briefing, Oral Argument, and Anders Procedure

## Verification boundary

This module verifies nationwide error standards at the level stated by the Federal Rules and controlling Supreme Court opinions, the constitutional minimum for an Anders withdrawal, and current national briefing and oral-argument rules. It does not assign a standard to an unidentified issue, decide preservation or prejudice on a record, supply a circuit's waiver doctrine, or replace local forms, schedules, record-excerpt rules, and Anders procedures.

## Preservation and selection of the governing standard

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-021 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34,AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
The appellant's brief must give, for each issue, a concise statement of the applicable standard of review. [Fed. R. App. P. 28(a)(8)(B)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf) A party preserves a claim of error by informing the court, when the ruling or order is made or sought, of the action it wishes the court to take or its objection and the grounds; the absence of an objection does not later prejudice a party who had no opportunity to object, and rulings admitting or excluding evidence are governed by Federal Rule of Evidence 103. Any error that does not affect substantial rights must be disregarded, while a plain error that affects substantial rights may be considered even though it was not brought to the court's attention. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 51, 52](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-022 authorities:AUTH-FRCRIM-P-32-51-52 status:verified_current -->
Rule 52(a) directs courts to disregard an error, defect, irregularity, or variance that does not affect substantial rights, but the substantive harmlessness inquiry and allocation of burden depend on the type of error and controlling authority. [Fed. R. Crim. P. 52(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-criminal-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-023 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-CHAPMAN-386-US-18 status:verified_current -->
For a preserved federal constitutional trial error subject to harmless-error review, *Chapman* requires the beneficiary of the error to prove harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; structural errors and distinct postures require separate analysis. [*Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, 22–24 (1967)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-386/pdf/USREPORTS-386-18.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-024 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-KOTTEAKOS-328-US-750 status:verified_current -->
For preserved nonconstitutional error governed by the federal harmless-error statute, reversal is required when the reviewing court, after examining the whole record, is left in grave doubt whether the error had substantial influence on the result. [*Kotteakos v. United States*, 328 U.S. 750, 764–65 (1946)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-328/pdf/USREPORTS-328-750.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-025 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-NEDER-527-US-1 status:verified_current -->
Most constitutional errors are subject to harmless-error analysis; only a limited class of structural defects affects the framework of the trial and defies ordinary harmlessness review, and *Neder* held that omission of an element from jury instructions was not categorically structural. [*Neder v. United States*, 527 U.S. 1, 7–15 (1999)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-527/pdf/USREPORTS-527-1.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-026 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-OLANO-507-US-725 status:verified_current -->
Rule 52(b) plain-error review requires an error that was not affirmatively waived, that is plain, and that affects substantial rights; even then, correction is discretionary and ordinarily requires an error seriously affecting the fairness, integrity, or public reputation of judicial proceedings. [*United States v. Olano*, 507 U.S. 725, 731–37 (1993)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-507/pdf/USREPORTS-507-725.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-027 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-HENDERSON-568-US-266 status:verified_current -->
For Rule 52(b), an error is assessed for plainness at the time of appellate review even when the law was unsettled at the time of the district-court ruling. [*Henderson v. United States*, 568 U.S. 266, 268–79 (2013)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-568/pdf/USREPORTS-568-266.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-028 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-OLANO-507-US-725 status:verified_current -->
Under the ordinary Rule 52(b) formulation, the appellant bears the burden to show that an unpreserved error affected substantial rights, usually meaning prejudice to the outcome; specialized presumptions and issue-specific applications require their own authority. [*Olano*, 507 U.S. at 734–35](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-507/pdf/USREPORTS-507-725.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-029 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-OLANO-507-US-725 status:verified_current -->
Satisfaction of the first three plain-error elements does not compel relief: the appellate court must separately decide whether to exercise its remedial discretion under the fourth element. [*Olano*, 507 U.S. at 735–37](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-507/pdf/USREPORTS-507-725.pdf)

## Anders procedure and counsel's issue selection

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-030 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ANDERS-386-US-738 status:verified_current -->
When appointed counsel conscientiously concludes that a first appeal as of right is wholly frivolous, *Anders* requires a request to withdraw accompanied by a brief referring to anything in the record that might arguably support the appeal, with a copy furnished to the client and time for the client to raise points; local rules govern additional filing details. [*Anders v. California*, 386 U.S. 738, 744 (1967)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-386/pdf/USREPORTS-386-738.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-031 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ANDERS-386-US-738,AUTH-SCOTUS-PENSON-488-US-75 status:verified_current -->
The appellate court must independently examine the record to determine whether the appeal is wholly frivolous; if it finds any arguable nonfrivolous issue, it must provide counsel to brief the appeal before deciding the merits. [*Anders*, 386 U.S. at 744](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-386/pdf/USREPORTS-386-738.pdf); [*Penson v. Ohio*, 488 U.S. 75, 80–85 (1988)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-488/pdf/USREPORTS-488-75.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-032 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ANDERS-386-US-738,AUTH-SCOTUS-PENSON-488-US-75 status:verified_current -->
The Anders threshold is whether the appeal is wholly frivolous, not whether success is unlikely; a legal point arguable on its merits is enough to require adversarial representation under the procedure. [*Anders*, 386 U.S. at 744](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-386/pdf/USREPORTS-386-738.pdf); [*Penson*, 488 U.S. at 83–85](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-488/pdf/USREPORTS-488-75.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-033 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-JONES-BARNES-463-US-745,AUTH-SCOTUS-ANDERS-386-US-738 status:verified_current -->
Appointed appellate counsel has no constitutional duty to raise every nonfrivolous issue requested by the defendant and may, as a matter of professional judgment, decide not to present those points. [*Jones v. Barnes*, 463 U.S. 745, 751–54 (1983)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-463/pdf/USREPORTS-463-745.pdf) If counsel finds the case wholly frivolous after a conscientious examination, counsel should so advise the court and request permission to withdraw, and that request must be accompanied by a brief referring to anything in the record that might arguably support the appeal. [*Anders v. California*, 386 U.S. 738, 744 (1967)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-386/pdf/USREPORTS-386-738.pdf)

## Briefs and oral argument

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-034 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34 status:verified_current -->
Rule 28(a) prescribes the principal components of an appellant's brief, including required disclosures where applicable, tables, jurisdiction, issues, the case statement, summary, argument with record and authority citations and the applicable standard of review, conclusion, and required compliance certificate; local rules may add or refine requirements. [Fed. R. App. P. 28(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-035 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34 status:verified_current -->
An appellee's brief must conform to the requirements of Rule 28(a)(1)–(8) and (10), except that the jurisdictional statement, statement of the issues, statement of the case, and statement of the standard of review need not appear unless the appellee is dissatisfied with the appellant's statement. The appellant may file a brief in reply to the appellee's brief, which must contain a table of contents and a table of authorities; unless the court permits, no further briefs may be filed. [Fed. R. App. P. 28(b), (c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-036 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34 status:verified_current -->
Under Rule 31(a)(1), the appellant must serve and file a brief within 40 days after the record is filed, and the appellee must serve and file a brief within 30 days after the appellant's brief is served. The appellant may serve and file a reply brief within 21 days after service of the appellee's brief, but a reply brief must be filed at least 7 days before argument, unless the court, for good cause, allows a later filing. Under Rule 31(a)(2), a court of appeals that routinely considers cases on the merits promptly after the briefs are filed may shorten the time to serve and file briefs, either by local rule or by order in a particular case. [Fed. R. App. P. 31(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-037 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34 status:verified_current -->
Rule 32 governs the form of briefs and appendices, page and type-volume limits, and certificates of compliance. Every court of appeals must accept documents that comply with Rule 32's form requirements and the rules' length limits, though by local rule or order a court may accept documents that do not meet all of them; the number of brief copies may be changed by local rule or by order in a particular case. [Fed. R. App. P. 31(b), 32](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-038 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34 status:verified_current -->
Oral argument is allowed unless a three-judge panel unanimously concludes after examining the briefs and record that the appeal is frivolous, the dispositive issue is authoritatively decided, or the facts and arguments are adequately presented and argument would not significantly aid decision. [Fed. R. App. P. 34(a)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)
