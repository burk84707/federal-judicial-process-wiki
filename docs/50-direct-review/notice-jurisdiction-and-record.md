# Criminal Appeal Notice, Jurisdiction, Waivers, and Record

## Verification boundary

This module verifies current nationwide notice-of-appeal rules, general criminal finality and appellate-jurisdiction statutes, narrow Supreme Court finality decisions, bounded waiver rules, and the Federal Rules governing the record. It does not decide whether a particular order is appealable, calculate a deadline without the docket and all deadline-affecting events, construe a particular waiver, identify every statutory interlocutory route, or replace circuit and local filing rules.

## Notice and filing

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-001 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
An appeal as of right is taken by filing a notice of appeal with the district clerk within Rule 4's time; filing the notice, not filing a merits brief in the court of appeals, begins the appeal. [Fed. R. App. P. 3(a)(1), 4(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-002 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
A defendant in a criminal case ordinarily must file within fourteen days after the later of entry of the judgment or order appealed from or the government's filing of a notice of appeal; the exact docket entry and any later government notice must be checked. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(1)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-003 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
When the government may appeal in a criminal case, its Rule 4(b) notice period ordinarily is thirty days after the later of entry of the judgment or order appealed from or the defendant's notice of appeal. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(1)(B)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-004 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
A defendant's notice filed after the court announces a decision, sentence, or order but before entry of the judgment or order is treated as filed on the entry date and after entry. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-005 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
For a defendant, a timely Rule 29 motion, Rule 34 motion, or Rule 33 motion extends the notice trigger to the later entry of the judgment or the order disposing of the last such motion, but a new-evidence Rule 33 motion has that effect only if made no later than fourteen days after judgment. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(3)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-006 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
A Rule 35(a) sentence-correction motion does not suspend the time to appeal the judgment of conviction, and a district court may correct under Rule 35(a) despite a filed notice; the two clocks therefore must be tracked separately. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(5)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-007 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
Upon a finding of excusable neglect or good cause, the district court may extend a criminal notice period for no more than thirty days from expiration of the original period, before or after expiration and with or without motion and notice; that possible extension is not part of the ordinary filing period. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(4)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-008 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
An institutional inmate may obtain Rule 4(c)'s filing date by timely depositing the notice in the institution's internal mail system and supplying the rule's declaration, notarized statement, or other qualifying evidence; a legal-mail system must be used when one is available. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(c)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-009 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
If a notice is mistakenly filed in the court of appeals, the circuit clerk must note the receipt date, send it to the district clerk, and it is considered filed in the district court on the date the circuit clerk received it. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(d)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-010 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MANRIQUE-581-US-116 status:verified_current -->
When restitution is deferred and a later amended judgment fixes the amount, a defendant wishing to challenge that order must file a notice from it; if no notice is filed and the government timely objects, the mandatory claim-processing rule bars review of that restitution order. [*Manrique v. United States*, 581 U.S. 116, 120–25 (2017)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-581/pdf/USREPORTS-581-116.pdf)

## Final judgment and statutory appellate authority

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-011 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BERMAN-302-US-211 status:verified_current -->
In a federal criminal case, final judgment ordinarily means the sentence, and the judgment is appealable even when execution of sentence is suspended. [*Berman v. United States*, 302 U.S. 211, 212–13 (1937)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-302/pdf/USREPORTS-302-211.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-012 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-1291 status:verified_current -->
Section 1291 gives the courts of appeals jurisdiction over appeals from final decisions of the district courts except where direct Supreme Court review is authorized; identifying a criminal sentence as ordinarily final does not make every later or interlocutory order appealable. [28 U.S.C. § 1291](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title28-section1291)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-013 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-FLANAGAN-465-US-259 status:verified_current -->
The final-judgment rule is applied with particular stringency in criminal cases, and the collateral-order doctrine covers only a narrow class; *Flanagan* held that a pretrial order disqualifying defense counsel was not immediately appealable. [*Flanagan v. United States*, 465 U.S. 259, 263–70 (1984)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-465/pdf/USREPORTS-465-259.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-014 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ABNEY-431-US-651 status:verified_current -->
A pretrial order denying a motion to dismiss on double-jeopardy grounds is immediately appealable to the extent it rejects a colorable Double Jeopardy Clause claim because the asserted right includes avoiding the second trial itself. [*Abney v. United States*, 431 U.S. 651, 659–62 (1977)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-431/pdf/USREPORTS-431-651.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-015 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3731 status:verified_current -->
Government criminal appeals depend on 18 U.S.C. § 3731's covered order, certification, thirty-day, diligent-prosecution, and Double Jeopardy limits; the statute does not grant the defendant a reciprocal interlocutory route. [18 U.S.C. § 3731](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A3731+edition%3Aprelim%29)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-016 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3742 status:verified_current -->
Section 3742 separately specifies the defendant's and government's grounds to appeal an otherwise final sentence and the appellate court's disposition authority; a disagreement with sentence alone is not a substitute for a statutory ground. [18 U.S.C. § 3742](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A3742+edition%3Aprelim%29)

## Guilty pleas and express appeal waivers

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-017 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-GARZA-586-US-232,AUTH-SCOTUS-CLASS-583-US-174 status:verified_current -->
A guilty plea or appeal waiver does not mean that no notice may be filed or that every issue is necessarily barred: *Garza* applies presumed prejudice when counsel disregards an express instruction to appeal despite a waiver, while *Class* holds that an ordinary guilty plea alone did not waive the identified constitutional attack on the statute of conviction. The text, scope, validity, exceptions, government invocation, and controlling circuit law for an express waiver still must be analyzed. [*Garza v. Idaho*, 586 U.S. 232, 238–49 (2019)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/18pdf/17-1026_2c83.pdf); [*Class v. United States*, 583 U.S. 174, 181–85 (2018)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/16-424_g2bh.pdf)

## Record on appeal

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-018 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-10-11 status:verified_current -->
The Rule 10 record consists of the original district-court papers and exhibits, any transcript, and a certified copy of the docket entries; appellate briefing is tied to that record rather than an opportunity to create an entirely new evidentiary record. [Fed. R. App. P. 10(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-019 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-10-11 status:verified_current -->
Within fourteen days after filing the notice, or entry of an order disposing of the last timely motion specified in Rule 4(a)(4)(A), an appellant must order the needed transcript portions and make satisfactory payment arrangements or file the prescribed certificate that no transcript will be ordered; issue-specific transcript and statement duties then apply. [Fed. R. App. P. 10(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-020 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-10-11 status:verified_current -->
When proceedings were not recorded or a transcript is unavailable, Rule 10(c) provides a statement procedure, and Rule 10(d) permits an agreed statement; Rule 11 then governs assembly, completion, retention, and transmission of the record, subject to circuit plans, local rules, and court orders. [Fed. R. App. P. 10(c)–(d), 11](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)
