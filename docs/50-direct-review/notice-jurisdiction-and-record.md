# Criminal Appeal Notice, Jurisdiction, Waivers, and Record

## Verification boundary

This module verifies current nationwide notice-of-appeal rules, general criminal finality and appellate-jurisdiction statutes, narrow Supreme Court finality decisions, bounded waiver rules, and the Federal Rules governing the record. It does not decide whether a particular order is appealable, calculate a deadline without the docket and all deadline-affecting events, construe a particular waiver, identify every statutory interlocutory route, or replace circuit and local filing rules.

## Notice and filing

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-001 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
An appeal as of right is taken by filing a notice of appeal with the district clerk within Rule 4's time; filing the notice, not filing a merits brief in the court of appeals, begins the appeal. [Fed. R. App. P. 3(a)(1), 4(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-002 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
In a criminal case, a defendant's notice of appeal must be filed in the district court within 14 days after the later of the entry of either the judgment or the order being appealed, or the filing of the government's notice of appeal. Rule 4(b)(3)(A) sets the time to appeal from a judgment of conviction when the defendant timely makes one of the motions it lists. Upon a finding of excusable neglect or good cause, the district court may extend the time to file a notice of appeal for a period not to exceed 30 days from the expiration of the time otherwise prescribed by Rule 4(b). A judgment or order is entered for purposes of Rule 4(b) when it is entered on the criminal docket. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(1)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-003 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
When the government is entitled to appeal in a criminal case, its notice of appeal must be filed in the district court within 30 days after the later of the entry of the judgment or order being appealed, or the filing of a notice of appeal by any defendant. Upon a finding of excusable neglect or good cause, the district court may extend the time to file a notice of appeal for a period not to exceed 30 days from the expiration of the time otherwise prescribed by Rule 4(b). [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(1)(B)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-004 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
A defendant's notice filed after the court announces a decision, sentence, or order but before entry of the judgment or order is treated as filed on the entry date and after entry. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(2)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-005 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
For a defendant, a timely Rule 29 motion, Rule 34 motion, or Rule 33 motion extends the notice trigger to the later entry of the judgment or the order disposing of the last such motion, but a new-evidence Rule 33 motion has that effect only if made no later than fourteen days after judgment. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(3)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-006 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
A Rule 35(a) sentence-correction motion does not suspend the time to appeal the judgment of conviction, and a district court may correct under Rule 35(a) despite a filed notice; the two clocks therefore must be tracked separately. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(5)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-007 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
Upon a finding of excusable neglect or good cause, the district court may extend a criminal notice period for no more than thirty days from expiration of the original period, before or after expiration and with or without motion and notice; that possible extension is not part of the ordinary filing period. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(b)(4)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-008 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
An institutional inmate's notice of appeal is timely under Rule 4(c) if it is deposited in the institution's internal mail system on or before the last day for filing and is accompanied by a 28 U.S.C. § 1746 declaration or notarized statement setting out the date of deposit and stating that first-class postage is being prepaid, or by evidence showing the deposit and prepaid postage; alternatively, the court of appeals may exercise its discretion to permit the later filing of a qualifying declaration or notarized statement. If the institution has a system designed for legal mail, the inmate must use that system to receive this benefit. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(c)(1)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-009 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-3-4-22 status:verified_current -->
If a notice is mistakenly filed in the court of appeals, the circuit clerk must note the receipt date, send it to the district clerk, and it is considered filed in the district court on the date the circuit clerk received it. [Fed. R. App. P. 4(d)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-010 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-MANRIQUE-581-US-116 status:verified_current -->
When restitution is deferred and a later amended judgment imposes it, a defendant who wishes to challenge that order must file a notice of appeal from it. The Court held this requirement is at least a mandatory claim-processing rule, without deciding whether it is jurisdictional; because the defendant filed no such notice and the government timely objected, the court of appeals properly declined to review the restitution amount. [*Manrique v. United States*, 581 U.S. 116, 120–22, 125 (2017)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-581/pdf/USREPORTS-581-116.pdf)

## Final judgment and statutory appellate authority

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-011 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-BERMAN-302-US-211 status:verified_current -->
In a federal criminal case, final judgment ordinarily means the sentence, and the judgment is appealable even when execution of sentence is suspended. [*Berman v. United States*, 302 U.S. 211, 212–13 (1937)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-302/pdf/USREPORTS-302-211.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-012 authorities:AUTH-USC-28-1291 status:verified_current -->
Section 1291 gives the courts of appeals (other than the Federal Circuit) jurisdiction of appeals from all final decisions of the district courts, except where a direct review may be had in the Supreme Court. By its terms, § 1291 does not grant jurisdiction of appeals from a later or interlocutory order unless that order is itself a final decision. [28 U.S.C. § 1291](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/1291)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-013 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-FLANAGAN-465-US-259 status:verified_current -->
The final-judgment rule is applied with particular stringency in criminal cases, and the collateral-order doctrine covers only a narrow class; *Flanagan* held that a pretrial order disqualifying defense counsel was not immediately appealable. [*Flanagan v. United States*, 465 U.S. 259, 263–70 (1984)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-465/pdf/USREPORTS-465-259.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-014 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-ABNEY-431-US-651 status:verified_current -->
A pretrial order denying a motion to dismiss on double-jeopardy grounds is a "final decision" that is immediately appealable to the extent it rejects the former-jeopardy claim, because the Double Jeopardy Clause is a guarantee against being twice put to trial for the same offense; other claims in the same motion are appealable only if they fall within *Cohen*'s collateral-order exception. [*Abney v. United States*, 431 U.S. 651, 660–63 (1977)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-431/pdf/USREPORTS-431-651.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-015 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3731,AUTH-USC-18-3742 status:verified_current -->
Section 3731 provides only for appeals by the United States: from orders dismissing an indictment or information or granting a new trial, except where the Double Jeopardy Clause prohibits further prosecution; from orders suppressing or excluding evidence or requiring the return of seized property, not made after jeopardy attaches and before the verdict, if the United States attorney certifies that the appeal is not taken for delay and the evidence is substantial proof of a material fact; and from orders granting release or denying revocation or modification of release. Each such appeal must be taken within thirty days and diligently prosecuted, and government appeals of sentences are separately governed by § 3742(b). [18 U.S.C. § 3731](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3731) [18 U.S.C. § 3742(b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3742)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-016 authorities:AUTH-USC-18-3742 status:verified_current -->
Section 3742 separately specifies the defendant's and government's grounds to appeal an otherwise final sentence and the appellate court's disposition authority; a disagreement with sentence alone is not a substitute for a statutory ground. The government may not further prosecute a sentence appeal without the personal approval of the Attorney General, the Solicitor General, or a designated deputy solicitor general. Under a plea agreement that includes a specific sentence, a defendant may not appeal under § 3742(a)(3) or (4) unless the sentence imposed is greater than the agreed sentence. The Code's constitutionality note refers readers to the table of laws held unconstitutional in whole or in part by the Supreme Court for certain provisions added or amended by Pub. L. 108-21 § 401(d)(2), (e). [18 U.S.C. § 3742(a)–(c), (f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/3742)

## Guilty pleas and express appeal waivers

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-017 authorities:AUTH-SCOTUS-GARZA-586-US-232,AUTH-SCOTUS-CLASS-583-US-174 status:verified_current -->
A guilty plea or appeal waiver does not mean that no notice may be filed or that every issue is necessarily barred: *Garza* applies presumed prejudice when counsel disregards an express instruction to appeal despite a waiver, while *Class* holds that an ordinary guilty plea alone did not waive the identified constitutional attack on the statute of conviction. The text, scope, validity, exceptions, government invocation, and controlling circuit law for an express waiver still must be analyzed. [*Garza v. Idaho*, 586 U.S. 232, 238–49 (2019)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/18pdf/17-1026_2c83.pdf); [*Class v. United States*, 583 U.S. 174, 181–85 (2018)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-583/pdf/USREPORTS-583-174.pdf)

## Record on appeal

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-018 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-10-11,AUTH-FRAP-28-31-32-34 status:verified_current -->
The Rule 10 record consists of the original district-court papers and exhibits, any transcript, and a certified copy of the docket entries, and the appellant's argument must cite the parts of the record on which the appellant relies. The parties may instead submit an agreed statement of the case, approved by the district court, in place of that record, and anything material omitted from or misstated in the record by error or accident may be corrected and a supplemental record certified and forwarded. [Fed. R. App. P. 10(a), (d), (e)(2), 28(a)(8)(A)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-019 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-10-11 status:verified_current -->
Within 14 days after filing the notice of appeal or entry of an order disposing of the last timely remaining motion of a type specified in Rule 4(a)(4)(A), whichever is later, an appellant must either order the needed transcript parts in writing (stating any Criminal Justice Act payment and filing a copy of the order with the district clerk within the same period) or file a certificate that no transcript will be ordered. Satisfactory payment arrangements must be made with the reporter at the time of ordering. Unless the entire transcript is ordered, the appellant must, within the same 14 days, file a statement of the issues the appellant intends to present and serve on the appellee a copy of the order or certificate and the statement; an appellant who will argue that a finding or conclusion is unsupported by or contrary to the evidence must include in the record a transcript of all evidence relevant to that finding or conclusion. [Fed. R. App. P. 10(b)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)

<!-- claim:CLM-DR-020 authorities:AUTH-FRAP-10-11 status:verified_current -->
When proceedings were not recorded or a transcript is unavailable, Rule 10(c) lets the appellant prepare a statement of the evidence or proceedings, which the appellee may answer with objections or proposed amendments within 14 days and which must then be submitted to the district court for settlement and approval; Rule 10(d) permits an agreed statement of the case, which must be approved by the district court. Rule 11 then governs assembly, transcript completion, retention, and forwarding of the record, with some matters left to local rules and court orders. [Fed. R. App. P. 10(c)–(d), 11](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-appellate-procedure.pdf)
